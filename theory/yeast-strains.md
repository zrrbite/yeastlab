# Yeast strains across crafts

A reference that spans **all the yeast (and LAB, and Brett) used in this repo's crafts** — sourdough wild cultures, baker's commercial yeast, ale yeast, lager yeast, kveik, Brett, mixed-ferm bacteria, and the wild microbes of coffee processing. Same chemistry; different selection pressures; different applications.

This file is **deliberately cross-craft**. Most yeast references organize by application (bread yeast vs beer yeast); this one organizes by **organism**, showing how the same microbe shows up in different crafts.

For application-specific deep dives, see the craft-specific yeast files:

- **[`../beer/yeast-strains.md`](../beer/yeast-strains.md)** — NEIPA-focused with brief notes on lagers and others
- **[`../sourdough/starter.md`](../sourdough/starter.md)** — wild sourdough cultures specifically
- **[`../coffee/processing/`](../coffee/processing/)** — wild fermentation in coffee cherries

## *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* — the workhorse

The most-used yeast on Earth. Same species across:

- **Baker's yeast** (active dry / instant) — selected for fast CO₂ production at warm temps
- **Most ale brewing strains** (US-05, WLP001, London III, Conan) — selected for flavor, attenuation, flocculation
- **Wine yeast** (EC-1118, K1V) — selected for alcohol tolerance, dryness
- **Many wild sourdough starters** carry it as one of several yeast species (alongside *Kazachstania humilis*, *Saccharomyces exiguus*)

The species is the same; the **strains** within it have been selected over centuries (or millennia, in the case of bread) for different traits.

### What the same species does in each craft

| Craft         | Selection priority                                  | Resulting character                       |
|---------------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Bread (commercial yeast) | Fast CO₂ production at 25–30 °C            | Quick rise, neutral flavor                 |
| Beer (US-05) | Clean fermentation, moderate flocculation, 18–22 °C | Neutral hop-forward beers                  |
| Beer (London III) | Esters, low flocculation, biotransformation     | Fruity, hazy beers                         |
| Wine (EC-1118) | High alcohol tolerance, fast, very clean           | Dry wines that ferment to completion       |
| Sourdough wild | Acid tolerance, slow growth, complex aromatics      | Tangy, slow-rising bread                   |

### Strain comparison highlights

For brewing across NEIPA / WCIPA / Cold IPA / Saison:

| Strain              | Attenuation | Flocculation | Best in                      |
|---------------------|-------------|--------------|------------------------------|
| Saflager W-34/70    | 80–85%      | High         | Pilsner, Cold IPA            |
| US-05 / WLP001      | 75–82%      | Medium-high  | WCIPA, Pale Ale              |
| Verdant IPA         | 75–80%      | Medium       | NEIPA (easy)                 |
| London Ale III      | 71–75%      | Medium-low   | NEIPA (default)              |
| Conan / Vermont     | 72–80%      | Medium-low   | NEIPA (Heady-style)          |
| Wyeast 3711 Saison  | 80–88%      | Variable     | Saison                       |
| Hornindal Kveik     | 75–82%      | Variable     | Hop-forward, fast            |
| WLP002 English      | 65–75%      | Very high    | Stout, English IPA           |

## *Saccharomyces pastorianus* — lager yeast

A natural hybrid of *S. cerevisiae* and *S. eubayanus*. **The yeast that built modern beer.**

- Bottom-fermenting (settles to the bottom rather than rising)
- Cold-tolerant (ferments at 8–14 °C)
- Cleaner fermentation than ale yeast
- Slower fermentation (weeks vs days)

Used in:

- **Pilsner / lager / Helles / Märzen / Doppelbock** — all the lager styles
- **Cold IPA** — the modern hop-forward use
- **American macro lagers** — most beer consumed globally

Major strains:

- **W-34/70 (dry)** — the workhorse; clean, broad temperature range
- **WLP800 / Wyeast 2001 Pilsner Urquell** — Czech lager character
- **WLP838 / Wyeast 2007 Southern German Lager** — German pilsner character
- **Mexican lager strains** — produce slight sulfur character desired in some traditional macros

## *Brettanomyces bruxellensis* and friends

A separate yeast genus. **Slow, oxygen-tolerant, produces distinctive funky character.**

- Used in **traditional Belgian sours** (Lambic, Flemish Red)
- Used in **modern Brett saisons** and **wild ales**
- Increasingly used in **modern New England-leaning IPAs** as a "Brett dry hop" addition for funk

Brett strains:

- **B. bruxellensis** — most common; aggressive funk
- **B. claussenii** — milder, more fruit-forward
- **B. lambicus** — tart, tropical

In coffee processing, **wild Brett-related yeasts** appear in **anaerobic and natural-process** fermentations, contributing some of the funky/winey character in those coffees.

## Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB)

Same family across:

- **Sourdough starters** (*Lactobacillus sanfranciscensis* especially)
- **Beer souring** (*Lactobacillus plantarum* in kettle sours)
- **Coffee processing** (LAB on coffee cherries during washed/honey/anaerobic processing)
- **Yogurt and kimchi and sauerkraut** (different species, same family)

What LAB does:

- **Produces lactic acid** → tart character (smooth in low concentrations, sharp at high)
- **Some species produce acetic acid** as well → vinegar character
- **Cleaves citrate to diacetyl** → buttery aroma
- **Produces hop-derived compound modifications** in beer — limited but present

LAB strains worth knowing:

- ***L. sanfranciscensis*** — primary sourdough LAB; defines the "sourdough" character
- ***L. plantarum*** — fast acidifier for kettle sours
- ***Pediococcus damnosus*** — slower, produces lactic + diacetyl in sour beers

## *Acetobacter* — vinegar bacteria

Acetic acid-producing bacteria. **Requires oxygen** to function.

- Produces **acetic acid (vinegar)** from ethanol
- Present in **Flemish Reds and other oxygen-exposed sour beers**
- Present in **some natural-process coffees** (especially in dry / oxidative environments)
- **Not wanted in clean beers** — acetic character is an off-flavor in IPAs and lagers

## Wild yeast in sourdough starters

Established sourdough starters typically host **several yeast species**:

- ***Kazachstania humilis*** — formerly *Candida humilis*; primary acid-tolerant yeast in many sourdough cultures
- ***Saccharomyces exiguus*** — another acid-tolerant yeast
- ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae*** — same as commercial; appears in some cultures
- ***Pichia*** species — present in some cultures, also in coffee fermentation

The exact composition varies wildly — your sourdough starter has its own unique microbial fingerprint.

## Wild yeast in coffee processing

Coffee cherries during fermentation host:

- ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae*** — produces alcohol from cherry sugars
- ***Pichia* species** — wild yeast, produces aromatic compounds
- ***Hanseniaspora* species** — early-fermentation yeast, contributes esters
- ***Candida* species** — variable, some produce off-flavors
- ***Brettanomyces*** — present in long-aged or anaerobic processing

The same wild yeasts appear in **wine fermentation** (especially natural / unfiltered wines), **beer mixed-fermentation**, and **traditional sourdough**. The chemistry rhymes across all these crafts.

## Kveik — Norwegian farmhouse yeast

A distinct category — **traditional Norwegian farmhouse yeast cultures** that:

- **Survive boiling temperatures** (some strains)
- **Ferment at 28–40 °C** (very warm)
- **Produce tropical / citrus / orange esters** depending on culture
- **Ferment fast** (3–5 days from pitch to FG)

Major kveik cultures:

- **Voss** — orange-citrus, very dry
- **Hornindal** — pineapple/mango/tropical, NEIPA-friendly
- **Stranda, Granvin, Espe** — various ester profiles

In bread baking, kveik is occasionally used as commercial-yeast replacement for fast, slightly fruitier breads. Underexplored.

## Pitch rates and starters — universal principles

Across all yeast applications:

- **More cells = faster, cleaner fermentation**
- **Underpitching = stress, off-flavors, slow start**
- **Liquid yeast vials** typically contain ~100 billion cells — enough for low-gravity ales but **not enough alone** for high-gravity beers or lagers
- **Yeast starters** (a small pre-fermentation in flavored wort) multiply cells before main pitch

Rough pitch rate guidelines:

- **Ale at 1.050 OG:** 1 vial = ok (or 1 packet US-05)
- **Ale at 1.070 OG (NEIPA):** 1.5–2× vial (or 1.5 packets US-05)
- **DIPA at 1.085 OG:** 2–3× vial (or 2 packets dry, ideally with starter)
- **Lager at 1.050 OG:** 2–3× vial (or 2 packets W-34/70)
- **Lager at 1.085 OG (Doppelbock):** 4–5× vial (serious starter required)
- **Sourdough:** "active and bubbly" — cells aren't counted, judged by visible activity

## Yeast banking for repeat use

Across crafts:

- **Sourdough:** maintained as a starter — feed weekly, indefinite
- **Beer (homebrew):** **top-cropping** (skim from active fermentation) or **bottom-cropping** (collect from cone) for repitching. Homebrewers usually fresh-pitch each batch; semi-pros repitch 2–4 generations.
- **Sourdough starter dried** is a **backup** — flake on parchment, store airtight, indefinite shelf life

## Cross-craft yeast experiments

Some interesting experiments worth knowing:

- **Wine yeast in beer:** EC-1118 produces a very dry, very clean fermentation. Used in Brut IPAs.
- **Beer yeast in bread:** US-05 in a sandwich loaf produces a slightly fruity, very reliable bread. Underexplored.
- **Brett in non-traditional beer:** Brett saison, modern wild IPAs — using Brett's distinctive character outside traditional sour styles.
- **Kveik in bread:** ferments warm and fast, producing slightly tropical-tinged bread. Niche but interesting.
