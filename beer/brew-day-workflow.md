# Brew day workflow

The practical playbook for an efficient, low-stress brew day on the **boiler + immersion chiller + FermZilla** setup. The goal isn't speed — it's **predictability** so you can brew when you want without it eating your whole day.

A solid brew day for a 23 L NEIPA on this kit takes **4.5–5.5 hours active time**, brew start to clean kitchen. With practice, this gets to a comfortable rhythm.

## The night before — pre-brew checklist

Spending **15 minutes the night before** saves you 30+ minutes on brew day. Do these:

### Recipe and yeast prep

- [ ] **Recipe printed or pulled up** — exact grains, hops, salts, schedule
- [ ] **Pull yeast from fridge** — let warm to room temp 4+ hours before pitch
- [ ] If using liquid yeast: **starter is fully fermented and refrigerated** for decant
- [ ] If using dry yeast: sachet at hand, no prep needed

### Water

- [ ] **Treat for chlorine** — drop a campden tablet in your brewing water (mash + sparge ~30 L). Stir, leave overnight.
- [ ] OR run a campden tablet treatment in the morning, 5 min before brewing

### Salts and acid pre-measured

- [ ] **Salts weighed in small cups** — labeled by addition (mash water vs kettle)
- [ ] **Phosphoric acid measured into a small dropper bottle**
- [ ] Saves "did I add it?" doubt during brewing

### Grain

- [ ] **Pull grain bag from storage** — pale malt, adjuncts
- [ ] **Crush** if you have a mill (and want fresh-crushed) — or order pre-crushed
- [ ] **Weigh out** the bill, keep in a labeled container

### Equipment

- [ ] **Sanitize spray bottle** filled with Star San
- [ ] **Fermenter clean and ready** — sanitize the FermZilla in the morning
- [ ] **Hop additions divided into labeled bags** by addition (60 min, whirlpool, dry hop, etc.)
- [ ] **Notebook open**, brew log started

## Brew day timeline (23 L NEIPA)

A representative brew day, with rough timing:

| Time     | Activity                                         |
|----------|--------------------------------------------------|
| 8:00 AM  | Heat strike water (mash water + treatment)       |
| 8:30 AM  | Mash in                                          |
| 8:45 AM  | Mash rest (60 min) — clean while you wait        |
| 9:45 AM  | Sparge / lauter                                  |
| 10:30 AM | Boil starts (60 min boil)                        |
| 10:30 AM | Bittering hops in                                |
| 11:15 AM | Whirlpool/late hops in                           |
| 11:30 AM | Boil ends, begin chilling                        |
| 11:45 AM | Whirlpool stand                                  |
| 12:15 PM | Chill to pitch temp                              |
| 12:30 PM | Transfer to fermenter                            |
| 12:45 PM | Pitch yeast                                      |
| 1:00 PM  | Cleanup begins                                   |
| 1:30 PM  | Cleanup done, brew day complete                  |

That's a 5.5-hour brew day. Faster is possible (BIAB shaves 30 min); slower is fine (no rush).

## Detailed step-by-step

### Stage 1: Strike water and mash (8:00–8:45 AM)

1. **Fill boiler** with mash water — typically 14–16 L for a 23 L batch (3 L/kg of grain ratio, roughly 2/3 of total water).
2. **Heat to ~74 °C / 165 °F** (strike temp; will drop to mash temp 67 °C when grain hits)
3. **Add salts** to mash water (per [water-tap-adjustments.md](water-tap-adjustments.md))
4. **Add phosphoric acid** to mash water if needed
5. **Mash in:** add grain to water (or water to grain in BIAB), stir well to eliminate dough balls
6. **Check mash temp** with thermometer — should be 65–67 °C; **adjust if off**
7. **Cover, set 60-min timer**

### Stage 2: Mash rest (8:45–9:45 AM)

This is your **first cleaning window**. While the mash rests:

- Wash the spoon, mixing tools, scale
- Sanitize the FermZilla (it'll need to be ready for transfer in 3 hours)
- Set up the immersion chiller — make sure water hookup is in place
- Pre-measure your hop additions if not done
- Drink coffee. Read. Don't stress.

**Check mash pH 15 min in** — should be 5.3–5.4. Adjust acid for next batch if off.

### Stage 3: Lauter / sparge (9:45–10:30 AM)

For a BIAB or BrewZilla:

- **Pull grain bag**, drain (squeeze gently — don't go aggressive on tannin extraction)
- **Add sparge water** (heated to 76 °C separately) directly to the kettle if needed for volume
- **Pre-boil volume target:** ~28 L for a 23 L into-fermenter batch (5 L boil-off)

For 3-vessel brewing:

- **Recirculate / vorlauf** to clear wort
- **Drain to kettle**, then sparge through grain bed to collect remaining sugars
- Same volume target

### Stage 4: Boil (10:30–11:30 AM)

- **Heat to boil** — takes 15–20 min from sparge end
- **At boil:** add **bittering hops** (Magnum or similar)
- **Set 60-min timer**
- **At 30 min remaining:** add any sugar additions
- **At 10 min remaining:** add **acid to hit 4.8–4.9 post-boil pH** (see [`water.md`](water.md))
- **At 5 min remaining:** any flavor additions (Irish moss, yeast nutrient, late hops)
- **Boil ends 1 hour later**

### Stage 5: Whirlpool (11:30–11:45 AM)

- **Stop heating, immersion chiller in**
- **Cool to 82 °C / 180 °F** with chiller (5–8 minutes)
- **Stop chilling.** Stir wort to create vortex.
- **Add whirlpool hops** in your aroma load
- **Cover, stand 25 minutes**

### Stage 6: Chill (12:00–12:30 PM)

- **Resume chilling** to pitch temp (18–20 °C for ales)
- **Time:** depends on ground water temp. Cold water (winter) chills in 10–15 min; warm water (summer) takes 25–35 min.
- **Tip:** if chilling is slow, **swap to colder water source** halfway (cold tap → fridge water → ice bath)

### Stage 7: Transfer + pitch (12:30–12:45 PM)

- **Sanitize transfer hose, FermZilla connections**
- **Transfer chilled wort** to FermZilla — try to leave hop debris in the kettle
- **Take OG reading** (refractometer or hydrometer)
- **Aerate** wort vigorously (pour, splash, shake) — OR use pure O₂ stone for 30 sec
- **Pitch yeast** — sachet sprinkled on (if dry), or starter slurry decanted (if liquid)
- **Seal FermZilla**, attach airlock or spunding setup

### Stage 8: Cleanup (12:45–1:30 PM)

- **Drain spent grain** from kettle / mash tun (compost, give to local farmer, etc.)
- **Rinse equipment** while it's still warm (much easier than dried-on residue)
- **Wash with PBW or unscented dish soap**
- **Final rinse**
- **Air dry on a rack**
- **Wipe down kitchen**

## Equipment-specific tips

### FermZilla All Rounder

- **Pre-flush with CO₂** before transfer if you have access to gas — reduces O₂ exposure on day 1
- **Use the bottom collection container** to drop trub after a few days of fermentation
- **Spunding valve setup:** install at ~70% attenuation, target serving pressure
- **Glycol cooling:** if you have it, use the FermZilla's bottom port for coolant in/out
- **Cleaning:** the All Rounder unscrews into pieces; clean the threads carefully

### Immersion chiller

- **Pre-clean before each use** — wipe the coils with a sanitizer-soaked cloth
- **Stir wort while chilling** — increases contact, faster chill
- **Plumbing tip:** a Y-valve on your tap lets you switch between cold tap and recirculated ice water without disconnecting

### Boiler

- **PID accuracy matters more than peak power** for hitting consistent mash and boil temps
- **For long boils** (90 min for pilsner), make sure your boiler keeps a vigorous boil — weak boil = DMS issues

## Common time-savers

### Brew on weekends, not after work

A 5.5-hour brew day after a 9-hour workday is brutal. **Weekend brewing** is the realistic pattern.

### Brew with a friend

Two people can brew in 4 hours instead of 5.5. The cleaning time alone is cut in half. Plus brewing is a social activity.

### Pre-mix salts and acid

Make a batch of "NEIPA salt mix" you can scoop by gram for each brew, instead of weighing 4 ingredients separately.

### Sanitize once, brew often

Brewing back-to-back batches (week 1, week 2) lets you reuse the same sanitized fermenter cleaning approach. Reduces "is this clean?" anxiety.

### Use hop bags

Adding hops in mesh bags makes whirlpool cleanup dramatically easier. Some brewers find hops stuck in bags don't extract as well; consider this trade-off.

### Plan brewing around the chamber

If you have a [fermentation chamber](process/temperature-control.md), brew when the chamber is free. **Don't transfer a NEIPA into a chamber that's holding another beer at the wrong temp.**

## A "first brew on this kit" approach

For a brewer setting up the FermZilla + boiler + immersion chiller for the first time:

### Brew #1

- Pick a **simple, forgiving recipe** — the [NEIPA template](recipes/neipa-template.md) or [Cold pizza dough... wait no — the WCIPA template](recipes/west-coast-ipa.md)
- **Don't try a DIPA first**. High-gravity needs more skill.
- Use **dry yeast** (Verdant IPA) — no starter complexity
- **Buy fresh** (every ingredient — hops, malt, yeast)

### What you'll learn

- Your boiler's actual heat-up time
- Your specific water's mash pH behavior
- Your chiller's chill time (depends on ground water temp)
- How long full cleanup takes
- What surprises you (it'll be something)

### Don't worry about

- **Perfect first batch.** First batches are practice.
- **Style points.** Make beer that's drinkable. Refine later.
- **Speed.** Take 6–7 hours if needed; you'll be faster next time.

### Document everything

- OG, FG, ABV
- Mash pH, post-boil pH, final beer pH
- Fermentation temperature curve (note any swings)
- Hop additions timing
- What worked, what surprised you
- Tasting notes at week 1, week 2, week 3

By brew #5, you'll have a personal playbook that beats anything published.

## When things go wrong

A few common brew-day disasters and recoveries:

### Stuck mash (no flow during sparge)

- Loosen the grain bed (if BrewZilla, recirculate; if BIAB, lift bag and let it drain)
- Add sparge water more slowly
- Next time: include rice hulls (5% of grain bill) for high-adjunct (oat / wheat) recipes

### Boil over

- **Spray with water bottle** to break the foam
- **Drop temperature** briefly
- Next time: bigger headspace in kettle, or add Fermcap-S

### Chiller too slow

- **Add ice** to a bath the kettle sits in
- **Swap to colder source** mid-chill
- **Or just wait** — slow chill isn't catastrophic (some DMS risk for pilsner; less for ale)

### Forgot a hop addition

- **Add it next time you can** — better late than never
- **Note in your log** for next batch

### Spilled wort

- Sanitize the area
- Note actual final volume (you've changed your gravity)
- **Continue** — don't waste a brew over a small spill

### Realized you forgot salts

- Add now if mash hasn't drained yet
- Or accept the variance and note it in your log

## Cleanup philosophy

Clean as you go = less misery. A fully-finished brew day with a still-dirty kitchen is bad. A brew day where you're rinsing equipment **as it cools** is much better.

Specific cleanup wins:

- **Scrape spent grain into compost immediately** — easier wet than dried
- **Run hot water through the chiller while it's still hot** — disinfects + cleans
- **Pour residual hot wort into the dirty boiler** to soak — easier later
- **Wash hop bags / mesh strainers immediately** — hop residue dries to glue
- **Wipe up spills as they happen**

A clean brew day takes 30–45 min of cleanup; a "clean later" brew day takes 90+ minutes the next morning.

## Building a brewing schedule

A sustainable rhythm:

- **One batch every 2–3 weeks** for active drinking
- **Brew Saturday morning**, drink starting in 2–3 weeks
- **Use the time between batches** for planning, recipe iteration, sourcing fresh ingredients
- **Brewing every weekend** = burnout (unless you're commercial)

For most homebrewers: **2 batches/month** is the sustainable sweet spot.
