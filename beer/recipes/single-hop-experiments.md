# Single-hop experiments

The fastest way to learn what individual hop varieties actually contribute is to **brew the same base beer with one hop at a time**. Side-by-side single-hop NEIPAs teach you more about variety character than reading 50 tasting notes.

This file is a framework for running single-hop experiments — not a recipe per se.

## The base recipe

A "single-hop NEIPA" should be a stripped-down version of the [`neipa-template.md`](neipa-template.md) — same grain bill, water, yeast, process, but **one variety throughout** the whole hop schedule. Every batch is identical except for the hop.

### Modifications from the template

- **Slightly lower hop rate** to make varietal character clearer: total ~10–12 g/L instead of 14 g/L.
- **All the same hop:** bittering, whirlpool, active-ferm DH, cold-side DH all use the same variety.
- **Lower OG** (1.054–1.058) so the beer is sessionable enough to drink several side-by-side without palate fatigue.

### Suggested base

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| OG | 1.056 |
| FG | 1.012 |
| ABV | 5.5–6.0% |
| Total hopping | ~11 g/L (~250 g for 23 L) |
| Yeast | Verdant IPA (or whatever you'll use across all batches) |

### Hop schedule

| Stage              | Grams (of the single hop) |
|--------------------|---------------------------|
| Bittering (60 min) | 10 g                      |
| Whirlpool (180 °F, 25 min) | 100 g             |
| Active-ferm DH     | 70 g                      |
| Cold-side DH (13 °C, 3 days) | 70 g            |
| **Total**          | **250 g (~11 g/L)**       |

## Choosing varieties to study

The point is to learn what **changes between hops** when everything else is held constant. Pick varieties you're curious about, but try to vary on at least one dimension:

### Suggested first 6 batches

If you're new to NEIPA hops, this sequence builds intuition:

1. **Citra** — the modern reference point. Big thiol/geraniol contribution, broad survivable profile.
2. **Mosaic** — terpene-driven, more myrcene character. The main "complement" to Citra.
3. **Galaxy** — the thiol monster. See what 4MMP/3MH at high concentration tastes like alone.
4. **Mosaic** Cryo — same hop as #2, different format. Compare to #2 to feel the format effect.
5. **Strata** — dank/cannabis, polyphenol-heavy, polarizing. See what's behind the controversy.
6. **Nelson Sauvin** — the wine/grape thiol. Distinct from #1–#5.

After these, expand to anything you're curious about.

### Variations to try across a hop you know

Once you have a baseline batch with (say) Citra, you can isolate single variables:

- **Same hop, different yeast.** Brew Citra single-hop with London Ale III, then Hornindal kveik. Compare biotransformation differences.
- **Same hop, different temperature dry hop.** One batch dry-hopped warm (20 °C), one cool (13 °C). Compare polyphenol/myrcene differences.
- **Same hop, different format.** T-90 batch and Cryo batch.
- **Same hop, different harvest year.** Fresh Citra and 2-year-old Citra. (Don't waste your good hops on this — small batches with old hops you have lying around.)

Off-flavors detected: 
  Onion / garlic / cardboard / cheese / grass / other:
  ________________________________________

  ________________________________________

A page per hop in a brewing notebook. After 10 single-hop batches you'll have a working personal reference that beats anything published.

## Side-by-side tasting tips

Single-hop batches are most useful when you **drink them in flights**, not days/weeks apart. After bottling/kegging each:

- **Pour 100 mL each into identical glasses.** Tulip glasses are best for hop aroma.
- **Same temperature** — chilled to 4–8 °C.
- **Smell first**, write notes before tasting.
- **Cleanse palate between hops** — water and unsalted crackers. Don't use sparkling water (CO₂ resets bitterness perception).
- **Compare in pairs first**, then triples. A 6-way blind comparison is hard to do justice to.
- **Re-taste in 30 min** — you'll perceive different things as your palate adapts.

If you have brewing friends, **blind tastings** are gold. Number cups, have someone else pour, tally guesses. You'll discover that your hop "intuitions" are sometimes very wrong.

## What single-hop reveals (and what it doesn't)

### Reveals

- **Varietal character** — what does Citra alone actually taste like, separated from Mosaic and Galaxy contributions in a normal blend?
- **Biotransformation effects** — geraniol-rich hops (Sabro, Centennial) shift dramatically in active-ferm dry hop; you can taste β-citronellol forming.
- **Format effects** — T-90 vs Cryo of the same variety differs in polyphenol load and pH.
- **Harvest year effects** — old hops vs fresh hops, same variety, side by side.
- **What "good" smells like** — calibrates your nose to identify off-flavors faster.

### Doesn't reveal

- **Combo synergies** — single-hop tells you about each hop alone. Most of the *interesting* NEIPA character comes from multi-hop combinations where hops enhance each other (e.g., 4MMP from Galaxy enhancing perception of Citra's geraniol).
- **Real-recipe context** — single-hop NEIPAs at 11 g/L are intentionally lower than your "real" recipes at 14 g/L. Variety character at the higher loading may differ.
- **Consumer reception** — single-hop NEIPAs are interesting to brew but rarely become favorite drinkers. They're a learning tool.

## Maintaining a single-hop "library"

A nice approach if you have keg space:

- Keep a **single-hop NEIPA in rotation** — brew one every 4–6 weeks, drink it through, brew another with a different hop.
- Cumulatively over a year you'll have brewed 10+ single-hop batches and built deep variety knowledge.
- **Keep the base recipe identical** so the comparison stays valid across time.

A few breweries do this commercially — Other Half's Single-Hop series, Maine Beer's single-hop releases, Yakima Chief's annual single-hop pilot batches. Worth seeking out for calibration.
