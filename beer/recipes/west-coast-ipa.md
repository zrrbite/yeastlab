# West Coast IPA template

Standard WCIPA at ~6.8% ABV. Built for **23 L into the fermenter** on a simple boiler + immersion chiller. Designed to be the cleanest, driest, most piney-citrusy version of the style — the kind of beer that makes drinkers remember why this style was the foundation of American craft.

This template is **deliberately simple** — pale malt, gypsum-forward water, Chico, and a focused hop bill. Modify after you've brewed it once and have a baseline.

## Vital stats

| Parameter        | Target              |
|------------------|---------------------|
| OG               | 1.064–1.068        |
| FG               | 1.010–1.012 (very dry) |
| ABV              | 6.8–7.2%           |
| IBU (calculated) | 65–75              |
| Color (SRM)      | 6–8 (pale-medium gold) |
| Mash pH          | 5.3                |
| Final beer pH    | 4.2–4.4            |
| Carbonation      | 2.6 vol CO₂        |

## Grain bill

For **23 L into fermenter** at OG 1.066 (~75% mash efficiency):

| Grain                         | Bakers % | Grams |
|-------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| 2-row pale malt               | 95%      | 5400  |
| Crystal 20L (light caramel)   | 4%       | 230   |
| Carapils (dextrin)            | 1%       | 60    |

**Total grain:** ~5.69 kg / 12.5 lb

Notes:

- **2-row pale** is the default. Use Maris Otter or English pale if you want a slightly nuttier base; both work but lean to "English IPA" character.
- **Crystal 20L** adds a touch of color and a hint of light caramel — supports the bitterness without sweetening. Skip if you want a paler, more modern look.
- **Carapils** adds 1% body without color or sweetness. Optional.
- **No wheat. No oats. No high-crystal.** This is intentional.

## Water

Target: **sulfate-forward, hard, low-chloride.** Inverted from NEIPA.

For 30 L total water from RO:

| Salt                          | Add to mash | Add to kettle | Total grams |
|-------------------------------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Calcium sulfate (gypsum)      | 8 g         | 6 g           | 14 g        |
| Calcium chloride (CaCl₂·2H₂O) | 1 g         | 0.5 g         | 1.5 g       |
| Magnesium sulfate (Epsom)     | 1 g         | —             | 1 g         |
| Sodium chloride               | 0.5 g       | —             | 0.5 g       |

This produces approximately:

- Ca: ~140 ppm
- Mg: ~10 ppm
- Na: ~7 ppm
- Cl: ~30 ppm
- **SO₄: ~270 ppm** (the headline)
- **Cl : SO₄ ≈ 1 : 9** (very SO₄-forward)

For brewers used to NEIPA water: this is the **inverted profile**. Where NEIPA leans heavily on calcium chloride, WCIPA leans on calcium sulfate.

**Acid:** phosphoric acid (10% food-grade), enough to hit:

- Mash pH **5.3** — typically 1–3 mL in mash water
- Don't worry about post-boil acidification for WCIPA — the bittering hops + sulfate naturally drop the pH

## Mash

- **Mash temp: 64 °C / 147 °F** (lower than NEIPA — favors fermentability for a drier finish)
- **Mash time:** 60 min
- **Mash thickness:** ~3 L/kg
- **Sparge:** batch or fly, collect ~30 L pre-boil. Sparge at 76 °C / 168 °F.

The lower mash temp is critical — it's what gives WCIPA its dry finish.

## Boil

- **75 min boil** — the extra 15 min over NEIPA's 60 helps drive off DMS and concentrate the wort slightly.
- **75 min:** Bittering hops added at start of boil.
- Through-boil and whirlpool additions below.

### Hop schedule

For a **6.8% WCIPA** with classic hop combo:

| Time / Stage              | Hops                                     | Grams | IBUs (approx) |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------|-------|---------------|
| **75 min (boil start)**   | Magnum (or Warrior, or Columbus) 14% AA | 18 g  | ~30 IBU       |
| **20 min**                | Centennial 9% AA                         | 25 g  | ~12 IBU       |
| **5 min**                 | Cascade 5.5% AA + Centennial 9% AA       | 25 g + 15 g | ~8 IBU |
| **0 min (flameout)**      | Stop boil, begin cooling to 85 °C       | —     | —             |
| **Whirlpool (85 °C, 20 min)** | Cascade 50 g + Centennial 30 g + Simcoe 30 g | 110 g | ~15 IBU |
| **Cold-side dry hop (5 days)** | Cascade 40 g + Centennial 30 g + Simcoe 30 g + Citra 20 g | 120 g | (no IBU) |

**Total hops:** ~313 g
**Total IBU (calculated):** ~65–75

### Variations

**Modern WCIPA (more citrusy):**

- Whirlpool: replace Simcoe with **Mosaic 30 g**, add **Citra 30 g**, drop Cascade
- Dry hop: lean heavier on Citra, add **Mosaic 30 g**

**Pliny-the-Elder-inspired:**

- Bittering: **Columbus 14%** at 75 min
- Whirlpool: **Centennial + Simcoe** in equal parts
- Dry hop: **Centennial + Simcoe + Cascade** in equal parts (split into 2 charges if you have time)

**Pine-bomb:**

- Whirlpool: **Chinook + Simcoe + Centennial** — equal parts
- Dry hop: **Chinook + Simcoe** — equal parts

## Fermentation

- **Pitch yeast at 18 °C / 64 °F** into oxygenated wort (8–12 ppm DO).
- **Yeast pitch:**
  - Liquid: 1.5 L starter from 1 vial/pack of WLP001 California Ale (or Wyeast 1056)
  - Dry: **11 g of Safale US-05** for 23 L (one and a half packs)
- **Days 1–2:** 18–19 °C, active fermentation begins.
- **Days 3–6:** Free-rise to 20–22 °C. Fermentation completes.
- **Day 6–7:** FG approached and stable for 2 days.
- **Day 7–8:** Optional diacetyl rest at 22 °C for 1–2 days. US-05 usually doesn't need it but doesn't hurt.

**No active-fermentation dry hop.** WCIPA is about clean hop expression, not biotransformation.

## Cold-side dry hop

- **At 18 °C** (cellar / fermentation temp), drop the cold-side dry hop under CO₂ purge.
- **~120 g total** (from the schedule above).
- **Hold 5 days** at 18 °C — longer than NEIPA's 3 days because you want **more polyphenol extraction** for the dry, hop-forward character that defines WCIPA.

After 5 days:

- Drop spent dry hops through bottom valve.

## Cold conditioning (the clarity step)

This is where WCIPA earns its clarity:

- **Crash to 0–2 °C / 32–35 °F.**
- **Hold 7–10 days** at this temperature.
- Yeast and hop debris drop out. Beer becomes brilliant.

**Don't skip this step.** A WCIPA that goes from dry hop to keg without proper cold conditioning will be hazy — and a hazy WCIPA is a defective NEIPA, not a style choice.

Optional: **gelatin fining** for extra clarity:

- Bloom 1 g of gelatin in 50 mL cold water, then heat gently to 60 °C
- Add to the cold-conditioning beer 24–48 hr before kegging
- Pulls out remaining yeast and hop polyphenols → optimal clarity

Some purists object; many great WCIPAs use it. Your call.

## Packaging

- **Spunding** (if using FermZilla): set valve at ~70% attenuation to **15 PSI** for ~2.6 vol CO₂ at 4 °C.
- **Closed transfer to a sanitizer-purged keg** (see [`../process/packaging.md`](../process/packaging.md)).
- **Cold storage at 2–4 °C.**

## Total timeline

| Day  | Activity                                          |
|------|---------------------------------------------------|
| 0    | Brew day                                          |
| 7    | FG reached, optional diacetyl rest                |
| 9    | Cold-side dry hop drops at 18 °C                  |
| 14   | Spent hops dropped, crash to 0–2 °C               |
| 21–24 | Cold-condition complete                          |
| 22–25 | Closed transfer to keg                           |
| 26+  | Drink — and it'll keep getting better for ~6 weeks |

About **3.5 weeks from brew to first pour.** WCIPA is more patient than NEIPA at every stage.

## Drinking it

Best enjoyed at **7–9 °C / 45–48 °F** — slightly warmer than NEIPA.

In a tulip glass — the inward curve concentrates the hop aromatics. A pint glass works fine too.

**Goes well with:** spicy food (the IBU cuts heat), grilled meats, sharp cheeses, fish & chips. Less ideal with delicate / sweet foods (the bitterness will steamroll them).

## Aging notes

WCIPA ages **better than NEIPA**:

- **Week 1:** very fresh, hop-forward, possibly slightly harsh
- **Weeks 2–4:** peak — bitterness softens, hops integrate, dry-finish settles
- **Weeks 4–8:** still excellent, hop character starts to fade slowly
- **Weeks 8–12:** drinkable, more malt-forward as hops fade
- **Beyond 3 months:** starts to taste old; some classic WCIPAs were designed to be cellared longer (Pliny the Elder famously says "drink fresh" but in practice many lots improve over 6–8 weeks)

Compare to NEIPA which peaks at 2–4 weeks and is rarely good past 8.

## Common issues

- **Beer not clear.** Insufficient cold conditioning, or yeast didn't drop. Crash longer / add gelatin.
- **Too sweet / not dry enough.** Mash temp was probably too high (>66 °C) — try lower (64 °C) next time. Or yeast didn't fully attenuate; check FG.
- **Bitterness is harsh / vegetal.** Old hops, high mash pH, or polyphenol overload. Check water (mash pH), check hop freshness.
- **Bitterness is one-note / boring.** Add hops at more time points (FWH + 60 + 20 + 5 + WP + DH). Layered bitterness is more interesting than a single big charge.
- **No piney / dank character.** You used the wrong hops. Make sure your aroma load is Cascade/Centennial/Chinook/Simcoe/Columbus territory, not Mosaic/Galaxy.
- **Cardboard / sherry within 2 weeks.** Oxidation. Closed transfer discipline.

## Why DEAM doesn't apply (much)

The Double Enzymatic Activation Method is a **sourdough framework**. WCIPA process is conventional brewing — single-fermentation, no biotransformation, no enzyme priming. Nothing in the DEAM playbook moves the needle for WCIPA.

What does transfer between bread and WCIPA: **water chemistry awareness, fermentation-temperature thinking, and the general "understand the chemistry, then design recipes" mindset.** See [`../../theory/`](../../theory/).
