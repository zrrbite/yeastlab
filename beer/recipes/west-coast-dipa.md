# West Coast DIPA template

The bigger sibling — **8.5–9.5% ABV, more hop, more body, more dangerous**. The classic American Double IPA, in the lineage of Pliny the Elder and Russian River.

WCIPA's intensity gets dialed up across the board: more grain, more hop, more attenuation. The trick is keeping it **drinkable** despite the alcohol — not a syrupy hop bomb, but a beer you'd actually want to finish.

## Vital stats

| Parameter        | Target              |
|------------------|---------------------|
| OG               | 1.080–1.088        |
| FG               | 1.012–1.016 (still dry) |
| ABV              | 8.5–9.5%           |
| IBU (calculated) | 80–100+            |
| Color (SRM)      | 6–9 (gold to deep gold) |
| Mash pH          | 5.3                |
| Final beer pH    | 4.2–4.4            |
| Carbonation      | 2.5 vol CO₂ (slightly less than WCIPA — alcohol carries the "kick") |

## Grain bill

For **23 L into fermenter** at OG 1.084 (~75% efficiency):

| Grain                          | Bakers % | Grams |
|--------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| 2-row pale malt                | 88%      | 6420  |
| Crystal 20–40L                 | 5%       | 365   |
| Carapils                       | 2%       | 145   |
| **Sugar (table sugar / dextrose)** | 5%   | 365   |

**Total grist:** ~7.3 kg of malt + 365 g sugar

The **sugar addition** is the secret of the great WCIPAs. It:

- **Boosts ABV** without adding body or malt sweetness
- **Drys out the finish** — sugar ferments fully, leaves nothing
- **Makes the beer more drinkable** despite the high ABV
- Pliny the Elder's recipe famously uses **6–10% sugar**

Add the sugar at the **end of the boil** (10–15 min before flameout), or in the fermenter on day 2 of fermentation (less common but works).

## Water

Same WCIPA profile, slightly more aggressive:

For 30 L total water from RO:

| Salt                          | Add to mash | Add to kettle | Total grams |
|-------------------------------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Calcium sulfate (gypsum)      | 9 g         | 7 g           | 16 g        |
| Calcium chloride              | 1 g         | 0.5 g         | 1.5 g       |
| Magnesium sulfate             | 1 g         | —             | 1 g         |
| Sodium chloride               | 0.5 g       | —             | 0.5 g       |

Approximate result:

- Ca: ~155 ppm
- **SO₄: ~310 ppm**
- **Cl : SO₄ ≈ 1 : 10**

Even more sulfate-forward than the standard WCIPA. Helps cut through the bigger hop load.

## Mash

- **Mash temp: 64 °C / 147 °F** — same as standard WCIPA. Maximum fermentability.
- **Mash time:** 60 min
- **Sparge:** to collect ~30 L pre-boil; with the bigger grain bill, mash thicker (~2.5 L/kg) to fit volume.

## Boil

- **90 min boil** — extra 15 min for a higher-gravity wort to reduce volume and concentrate sugars.
- Substantial bittering and aroma loads throughout.

### Hop schedule

For an aggressive ~9% DIPA. **Total hopping ~24 g/L** — higher than NEIPA but still sane.

| Time / Stage              | Hops                                     | Grams | IBUs (approx) |
|---------------------------|------------------------------------------|-------|---------------|
| **90 min**                | Magnum 14% AA (or Columbus)             | 25 g  | ~40 IBU       |
| **30 min**                | Centennial 9% AA                         | 30 g  | ~18 IBU       |
| **10 min**                | Add **365 g sugar** here as it dissolves | —     | —             |
| **5 min**                 | Centennial + Cascade                     | 30 g + 30 g | ~12 IBU |
| **0 min (flameout)**      | Stop boil, cool to 85 °C                | —     | —             |
| **Whirlpool (85 °C, 25 min)** | Centennial + Simcoe + Cascade        | 60 g + 50 g + 50 g | ~20 IBU |
| **Cold-side dry hop #1 (3 days at 18 °C)** | Centennial + Simcoe + Cascade | 50 g + 50 g + 40 g | (no IBU) |
| **Cold-side dry hop #2 (3 days at 18 °C)** | Centennial + Cascade + Citra (modern variant) | 40 g + 40 g + 30 g | (no IBU) |

**Total hops:** ~525 g = ~23 g/L
**Total IBU:** ~90–100

The **two-charge dry hop** (3 days each, with the first charge dropped before the second is added) extracts more total aroma than a single 6-day charge — and avoids the polyphenol overload that comes from extended dry-hop contact.

### Modern WCIPA DIPA variants

- **Bring in Citra/Mosaic** for a more contemporary fruit-meets-pine character
- Drop Cascade in favor of **Galaxy** for tropical edge
- Use **Cryo Hops®** for some dry hop charges to manage polyphenol load

## Fermentation

- **Pitch yeast at 18 °C / 64 °F** into oxygenated wort.
- **Yeast pitch:** for a 9% DIPA, **double the pitch rate** of standard WCIPA:
  - Liquid: 2.5–3 L starter from 1 vial/pack of WLP001 (a "starter on a stir plate" is ideal here)
  - Dry: **22 g of US-05 (3 packs)** rehydrated. Underpitching a high-gravity wort = stuck fermentation.
- **Days 1–3:** 18–19 °C
- **Days 4–7:** Free-rise to 22 °C — important for full attenuation in a high-gravity beer
- **Days 7–10:** Hold at 22 °C until FG reached and stable
- **Day 10–11:** Diacetyl rest at 22–23 °C for 2 days (more important here than in standard WCIPA — bigger beer, more diacetyl risk)

## Cold-side dry hop (two stages)

**Stage 1:** at FG, drop dry hop #1 at 18 °C, hold 3 days, then drop spent hops.
**Stage 2:** drop dry hop #2 at 18 °C, hold 3 days, then drop spent hops.

Total dry hop contact: 6 days but with hop solids refreshed midway.

## Cold conditioning

- **Crash to 0–2 °C / 32–35 °F.**
- **Hold 10–14 days** — longer than standard WCIPA. Bigger beer, more polyphenols, more time needed for clarity.
- **Optional gelatin fining** — recommended for DIPA. The polyphenol load is high; fining helps clarity dramatically.

## Packaging

- Spunding to **2.5 vol CO₂** (slightly lower than standard WCIPA — alcohol carries enough kick)
- Closed transfer to purged keg (see [`../process/packaging.md`](../process/packaging.md))
- **Cold storage at 2–4 °C**

## Total timeline

| Day  | Activity                                          |
|------|---------------------------------------------------|
| 0    | Brew day                                          |
| 7    | FG approached                                     |
| 10–11 | Diacetyl rest                                    |
| 11   | Dry hop #1 at 18 °C                              |
| 14   | Drop spent hops, dry hop #2                       |
| 17   | Drop spent hops, crash to 0–2 °C                  |
| 24–28 | Cold-condition complete                          |
| 28+  | Closed transfer to keg                            |
| 30+  | Drink                                             |

About **4 weeks brew-to-pour.** DIPA needs more time than standard WCIPA; respect the timeline.

## Drinking it

Best at **8–10 °C / 46–50 °F** — slightly warmer than standard WCIPA to let the alcohol and hops express.

In a tulip glass, ideally. Pour with some head — the carbonation lifts the aromatics.

**A note on ABV:** at 9% you'll feel it. Don't drink three of these and try to make decisions. The cardinal sin of DIPAs is brewing them so dangerously drinkable that you forget what you're doing — which is exactly what good ones do.

## Aging notes

DIPAs age **better than standard IPAs**:

- Higher alcohol = better preservation
- Bigger malt body = more substrate for slow Maillard development
- Bigger hop load = takes longer to fade

Peak: **3–6 weeks**. Still very drinkable to **3 months**. Some classic DIPAs (Pliny the Elder, etc.) are designed to be drunk fresh; others (Stone Enjoy By series specifically) demand fresh consumption. Your DIPA will fall somewhere in this range.

## Common issues specific to DIPA

- **Stuck fermentation (FG too high).** Underpitched, fermentation too cold, or wort too oxygenated initially. Pitch big, pitch healthy.
- **Hot alcohol / fusel character.** Fermentation got too warm too fast (>23 °C in early ferm). Pitch cool, free-rise later.
- **Cloying / sweet despite high attenuation.** Mash temp was too high. The sugar addition helps but only goes so far if your mash is already too sweet.
- **Polyphenol astringency.** Too much dry hop, too long. Switch to Cryo for some additions, or shorten contact.
- **One-note bitterness.** All bitterness from boil; not enough whirlpool/dry hop layering.

## Why this works

A great WCIPA DIPA is built on three principles:

1. **Drinkability through dryness** — sugar additions, low mash temp, high attenuation. The beer should not feel sweet.
2. **Bitterness through layering** — multiple hop additions across boil, whirlpool, and dry hop. Not a single bitter wallop.
3. **Clarity through cold conditioning** — same as standard WCIPA but more important here. A hazy DIPA looks (and tastes) wrong.
