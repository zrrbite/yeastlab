# Cold IPA template

A classic Cold IPA recipe — pilsner malt with rice adjunct, lager yeast at 12 °C, modern hop bill (Citra + Mosaic + Strata), brilliantly clear, dry, hop-forward. ~7% ABV.

Built for **23 L into the fermenter** on a simple boiler + immersion chiller in a FermZilla under spunding. Cold IPA needs **longer total time** than ale-yeast IPAs (5–6 weeks brew-to-keg), but the result is uniquely clean.

## Vital stats

| Parameter        | Target              |
|------------------|---------------------|
| OG               | 1.064–1.068        |
| FG               | 1.010–1.012        |
| ABV              | 7.0%               |
| IBU (calculated) | 55–65              |
| Color (SRM)      | 3–4 (very pale)    |
| Mash pH          | 5.3                |
| Final beer pH    | 4.2–4.4            |
| Carbonation      | 2.8 vol CO₂        |

## Grain bill

For **23 L into fermenter** at OG 1.066 (~75% efficiency):

| Grain                          | Bakers % | Grams |
|--------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Pilsner malt (German or Belgian) | 80%   | 5400  |
| Flaked rice                    | 20%      | 1350  |

**Total grain:** ~6.75 kg

Notes:

- **German Weyermann or Best Pilsner** is excellent. Belgian pilsner works too. Avoid 2-row pale; you want the lighter character of pilsner.
- **Flaked rice** is the classic Cold IPA adjunct. **Flaked corn** also works (slightly different character — corn = soft sweetness even after attenuation; rice = neutral). Both are widely available.
- **No crystal, no carapils, no specialty malt.** The grain is intentionally background.

## Water

For 30 L total water from RO:

| Salt                          | Add to mash | Add to kettle | Total grams |
|-------------------------------|-------------|---------------|-------------|
| Calcium sulfate (gypsum)      | 4 g         | 3 g           | 7 g         |
| Calcium chloride              | 2 g         | 1 g           | 3 g         |
| Magnesium sulfate             | 1 g         | —             | 1 g         |
| Sodium chloride               | 0.5 g       | —             | 0.5 g       |

Approximate result:

- Ca: ~95 ppm
- SO₄: ~140 ppm
- Cl: ~70 ppm
- **Cl : SO₄ ≈ 1 : 2**

**Acid:** phosphoric to hit mash pH **5.3** — typically 1–2 mL.

## Mash

- **Mash temp: 65 °C / 149 °F** — slightly higher than WCIPA (which mashes 64) for slight body in this very dry beer
- **Mash time: 60 min**
- **Sparge:** to collect 30 L pre-boil

## Boil

- **75 min boil** — the extra 15 min over ale-IPA's 60 min helps with pilsner-malt DMS volatilization

### Hop schedule

| Time / Stage              | Hops                                | Grams | IBUs (approx) |
|---------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------|---------------|
| **75 min**                | Magnum 14% AA                       | 14 g  | ~28 IBU       |
| **20 min**                | Citra 12% AA                        | 12 g  | ~5 IBU        |
| **5 min**                 | Citra + Mosaic                      | 15 g + 15 g | ~3 IBU |
| **0 min (flameout)**      | Stop boil, cool to 85 °C            | —     | —             |
| **Whirlpool (85 °C, 25 min)** | Citra + Mosaic + Simcoe         | 35 g + 35 g + 30 g | ~15 IBU |
| **Cold-side dry hop (12 °C, 5 days)** | Citra + Mosaic + Strata | 35 g + 35 g + 30 g | (no IBU) |

**Total hops:** ~256 g (~11 g/L) — moderate by IPA standards but plenty for a Cold IPA where every gram expresses cleanly.

## Fermentation

### Pitch yeast at 10–12 °C

- **Pitch yeast at 10 °C / 50 °F** into oxygenated wort (well-oxygenated; lager yeast needs O₂).
- **Yeast pitch:**
  - **Liquid:** 2.5 L starter from 1 vial of WLP800 Pilsner Lager (or Wyeast 2001) — make starter on a stir plate, decant
  - **Dry:** **22 g (2 packs) of Saflager W-34/70** — rehydrate per instructions

### Lager fermentation schedule

| Day  | Temp           | Activity                                     |
|------|----------------|----------------------------------------------|
| 0    | 10 °C          | Pitch                                        |
| 1–2  | 12 °C          | Active fermentation begins                   |
| 3–10 | 13 °C          | Active fermentation                          |
| 10–12 | 14–15 °C      | Free-rise as fermentation slows              |
| 12–14 | 18–20 °C      | **Diacetyl rest** (critical — don't skip)    |
| 14   | Drop to 12 °C  | Cool back down                               |
| 14   | (at 12 °C)     | **Cold-side dry hop drops** under CO₂ purge  |
| 14–19 | 12 °C         | Cold-side dry hop contact (5 days)           |
| 19   | (at 12 °C)     | Drop spent dry hops through bottom valve     |
| 19   | Crash to 0–1 °C | Begin cold lagering                         |
| 19–40 | 0–1 °C        | **Cold lagering (2–3 weeks)**                |
| 40+  | (at 0–1 °C)    | Closed transfer to keg                       |

The cold lagering phase is what makes Cold IPA distinct from a sloppy lager-yeast IPA. **Don't shortcut it.** Two weeks is OK; three is better.

## The diacetyl rest

This step is **critical**. Lager yeast at low temperatures produces diacetyl that's harder to clean up than ale yeast. The diacetyl rest:

- Done at **18–20 °C for 2–3 days**
- Yeast becomes more active at warmer temp → consumes diacetyl
- **Test by warming a sample to room temp, stirring, smelling.** No butter = clean.
- If diacetyl still present after 3 days, hold longer.

Skipping the diacetyl rest is the **#1 Cold IPA mistake**. Don't do it.

## Cold conditioning (lagering)

Two weeks minimum. Three weeks better. Yeast and hop debris drop out completely; flavor integrates.

Optional: **gelatin fining** at the start of cold conditioning improves clarity dramatically. Recommended for Cold IPA.

## Packaging

- **Spunding** to **2.8 vol CO₂** at 0 °C (set spunding valve at ~16 PSI)
- Closed transfer to a sanitizer-purged keg
- Cold storage at 2 °C
- **Drink within 6–10 weeks** — Cold IPA holds up better than NEIPA

## Total timeline

| Day  | Activity                                  |
|------|-------------------------------------------|
| 0    | Brew day                                  |
| 14   | Diacetyl rest                             |
| 14   | Dry hop drops                             |
| 19   | Spent hops dropped, crash to 0 °C         |
| 19–40 | Cold lagering                            |
| 40+  | Closed transfer to keg                    |
| 42+  | Drink                                     |

About **6 weeks brew-to-pour.** This is patient brewing.

## Drinking it

Best at **6–8 °C / 43–46 °F** — colder than other IPAs, more like a pilsner.

In a tall glass — pilsner glass or a tulip both work.

**Goes well with:** spicy food, Asian cuisines (Thai, Vietnamese, Japanese), grilled fish, salads. The dry, crisp profile makes it a remarkable food beer — much more so than NEIPA.

## Variations

### Cold DIPA (Imperial Cold IPA)

Bump to 8–9% ABV with a bigger grain bill (7.5 kg pilsner) and same 20% rice ratio. Increase hops proportionally (~14 g/L). Same lager yeast and process. Longer cold lagering (3+ weeks).

### Single-hop Cold IPA

Excellent for showcasing a hop. Use the same recipe but all-Citra (or all-Mosaic, or all-Galaxy) across whirlpool and dry hop. Lager fermentation lets the variety express clearly. Build a single-hop series like the [`single-hop-experiments.md`](single-hop-experiments.md) framework but with lager yeast.

### "Italian Pilsner" influence

Some modern Cold IPAs draw from the Italian Pilsner tradition (Birrificio Italiano's Tipopils, etc.) — pilsner with a small dry hop. A "lighter Cold IPA" can be brewed at 5–5.5% ABV with the same approach but smaller hop bill.

## Common Cold IPA mistakes

- **Pitching too small.** Lager fermentation needs serious yeast counts. **Pitch big.**
- **Underventing or under-oxygenating wort.** Lager yeast needs more O₂ than ale yeast. Aerate well.
- **Skipping the diacetyl rest.** Critical step. Don't shortcut.
- **Shortcutting cold lagering.** "I'll just keg after 1 week of cold." Result: hazy, sulfury, off-flavors. **Lager longer.**
- **Using ale yeast.** This makes it an IPA-with-pilsner-malt, not a Cold IPA. The lager yeast is the style.
- **Treating it like an IPA on the timeline.** Cold IPA needs 5–7 weeks; respect it.
- **Using too many adjuncts.** 20% rice/corn is the sweet spot; 30% can produce a thin, beer-like-water result.

## What this teaches you

Brewing Cold IPA well requires:

- **Patience** — the lagering step
- **Pitch discipline** — bigger pitches than you're used to
- **Temperature control** — cold ferm + warm rest + cold crash
- **DO discipline** — the clean, hop-forward profile shows oxidation more clearly than NEIPA does

These are all **transferable skills** — a brewer who can make a clean Cold IPA can probably make a clean pilsner, and probably a much better lager generally. Cold IPA is "lager training" disguised as IPA.
