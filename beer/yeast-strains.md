## Categories

### Ale yeast — *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*

- Top-fermenting, warm temps (typically 18–22 °C / 64–72 °F)
- Subcategories worth their own notes:
  - **English ale** (Fuller's, Whitbread B, Nottingham) — esters, mineral character
  - **American ale** (US-05, WLP001) — clean, neutral
  - **Belgian** (T-58, Westmalle) — phenolics, banana, clove, peppery
  - **German Hefeweizen** — strong banana / clove from POF+ wheat strains
  - **Saison** (DuPont, French) — high attenuation, peppery, sometimes funky
  - **Kveik** — high-temp Norwegian farmhouse strains, ester-driven, fast

### Lager yeast — *Saccharomyces pastorianus*

- Bottom-fermenting, cold temps (typically 8–12 °C / 46–54 °F)
- Slow, clean, sulfur on the way through
- Two main lineages:
  - **Saaz / Carlsberg group** — closer to *S. eubayanus* parent
  - **Frohberg group** — more attenuative, common in modern lagers

### *Brettanomyces*

- Slow, oxygen-tolerant, produces 4-ethylphenol (barnyard, leather) and acetic acid in oxygenated conditions
- Used solo (100% Brett saisons), in secondary (lambics, sours), or as a "house" microbe

### Mixed cultures

- Lambic / Flemish reds — *S. cerevisiae* + *Brett* + *Lactobacillus* + *Pediococcus*
- Wild captures — environmental, harder to control, very expressive

## What to record per strain

When this file gets fleshed out, each strain entry should cover:

- **Source / vendor / equivalent codes** (Wyeast, White Labs, Imperial, Lallemand, etc.)
- **Temperature range** (and what changes at each end)
- **Apparent attenuation** (% of sugars consumed)
- **Flocculation** (low / medium / high)
- **Ester / phenolic profile** — what flavors it's known for
- **Pitch rate guidance** — typical cell count per liter per °P
- **Diacetyl tendency** — does it leave a slick, do you need a rest
- **Repitching behavior** — how many generations stays clean
