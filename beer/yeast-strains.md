# Yeast strains for NEIPA

Yeast is one leg of the hop / yeast / water triangle. For NEIPA specifically, you're choosing for **biotransformation activity, ester profile, suspension behavior, and ability to ferment cleanly under heavy hop loads**. A wrong-yeast NEIPA — even with great hops, water, and process — won't be great.

This file focuses on **NEIPA-relevant strains**. For a broader strain reference covering ales, lagers, and Brett, see "other styles" — to be added later as the repo grows beyond NEIPA.

## What you're looking for in a NEIPA yeast

### 1. Biotransformation activity

The yeast should produce **β-glucosidase** (cleaves hop glycosides → frees thiols) and **monoterpene reductases** (geraniol → β-citronellol). Different strains do this at very different rates.

### 2. Esters that complement hops

NEIPA wants **soft tropical / stone-fruit esters** from yeast that *amplify* the hop character — not compete with it. Want: peach, apricot, soft tropical. Don't want: banana (Hefeweizen), clove (Belgian), strong pear/banana (some Saisons).

### 3. Low-to-medium flocculation

NEIPA's **stable haze depends on yeast staying in suspension**. Highly flocculant strains (Nottingham, Wyeast 1968) drop bright fast — bad for the style. Mid-low flocculation = haze that holds.

### 4. Tolerates the dry hop

Heavy dry hopping disrupts yeast in suspension, can crash flocculation, and contributes to hop creep. You want a strain that handles this without going fully off — keeps producing CO₂ to scrub introduced O₂, and finishes attenuation cleanly.

### 5. Ferments to a sensible terminal gravity

Around **75–80% apparent attenuation** is the NEIPA sweet spot. Lower (< 72%) = sweet/cloying. Higher (> 85%) = thin/dry, fights the soft mouthfeel.

## Top strains

### London Ale III (Wyeast 1318 / WLP066 / Imperial A38 Juice / GY054)

**The original NEIPA workhorse.** Most of the foundational New England breweries (Trillium, Tree House, Burlington Beer Co.) used or still use this strain or a close relative.

- **Origin:** A London ale strain (Boddington's lineage).
- **Attenuation:** 71–75% (medium). Leaves some body — good for soft mouthfeel.
- **Flocculation:** Medium-low. Stays hazy, drops out slowly.
- **Temperature range:** 17–22 °C / 64–72 °F. Free-rise to 21 °C late in fermentation for aroma.
- **Ester profile:** Soft fruity — peach, apricot, slight tropical, mild vanilla.
- **Biotransformation:** Strong. Excellent at thiol liberation and geraniol conversion.
- **Notes:** **The default** unless you have a specific reason to pick something else. Very forgiving. Plays well with almost any hop combo.

### Vermont Ale / "Conan" (Imperial A04 Barbarian / GY054 / WLP095 Burlington Ale)

The strain behind **The Alchemist's Heady Topper and Focal Banger** — and a thousand homebrew clones.

- **Origin:** Likely from The Alchemist, propagated through homebrew banking.
- **Attenuation:** 72–80% (medium-high). Slightly drier than London III.
- **Flocculation:** Medium-low. Hazy.
- **Temperature range:** 18–22 °C / 64–72 °F.
- **Ester profile:** **Distinctive peach** — the signature Conan note. Soft tropical underneath.
- **Biotransformation:** Strong. Different ester signature than London III but similar biotransformation work.
- **Notes:** If you've had Heady Topper and want to chase that character, this is the yeast. Slightly more peach-forward than London III; somewhat narrower flavor window.

### Lallemand Verdant IPA (dry)

**The dry-yeast option** that's actually competitive with the liquids. A modern strain selected specifically for hazy IPA.

- **Origin:** Lallemand's modern IPA-focused yeast, released ~2020.
- **Attenuation:** 75–80%.
- **Flocculation:** Medium. Stays hazy at fermentation temp; drops cleaner with crashing.
- **Temperature range:** 18–25 °C / 64–77 °F. Tolerates higher temps better than London III.
- **Ester profile:** Soft tropical, peach, mild ester with English ale undertones.
- **Biotransformation:** Solid. Less studied than the London III lineage but the brewer feedback is consistent.
- **Notes:** **The most accessible NEIPA yeast.** Cheap, available everywhere, no starter required, ferments fast and clean. Excellent first-yeast for a homebrewer getting into NEIPAs. Hop creep tolerance is reportedly above average.

### Imperial A24 Dry Hop / Lallemand New England

A US East Coast IPA-focused yeast.

- **Attenuation:** 72–78%.
- **Flocculation:** Medium-low.
- **Ester profile:** Tropical fruit, citrus, soft.
- **Biotransformation:** Strong.
- **Notes:** Similar territory to London III. Some homebrewers prefer it for slightly more pronounced tropical character.

### Wyeast 1098 British Ale / WLP007 Dry English Ale

An older British ale strain that pre-dates the NEIPA era but works well.

- **Attenuation:** 75–80%. Higher than London III, drier finish.
- **Flocculation:** Medium-high. Will drop more bright than London III — less ideal for stable haze.
- **Ester profile:** Mild fruit, slightly more "English" character.
- **Notes:** Workable for hazy IPAs that want a slightly cleaner / drier finish. Not the first-pick if max haze is the goal.

### Hornindal Kveik (Imperial A43 / Omega OYL-091)

**The kveik option** that has the strongest hop-pairing reputation.

- **Origin:** Norwegian farmhouse yeast (kveik culture from Hornindal, Norway).
- **Attenuation:** 75–82%. Drier finish than London III.
- **Flocculation:** Variable — some cell lines drop fast, some stay hazy.
- **Temperature range:** **22–35 °C / 72–95 °F**. Ferments fast and warm.
- **Ester profile:** **Tropical fruit** — pineapple, mango, soft citrus. The kveik that tastes most like a hop-forward beer wants a yeast to taste.
- **Biotransformation:** Very strong. Liberates thiols aggressively.
- **Notes:** **Underrated for NEIPA.** Ferments at high temps (25–30 °C) → 3–4 day fermentation, less hop scrubbing during long ferms, biotransformation in days not weeks. Pairs especially well with **Citra, Mosaic, Galaxy, Strata**. The "modern NEIPA shortcut" if you want to brew faster. Some find the yeast character too distinctive — try a small batch first.

### Voss Kveik (Imperial A43 / Omega OYL-061 / Lallemand Voss)

The **citrus-forward kveik**.

- **Attenuation:** 80–85%. Very dry. Can over-attenuate a NEIPA.
- **Temperature range:** 27–40 °C.
- **Ester profile:** **Orange/citrus** — pronounced. Some find it too "fake orange."
- **Notes:** Less ideal than Hornindal for NEIPA in my opinion — the orange ester can dominate. Better for pale ales than haze-forward IPAs.

### Espe / Other kveik cultures

Many other kveik cultures exist (Espe, Stranda, Granvin). Each has a different ester signature. Hornindal remains the most NEIPA-suitable in general; the others are worth experimenting with.

## Pitch rate and yeast health

NEIPA needs a **healthy, vigorous pitch** — biotransformation requires active yeast.

- **Liquid yeast:** make a starter. 1.5–2× the standard rate (so for a 23 L NEIPA at OG 1.065, plan a ~2 L starter from 1 vial / pack).
- **Dry yeast:** rehydrate per instructions. **Pitch 11–14 g for 23 L** at OG 1.065 (one and a half packs). Underpitching is the most common dry-yeast mistake.

A weak / underpitched fermentation:

- Slower start → more O₂ exposure window
- Less biotransformation
- More fermentation stress → off-flavors
- Worse hop creep tolerance

## Fermentation temperature schedule

A typical NEIPA ferm:

| Day | Temp        | What's happening                                              |
|-----|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0   | 18 °C       | Pitch, yeast lag                                              |
| 1   | 18 °C       | Active fermentation begins, krausen forms                     |
| 2   | 19–20 °C    | Free-rise begins, fermentation peak                           |
| 2–3 | 20 °C       | **Active-ferm dry hop drops** at ~50–70% attenuation          |
| 4–5 | 20–21 °C    | Late fermentation, biotransformation                          |
| 6–7 | 20 °C       | FG approached, optional diacetyl rest                         |
| 7   | Crash to 13 °C | **Cold-side dry hop drops** at 13 °C (Janish-recommended cool dry hop) |
| 7–10 | 13 °C     | Cold-side dry hop contact (3 days)                            |
| 10–11 | 4 °C     | Crash for clarification + finishing                           |
| 11+ | Package     | Closed transfer to keg under CO₂                              |

Adjust for your strain's preferred temperature range. Kveik strains compress this whole schedule into 4–5 days.

## Repitching

NEIPA yeast can be repitched, but with caveats:

- **Hop polyphenols stress yeast** — repitched cells from a heavy NEIPA may be less vigorous. Limit reuses to 2–3 generations.
- **Top-cropping** (skimming the krausen layer) gives healthier yeast than bottom-cropping (collecting from the cone) for ester-positive strains.
- **Wash and store cold** under sterile beer or wort.
- For homebrew scale, **fresh dry yeast every batch** is cheap and easy. Repitching is more relevant at semi-pro scale.
