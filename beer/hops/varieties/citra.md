# Citra (HBC 394)

The defining hop of modern American IPA. If hops were genres of music, Citra would be the song that launched a movement — every contemporary hop bill is in dialogue with it, even when avoiding it.

This file goes deeper than the [`varieties.md`](../varieties.md) entry. For brewing-decision-level overview, start there.

## Pedigree and history

- **Bred by:** Hop Breeding Company (a joint venture between John I. Haas and Yakima Chief Ranches)
- **Released:** 2008
- **Origin parents:** Hallertauer Mittelfrüh × U.S. Tettnanger × East Kent Goldings + several wild U.S. selections — a complex multi-parent pedigree from the public USDA breeding program before being commercialized by HBC
- **Plant variety protection:** registered, propagated only via licensed growers

The release date matters. **Citra became commercially significant just as the American craft beer industry was hitting an inflection point** in the early 2010s. It arrived right as brewers were searching for "the next Cascade" — and it became a generational hop instead. By 2015, Citra was the highest-demand specialty hop in the world.

## Oil and acid composition

| Property                | Typical range            |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Alpha acids             | 11–14%                    |
| Beta acids              | 3.0–4.5%                  |
| Cohumulone              | 22–24% of alpha           |
| Total oils              | 2.2–2.8 mL/100g           |
| Myrcene                 | 60–70% of oil             |
| Humulene                | 7–12%                     |
| Caryophyllene           | 6–8%                      |
| Farnesene               | < 1%                      |
| Geraniol                | 0.5–1.0%                  |
| Linalool                | moderate                  |
| **Thiol precursors**    | **Very high (3MH, 4MMP)** |

The defining chemistry is the **thiol load**. Citra is one of the few hops with high concentrations of **both** 3-mercaptohexanol (3MH) and 4-mercapto-4-methylpentan-2-one (4MMP) precursors. This is what produces the distinctive "lychee, passionfruit, grapefruit" character — and it's why Citra is so dramatically transformed by yeast biotransformation in NEIPAs.

## Sensory profile

### Headline descriptors

- **Citrus** — grapefruit, lemon, tangerine, orange (varies by lot)
- **Tropical** — passionfruit, lychee, mango, papaya
- **Stone fruit** — peach, white peach (especially with biotransformation)
- **Gooseberry** — particularly with thiol-active yeast
- **Slight pithy / herbal note** — at high concentrations

### What you actually smell

A typical fresh Citra bag will smell like:

- Open the bag → **bright tropical punch** (passionfruit, lychee)
- Rub between palms → **citrus zest** comes forward (grapefruit, tangerine)
- 30 seconds later → **softer floral notes**, light pine

The bag-to-bag variation is real:

- Some lots are **more grapefruit-leaning** (especially older crops)
- Some are **more passionfruit / tropical** (newer crops, ideal storage)
- Some are **more melon / floral** (lower-myrcene lots)

This year-to-year and lot-to-lot variation is one reason **single-hop Citra brews come out differently** even when you think you're brewing the same recipe. The hop *is* different from year to year.

### What "old Citra" smells like

The signature degradation: **onion, garlic, sweat.** This happens because Citra's high thiol load is also chemically vulnerable to oxidation — the same compounds that produce its juiciness produce sulfur off-flavors when degraded.

Distinguishing fresh from aged:

- Fresh: clean, vivid, layered — passionfruit / lychee / grapefruit
- Slightly aged (6+ months at warm storage): muted, less complex
- Aged: **sulfur character** — garlic powder, sweat, oxidized onion. **Don't dry hop with this.** Bittering use only.

This is why **Citra has the strictest freshness rules** of any common hop. A 6-month-old Citra at room temp is significantly degraded; a 12-month-old Citra at room temp is borderline unusable for aroma.

## Biotransformation behavior

Citra responds **dramatically** to active-fermentation dry hopping. The yeast does three things:

1. **Cleaves bound thiol precursors** → frees 3MH and 4MMP into the beer (more "tropical" character)
2. **Converts geraniol → β-citronellol** → softer, rosier citrus character
3. **Cleaves citrus glycosides** → liberates aromatic compounds bound to sugar molecules in the hop

The net effect: a Citra dry hop that goes in at active fermentation tastes **markedly more tropical and complex** than the same dose added cold-side after fermentation. Different hop, essentially.

This is the chemistry behind the "active-ferm dry hop" stage in the [DEAM-aware NEIPA recipe](../../recipes/neipa-template.md) and Janish's recommendation in the MBAA TQ paper.

For brewing strategy:

- **Whirlpool charge:** captures the immediate Citra character (citrus, tropical) — survivable compounds
- **Active-ferm dry hop:** maximizes biotransformation — tropical / lychee / passionfruit character
- **Cold-side dry hop:** brightness, freshness — what you smell when you crack the can

A NEIPA that uses Citra **only cold-side** is missing 30–40% of the variety's potential.

## Hop pairings

Citra plays well with almost everything, which is part of why it dominates modern recipes. The classic pairings:

### Citra + Mosaic — the foundational duo

Adds dank/blueberry/pine to Citra's tropical/citrus. The most-cloned NEIPA combination of all time.

### Citra + Galaxy

Two thiol monsters together. Risk: too much "juicy" can become "muddy" without a contrasting hop. Add Mosaic or Simcoe for complexity.

### Citra + Nelson Sauvin

Bridges tropical (Citra) with white wine / gooseberry (Nelson). The "dry hopped Sauv blanc" of beer.

### Citra + Simcoe

Citra's brightness + Simcoe's pine/dank. A classic American IPA pairing that bridges WCIPA and NEIPA.

### Citra + Strata

Both thiol-rich. Strata adds dank/berry that Citra lacks. Polarizing combo — distinct character.

### Citra + Cascade + Centennial

Old-school WCIPA with Citra added for modern brightness. The "modern WCIPA" template.

### Citra + Sabro

Coconut + tropical fruit. Sabro is loud; Citra is the more familiar voice. Use Citra at 60%+, Sabro at 20–30%.

## Substitutions and adjacent hops

When Citra is unavailable / expensive / aged:

- **Mosaic** — different but in the same conversational space
- **Galaxy** — more passionfruit-leaning, less general "lychee"
- **Idaho 7** — more "tropical resin," less juicy
- **El Dorado** — more "candy-tropical," less defined
- **Pahto (HBC 682)** — newer, even higher 3MH thiol load, claims to be "next Citra"
- **HBC 472 / Krush** — newer melon-citrus, different but interesting

There's no true "Citra substitute" — the variety is distinctive enough that subbing it changes the beer character. But these get you in the same neighborhood.

## Recipe positioning

### Headline recipes

- **Many famous NEIPAs:** Hill Farmstead Susan, Tree House Julius, Trillium Congress Street — all heavy Citra
- **Modern WCIPA renaissance recipes:** Russian River STS Pils, Modern Times' Citra Slick
- **Hazy DIPAs:** essentially every popular Hazy DIPA has substantial Citra

### How much Citra in a recipe

Approximate guidelines:

- **As "the" hop (single-hop):** 12–14 g/L total works
- **As lead in a 4-hop combo:** 40–60% of the bill
- **As supporting:** 15–30% of the bill
- **As "punch":** even 5–10% of the bill adds noticeable Citra character

A famous trick: **a small late dry-hop addition of Citra alone** can lift any IPA toward "more interesting." The thiol contribution is potent at low doses.

## Cryo Citra

Yakima Chief's lupulin-powder version of Citra. Notable because:

- **Roughly 2× the alpha acids and oils** per gram of T-90
- **Less polyphenol/leaf material** → less astringency at heavy hop loads
- **Slightly less complex** than T-90 in some hands — the bract material contributes some supporting character that Cryo lacks
- **Most common Cryo variety** — almost universally available

Use **half the rate** of T-90 Citra for the same intensity. For NEIPA cold-side dry hop, **Cryo Citra is excellent** — bright, less astringent, less pH rise.

For whirlpool: T-90 Citra works fine (the polyphenol load matters less hot-side). Some brewers use Cryo Citra in whirlpool too; works either way.

## Common Citra mistakes

- **Using old Citra and not realizing it.** The freshness rules are strict. **Smell every bag.**
- **Overrelying on Citra alone.** Single-hop Citra is fine but rarely the most interesting beer you can make. Pair it.
- **Putting all the Citra cold-side.** You're missing biotransformation. Distribute across whirlpool / active-ferm / cold-side.
- **Treating "Citra" as a fixed thing.** Specific lots vary. Smell, evaluate, adjust dose accordingly.
- **Using Citra in a Belgian / wheat / saison.** It can work but the citrus character clashes with most Belgian yeast esters. Better hops for those styles.
