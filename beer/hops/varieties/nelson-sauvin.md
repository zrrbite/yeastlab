# Nelson Sauvin

The hop named after a wine. **The variety with the strongest single sensory association in the modern hop world** — Nelson Sauvin tastes like Sauvignon Blanc wine, and almost nothing else does. A New Zealand hop that became a cult favorite among brewers and an inevitable surcharge on every bag.

## Pedigree and history

- **Bred by:** New Zealand Hops Ltd (now part of NZ Hops)
- **Released:** 2000
- **Parents:** Smoothcone × New Zealand male
- **Production:** Nelson region, top of New Zealand's South Island
- **Naming:** Named after both the **Nelson region** where it's grown and the **Sauvignon Blanc** wine character it produces

The Sauvignon Blanc connection is genuine, not marketing. Nelson Sauvin contains exceptionally high levels of **3-mercaptohexanol (3MH)** — the same thiol compound that gives Sauvignon Blanc wine its distinctive gooseberry / passionfruit / grapefruit character. Wine science recognized 3MH's role in Sauvignon Blanc in the 1990s; brewers exploiting it in beer is more recent.

This is one of the few cases where **hop sensory character traces back to a single, identifiable, named compound** — most hop character is a complex mixture.

## Oil and acid composition

| Property                | Typical range            |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Alpha acids             | 12–13%                    |
| Beta acids              | 7–8.5%                    |
| Cohumulone              | 24–26% of alpha           |
| Total oils              | 1.0–1.4 mL/100g (modest)  |
| Myrcene                 | 25–35% of oil             |
| Humulene                | 10–14%                    |
| Caryophyllene           | 8–12%                     |
| Farnesene               | 1–2%                      |
| Geraniol                | low                       |
| Linalool                | low                       |
| **3MH thiol precursor** | **Exceptionally high — defining feature** |

The 3MH precursor load is **the chemistry of Nelson Sauvin in a nutshell.** Total oil content is modest by modern standards, but the specific compound concentration of 3MH is exceptional.

The relatively high humulene + caryophyllene + farnesene gives Nelson its **noble-hop-like** secondary character — a bit "European elegance" beneath the wild thiol punch.

## Sensory profile

### Headline descriptors

- **White grape** — the defining note. Specifically Sauvignon Blanc.
- **Gooseberry** — fresh, slightly tart fruit
- **Passionfruit** — tropical aspect (3MH-derived)
- **Grapefruit** — citrus aspect (also 3MH-derived)
- **Fresh-cut grass** — sometimes described as "vegetal" — actually a Sauvignon Blanc characteristic
- **Cat's piss** (in a positive wine-tasting way — distinct from Citra's onion-aged character) — at high concentrations

### What you actually smell

A typical fresh Nelson Sauvin bag will:

- Open the bag → **immediate white-wine punch**, distinctive
- Rub between palms → **gooseberry / grapefruit pith** comes forward
- 30 seconds later → **softer fruity / floral notes**
- The character is **unambiguous** — you'll know it's Nelson Sauvin in 5 seconds

Compared to other "tropical" hops (Citra, Galaxy):

- **Citra:** broader citrus + tropical, "lychee + grapefruit"
- **Galaxy:** focused passionfruit + peach
- **Nelson Sauvin:** specifically *wine-like* — gooseberry + Sauvignon Blanc character that no other hop captures

### Lot-to-lot variation

Nelson is moderately variable:

- **Years with strong wine character** (the "good Nelson years")
- **Years with more general "hop" character** — less obviously wine-like

The wine character is **what most brewers want from Nelson** — if you don't get it, the variety is essentially "expensive moderate hop." Always smell-test before brewing.

### What "old Nelson Sauvin" smells like

Like other thiol-rich hops, Nelson can develop **sulfur off-aromas** over time — though the degradation is somewhat different in character than Citra. Aged Nelson can become:

- **Less wine-like, more general grape**
- **More vegetal, less defined**
- **Sometimes mild onion/garlic** notes

Always smell-test. Old Nelson is "expensive disappointment" if you brew with it for the wine character.

## Biotransformation behavior

Nelson Sauvin is **textbook biotransformation hop**, similar to Galaxy. The yeast cleavage of bound 3MH precursors **dramatically amplifies** the wine character.

Specifically:

1. **Yeast β-glucosidase frees 3MH** from glycoside-bound precursors → much more free 3MH in the beer than a cold extraction would yield
2. **3MH → 3MHA conversion** by yeast → 3MHA has even lower threshold than 3MH (4 ng/L vs 55 ng/L) and shifts character toward intense passionfruit
3. **Linalool isomerization** → softer floral notes

A Nelson Sauvin beer brewed with **thiol-active yeast (London III, Conan, Hornindal kveik) and active-fermentation dry hopping** can taste **dramatically more "wine-like"** than the same beer brewed with US-05 yeast and cold-side dry hopping alone.

For maximum Nelson character: **active-ferm dry hop with London III or Hornindal kveik**.

## Hop pairings

### Nelson Sauvin + Citra

Both thiol-rich. Nelson provides the wine character; Citra provides the broader tropical/citrus brightness. Excellent. Common in modern hazy IPAs.

### Nelson Sauvin + Galaxy

Both at the high end of thiol concentrations. Together they're **almost overwhelming** — sometimes called "thiol bombs." Use with a balancing terpene-driven hop (Mosaic, Simcoe) for complexity.

### Nelson Sauvin + Riwaka

The classic NZ pairing. Riwaka's intense grapefruit + Nelson's wine character = clean Antipodean profile. Both are NZ hops with terroir-aligned character.

### Nelson Sauvin + Motueka

Another NZ classic. Motueka's lime/citrus + Nelson's wine = light, refreshing, distinctly NZ.

### Nelson Sauvin + Hallertau Blanc

The "Sauv Blanc" pairing — both hops have wine character. Sophisticated, refined.

### Nelson Sauvin + Mosaic

Less typical but works. Mosaic provides depth Nelson alone lacks.

### Nelson Sauvin in Saison

Excellent. Nelson's wine character pairs beautifully with Saison's funky/dry profile. Some of the best modern Saisons use Nelson as the headline hop.

### Nelson Sauvin in single-hop Pilsner

Surprising but excellent. The wine/grape character with a clean lager fermentation produces a distinctive cup.

## Substitutions and adjacent hops

When Nelson Sauvin is unavailable / expensive / aged:

- **Hallertau Blanc** — German "Sauvignon Blanc" hop; cleaner, less intense, similar territory
- **Galaxy** — different fruit (passionfruit vs gooseberry), but similar thiol intensity
- **Pacific Jade** — NZ, lighter wine character
- **Sorachi Ace** — Japanese hop with dill/lemongrass character; not a Nelson sub but sometimes used in similar adventurous recipes
- **Wai-iti** — softer NZ hop with similar light-citrus character

There's no real Nelson Sauvin substitute. The variety is too distinctive.

## Recipe positioning

### Headline recipes

- **Many Saisons** (Hill Farmstead's Anna and similar)
- **Modern hazy IPAs** featuring Nelson alongside Citra/Galaxy
- **NZ-focused single-hop showcases**
- **Wine-leaning specialty beers** (Brut IPA, dry-hopped brett beers)

### How much Nelson Sauvin in a recipe

Nelson is **expensive enough to dose carefully**, and its character is intense enough to work at moderate doses:

- **Single-hop Nelson:** 8–12 g/L (lower than typical NEIPA — Nelson is loud)
- **As lead in a 4-hop combo:** 25–40% of the bill
- **As supporting / featured:** 15–30%
- **As accent:** 5–15% (even small Nelson contributions are detectable)

Common move: **Nelson Sauvin as 20–30% of an otherwise Citra/Mosaic NEIPA bill** adds a distinctive wine character without overwhelming the overall beer.

## Practical sourcing notes

Nelson Sauvin is **expensive and supply-constrained**:

- **Nelson region production is small** — limited supply globally
- **Demand consistently exceeds supply** — frequent shortages
- **Prices are typically 1.5–2× the price of Citra** per pound
- **For homebrewers:** buy when you see fresh stock; doesn't pay to wait

Suppliers to trust: Yakima Valley Hops (carries some NZ), Hops from NZ (direct), specialty NZ-focused homebrew shops.

## The Riwaka BBC question

**Riwaka BBC** (or "Riwaka concentrated lupulin") is a NZ-specific hop product similar to Cryo for Riwaka. It's worth knowing about for Nelson Sauvin too: NZ Hops occasionally produces concentrated lupulin versions of Nelson Sauvin under various names. Worth seeking out — doesn't appear in regular catalogs, often available only through specialty channels.

## Common Nelson Sauvin mistakes

- **Cold-side-only.** Wastes the biotransformation potential. Distribute across whirlpool and active-ferm for max wine character.
- **Pairing with cleaner yeast.** US-05 won't deliver the biotransformation; the beer will be a milder version of itself.
- **Overdosing.** More Nelson past 5–6 g/L isn't more wine character — it's more vegetal / "fresh grass" character. Less can be more.
- **Substituting "another tropical hop."** Galaxy is tropical but doesn't have the wine character. Citra is tropical but isn't gooseberry.
- **Using old Nelson Sauvin and not realizing the wine character is gone.** Smell-test every batch.
