# Galaxy

Australia's signature hop. The single most aromatically intense hop in the modern catalog, and the closest thing the Australian hop industry has to a flagship variety. **If Citra is the modern American IPA standard, Galaxy is its Antipodean counterpart** — distinct enough that "Galaxy beer" is a recognizable subgenre.

## Pedigree and history

- **Bred by:** Hop Products Australia (HPA), the dominant Australian hop breeder
- **Released:** 2009
- **Origin:** Cross between an unnamed female (German selection) and the Australian J78 male
- **Production location:** Almost exclusively Tasmania and Victoria, Australia
- **Cultivation note:** Australia harvests in **March–April** (Southern Hemisphere), giving Australian hops a different "freshness clock" than American hops (which harvest August–September). A "fresh" Australian hop in November in the US is a 7-month-old harvest; "fresh" American Citra in November is 2 months old.

The release timing — 2009 — placed Galaxy at the intersection of the American craft beer revolution and Australia's emerging beer scene. It became famous internationally before it was famous at home.

## Oil and acid composition

| Property                | Typical range            |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Alpha acids             | 13–16%                    |
| Beta acids              | 5–7%                      |
| Cohumulone              | 32–36% of alpha           |
| Total oils              | 2.5–3.5+ mL/100g (often higher than Citra) |
| Myrcene                 | 35–45% of oil             |
| Humulene                | 2–4% (low)                |
| Caryophyllene           | 4–6%                      |
| Farnesene               | 0.5–1.5%                  |
| Geraniol                | very low                  |
| Linalool                | very low                  |
| **Thiol precursors**    | **Extremely high (3MH especially, also 4MMP)** |

The headline: **Galaxy is the most thiol-rich common hop** by a wide margin. The 3MH precursor load is so high that even relatively small Galaxy charges produce dramatic passionfruit character in beer with thiol-active yeast.

## Sensory profile

### Headline descriptors

- **Passionfruit** — the defining Galaxy note. More intense and "true" than any other hop.
- **Peach** — ripe, syrupy
- **Citrus** — broad, often grapefruit / mandarin
- **Tropical fruit** — pineapple, mango, papaya
- **Slight herbal / vegetal** at concentration — sometimes described as "Galaxy funk"

### What you actually smell

A typical fresh Galaxy bag will:

- Open the bag → **explosive passionfruit** punch — almost cartoonishly intense
- Rub between palms → **stone fruit** comes forward (peach especially)
- Wait 30 sec → **citrus / mandarin** notes appear
- Linger → **slight herbal / dank** undertone

Galaxy is **louder per gram** than most hops. A 50 g whirlpool addition of Galaxy can dominate a beer; the same dose of Cascade is barely detectable.

### Lot-to-lot variation

Galaxy is notoriously variable year-to-year:

- **Some years (2018, 2021):** explosive passionfruit, top-tier Galaxy character
- **Other years:** less passionfruit, more general "tropical," sometimes more vegetal
- **Drought years in Australia:** can produce Galaxy with reduced oil content overall

This is one reason **vintage matters with Galaxy more than with most hops.** A 2024 harvest crop and a 2023 harvest crop can taste meaningfully different.

### What "old Galaxy" smells like

Like Citra, Galaxy degrades into **sulfur off-aromas** — onion, garlic, sweat. The thiol degradation pathway is similar.

But Galaxy has a second degradation mode: **loss of "punch" without obvious off-aromas.** A 12-month-old Galaxy at room temp may smell muted but not actively bad — and you might use it in a beer thinking it'll be fine, only to find the finished beer tastes weak. Always smell, always evaluate intensity.

## Biotransformation behavior

Galaxy is **the textbook biotransformation hop.** Its high 3MH precursor load means that **yeast cleavage liberates dramatic amounts of free 3MH (grapefruit/passionfruit) and 3MHA (intense passionfruit)** during active fermentation.

The biotransformation contribution is so significant that:

- **Galaxy in active-fermentation dry hop** → very different beer than Galaxy in cold-side dry hop alone
- **Yeast strain matters massively** — London III, Conan, and Hornindal kveik are particularly good at thiol cleavage; cleaner ale strains (US-05) less so
- **Cold-side-only Galaxy** wastes the variety's potential — you get the surface aromatics but miss the depth

For a "Galaxy showcase" NEIPA, **at least 30–50% of the Galaxy load should go in active-ferm** to maximize biotransformation.

## Hop pairings

### Galaxy + Citra

The Antipodean / American summit. Both thiol-heavy; together they're **almost too much** for some palates. Mosaic or Simcoe as a balancer is common.

### Galaxy + Mosaic

Galaxy's passionfruit + Mosaic's dank/blueberry. Excellent. Mosaic provides the depth that Galaxy alone can lack.

### Galaxy + Vic Secret + Riwaka

The Antipodean trifecta. Vic Secret adds pine/herbal; Riwaka adds lifted citrus. Distinctive, more "Southern Hemisphere" than the American Citra-Mosaic axis.

### Galaxy + Nelson Sauvin

Both wine-leaning. Galaxy's passionfruit + Nelson's Sauvignon-Blanc-like gooseberry. Sophisticated, polarizing.

### Galaxy + Sabro

Galaxy's punch + Sabro's coconut. Tropical maximalist.

### Galaxy in WCIPA?

Possible but unusual. Galaxy's "soft tropical" character can clash with WCIPA's "hard pine/bitter" profile. If used in WCIPA, **pair with traditional Cs** (Cascade, Centennial) to ground it.

## Substitutions and adjacent hops

When Galaxy is unavailable / expensive / aged:

- **Vic Secret** — same farm (HPA), more pine, less passionfruit, often a substitute when Galaxy supply is tight
- **Pahto (HBC 682)** — newer American hop with very high 3MH; closest American thiol equivalent
- **Citra** — different but in the same conversational space
- **Eclipse** — newer Australian, more mandarin-orange than Galaxy; not a 1:1 sub but in the family
- **Strata** — more dank, not passionfruit, but high thiol load

There's no true Galaxy substitute. The variety is too distinctive.

## Recipe positioning

### Headline recipes

- **Stone & Wood Pacific Ale** — Australian classic, Galaxy as the lead hop
- **Many famous American hazies** — Galaxy as the "punch hop" alongside Citra/Mosaic
- **Pliny the Younger (some years)** — Galaxy in the dry hop

### How much Galaxy in a recipe

Approximate guidelines:

- **Single-hop Galaxy:** 8–14 g/L (Galaxy's intensity means you can go lower than with most hops)
- **As lead in a 4-hop combo:** 30–50% of the bill — leaving room for grounding hops
- **As supporting / punch:** 15–25%
- **Don't push past 60%** of the bill — Galaxy alone can become muddled / vegetal

A common Galaxy mistake: **dose too high, expecting "more Galaxy = more passionfruit."** Beyond a certain point you stop getting more flavor and start getting more polyphenol/vegetal. **Galaxy at 5 g/L is usually better than Galaxy at 10 g/L** in the dry hop.

## Galaxy Cryo

Available; uncommon outside specialty suppliers (Australian production of Cryo is limited compared to American). When available:

- **Roughly 2× the intensity per gram** of T-90 Galaxy
- **Less polyphenol / pH rise**
- **Use half the rate** as T-90 Galaxy

For Galaxy specifically, **Cryo is genuinely useful at high hop loads** because of how easily Galaxy alone can push polyphenol astringency. If you're running a 14+ g/L hop bill with significant Galaxy, **using Cryo for the cold-side dry hop** is well worth the cost and supply hassle.

## Practical sourcing notes

Galaxy is **expensive and supply-volatile**:

- **Supply tightness is real.** Australian hop production is much smaller than American; Galaxy specifically can sell out months in advance.
- **Prices have risen substantially since 2018** as global demand outpaced production.
- **For homebrewers:** buy when you see it, even if you don't have an immediate plan to brew. Galaxy stores well frozen.
- **Suppliers to trust:** Yakima Valley Hops (carries some Australian), specialty Australian/NZ-focused homebrew shops, Hops from NZ.

## The Galaxy "vintage" question

Some brewers track **harvest year** for Galaxy more carefully than for other hops because vintage variation is real. A few practices:

- **Buy multiple bags from a single harvest** — at least you know they're consistent
- **Open one bag from a new harvest immediately** to evaluate; smell-test before brewing with the rest
- **Keep a notebook** of Galaxy harvests you've used and what they tasted like — over years this becomes a useful personal reference

## Common Galaxy mistakes

- **Thinking "Galaxy means passionfruit" universally.** Some lots are more general tropical / less defined passionfruit. Smell first.
- **Overdosing.** More Galaxy past ~5 g/L isn't more passionfruit; it's more astringency.
- **Cold-side-only.** Wastes the biotransformation potential. Distribute across whirlpool and active-ferm.
- **Pairing with cleaner yeast (US-05).** Less biotransformation than London III / Conan / kveik. Galaxy's potential isn't fully expressed.
- **Substituting freely.** "I'll use Vic Secret instead because it's the same farm." Vic Secret is *similar*, not equivalent. Different beer.
