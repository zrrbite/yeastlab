# Mosaic (HBC 369)

The other half of modern American IPA's foundational duo. **If Citra is the soprano, Mosaic is the bass** — providing the dank/blueberry/pine depth that makes Citra's brightness sound finished. The "Citra + Mosaic" combination is the most-cloned NEIPA hop pairing of all time.

## Pedigree and history

- **Bred by:** Hop Breeding Company (HBC)
- **Released:** 2012
- **Parents:** **Daughter of Simcoe** crossed with a Nugget-derived male
- **The Simcoe lineage matters** — Mosaic inherits some of Simcoe's dank/pine/resin character but carries it in a different direction (more fruit-forward, less austere)
- **Production:** Major US hop, widely available, less supply-volatile than Citra or Galaxy

Mosaic was released 4 years after Citra, just as the modern hop-forward movement was hitting full stride. Together with Citra (2008) and Simcoe (2000), Mosaic completed what became the **"holy trinity" of contemporary American hops** — three varieties that dominate IPA recipes globally.

## Oil and acid composition

| Property                | Typical range            |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Alpha acids             | 11–13.5%                  |
| Beta acids              | 3.0–4.5%                  |
| Cohumulone              | 24–26% of alpha           |
| Total oils              | 1.5–2.2 mL/100g           |
| Myrcene                 | 50–60% of oil             |
| Humulene                | 10–14%                    |
| Caryophyllene           | 6–9%                      |
| Farnesene               | < 0.5%                    |
| Geraniol                | low–moderate              |
| Linalool                | moderate                  |
| **Thiol precursors**    | **Moderate** (4MMP especially, less 3MH than Citra) |

The chemistry tells the story: **Mosaic is more terpene-driven than thiol-driven.** Where Citra owes its character to thiols liberated by yeast, Mosaic's character comes from its broader **myrcene / humulene / caryophyllene** profile — compounds that survive boiling well and extract effectively in whirlpool.

This means Mosaic is **less dependent on biotransformation** than Citra or Galaxy. You get the headline Mosaic character from whirlpool and cold-side dry hop alone.

## Sensory profile

### Headline descriptors

- **Blueberry** — the distinctive Mosaic note. Specific, definable.
- **Mango / pineapple** — tropical fruit
- **Dank** — cannabis, slightly resinous
- **Pine** — softer than Simcoe but present
- **Stone fruit** — sometimes peach, apricot
- **Citrus** — present but not dominant
- **Slight vegetal / herbal** at high doses

### What you actually smell

A typical fresh Mosaic bag will:

- Open the bag → **dank punch first**, with tropical underneath
- Rub between palms → **mango / blueberry / dank** balance
- 30 seconds later → **softer fruit notes**, slight pine
- The "blueberry" character is real but variable — some lots are barely detectable, others are dominant

### Lot-to-lot variation

Mosaic is moderately variable year-to-year:

- **Some crops** lean strongly into the **blueberry / fruit** territory
- **Other crops** are more **dank / herbal / pine** — less obviously fruity
- **Storage degradation** shifts character toward the dank side (the fruit notes fade first)

The blueberry character is what most brewers seek out from Mosaic. If your lot doesn't have it strongly, the beer will be more "general dank IPA" than "blueberry-forward NEIPA."

### What "old Mosaic" smells like

Mosaic ages **better than Citra or Galaxy** because its character isn't as thiol-dependent. Aged Mosaic loses fruit character but doesn't develop the dramatic onion / garlic off-aromas as fast as aged Citra.

Distinguishing fresh from aged:

- Fresh: blueberry, mango, dank — distinct layered character
- Slightly aged: more dank, less fruit; still recognizably Mosaic
- Aged: muted, generic-hop, may have light cardboard
- Very aged: cardboard, slight cheese, but rarely the sulfur off-character of aged Citra

## Biotransformation behavior

Mosaic responds to biotransformation **less dramatically** than Citra or Galaxy. The main effects:

1. **Some 4MMP liberation** — adds tropical depth
2. **Geraniol → β-citronellol** — minor effect (Mosaic is moderate-geraniol)
3. **Some glycoside cleavage** of ester precursors

Practical implication: **Mosaic in a NEIPA doesn't *need* the active-ferm dry hop the way Citra/Galaxy do.** Cold-side-only Mosaic is largely fine. But if you have an active-ferm dry hop in the recipe, **Mosaic shines as a partner** to the thiol-rich biotransformation hops — it adds depth that Citra/Galaxy alone can lack.

A common move: **Citra in active-ferm dry hop (biotransformation-driven), Mosaic in cold-side dry hop (terpene-driven).** Plays to each variety's strength.

## Hop pairings

### Mosaic + Citra — the canonical duo

The most-used NEIPA combination. Citra brings bright tropical/citrus; Mosaic brings dank/blueberry/depth. Equal parts is the default; varying ratios shifts character:

- **More Citra (60/40):** brighter, more juicy
- **More Mosaic (40/60):** danker, more depth
- **Equal:** the modern NEIPA default

### Mosaic + Galaxy

Mosaic anchors Galaxy's punch. Galaxy provides passionfruit; Mosaic provides depth. Less "loud" than Citra+Galaxy.

### Mosaic + Strata

Both dank-leaning. Stacks rather than balances. **Polarizing combo** — produces very dank, less juicy NEIPAs.

### Mosaic + Sabro

Mosaic + coconut. Excellent. Mosaic provides the structure Sabro alone can lack.

### Mosaic + Simcoe

Returning to the genetic line — Simcoe is Mosaic's parent. Pairing them gives a classic American IPA character with modern fruit-forward expression. Excellent in **modern WCIPA**.

### Mosaic + Cascade + Centennial

Modern WCIPA territory. Mosaic provides the modern "fruit + dank" the classic Cs alone lack.

## Substitutions and adjacent hops

When Mosaic is unavailable / aged:

- **Strata** — different but in the same dank-fruit conversation
- **Idaho 7** — more "candy-tropical," less dank
- **Simcoe** — Mosaic's parent; more pine, less fruit
- **El Dorado** — more "candy-sweet," less dank-blueberry
- **Sultana** — more pineapple, similar weight in a recipe

There's no perfect Mosaic substitute. Mosaic's "blueberry + dank" character is hard to replicate.

## Recipe positioning

### Headline recipes

- **Most modern NEIPA recipes** include Mosaic somewhere — it's the most-used dank-fruit hop
- **Modern WCIPAs** increasingly include Mosaic as a "modern" element alongside Cascade/Centennial
- **Hazy DIPAs** lean heavily on Mosaic for depth

### How much Mosaic in a recipe

- **Single-hop Mosaic:** 10–14 g/L (Mosaic's intensity is moderate; you can dose generously)
- **As lead in a 4-hop combo:** 30–50% of the bill
- **As supporting:** 20–35%
- **As accent:** 10–20%

Mosaic is **forgiving on dose** — overshooting doesn't typically push it into "vegetal" the way Citra or Galaxy can. You have more dosing latitude with Mosaic than with the thiol-heavy hops.

## Cryo Mosaic

Yakima Chief's Cryo version is widely available and excellent.

- **Roughly 2× alpha and oils** per gram of T-90
- **Less polyphenol load** — useful for heavy-hopped recipes
- **Slightly more "dank" character** in some hands — perhaps because the bract material that softens Mosaic's profile is removed
- **Use half the rate** as T-90 for the same intensity

For NEIPA cold-side dry hop at high hop loads, **Cryo Mosaic is one of the best uses of Cryo Hops.** The polyphenol savings are real and the character holds up well.

## Common Mosaic mistakes

- **Confusing T-90 vs Cryo dose.** Cryo Mosaic at the T-90 rate will dominate the beer.
- **Pairing only with Citra and assuming it'll work.** Mosaic + Citra is reliable but not the only option. Try Mosaic + Galaxy or Mosaic + Strata for variety.
- **Using Mosaic in delicate styles.** Mosaic's dank character can overpower lighter beers (Pale Ales, Session IPAs). Better suited to medium-to-full hop bills.
- **Substituting freely with "another dank hop."** Strata is dank but different; Simcoe is dank but more piney. Each substitute is a different beer.
