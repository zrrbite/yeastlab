# Hop varieties

A reference for ~30 hops relevant to NEIPA brewing. Each entry covers origin, typical oil chemistry (the underlying *why* of the descriptors), what it does in the glass, and what it pairs with.

Numbers are typical ranges from Yakima Chief variety sheets and crop reports — actual values vary year to year. Always check the spec sheet on the bag you bought.

A note on reading these:

- **Alpha % / Total oils** are headline numbers but not the whole story. Two hops at 12% alpha can taste very different.
- **Myrcene** drives green/dank/citrus character. High myrcene = vivid fresh hop punch but more degradation risk.
- **Humulene** drives noble/woody/spicy character.
- **Caryophyllene** is woody/peppery.
- **Farnesene** is floral/herbal/fruity.
- **Geraniol** is rosy/citrusy and **biotransformable** by yeast (→ β-citronellol). Hops high in geraniol behave differently in active-ferm dry hopping.
- **Linalool** is floral/citrus and contributes to the "smooth" character.
- **Thiols** (4MMP, 3MH, 3MHA) are tropical / passionfruit / cat-pee at threshold. Liberated by yeast from bound precursors during fermentation. Most NEIPA "juice" character comes from thiols.
- **Hop-derived esters** (isoamyl isobutyrate, methyl geranate, 2MIB) are fruity apple/apricot. Survive boiling poorly → push them in via whirlpool, not dry hop.

Compound-richness annotations below come from Janish (2021) — see [`dry-hopping.md`](dry-hopping.md). Where a hop is noted as "rich in" a specific compound, that's a Janish/Yakima Chief data point worth designing around.

## American — Pacific Northwest

### Citra (HBC 394)

The defining hop of the modern hazy era. 

- **Origin:** Yakima Chief Ranches, released 2008
- **Alpha:** 11–14% • **Total oils:** 2.2–2.8 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 60–70%, geraniol 0.5–1%, high in thiol precursors
- **Descriptors:** Grapefruit, passionfruit, lychee, mango, gooseberry, papaya
- **Why it works:** Massive thiol precursor load → big juicy character with the right yeast. Geraniol responds well to biotransformation. Versatile across whirlpool and dry hop.
- **Pairs with:** Mosaic, Galaxy, Nelson, Simcoe, El Dorado, Idaho 7. Honestly: most things.
- **Notes:** The single most-used NEIPA hop. Ages poorly — buy fresh, store cold. Old Citra goes onion/garlic fast.

### Mosaic (HBC 369)

The other half of the foundational duo.

- **Origin:** Yakima Chief Ranches, released 2012. Daughter of Simcoe.
- **Alpha:** 11–13% • **Total oils:** 1.5–2.2 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–60%, caryophyllene 6–9%
- **Descriptors:** Blueberry, mango, dank, pine, pineapple, citrus, stone fruit
- **Why it works:** More terpene-driven than thiol-driven. Heavy whirlpool extracts the fruit; dry hopping pushes the dank/resin character. Loses some character in long active-ferm exposure.
- **Pairs with:** Citra (the canonical pairing), Galaxy, Strata, Sabro, Simcoe.
- **Notes:** Watch for "Mosaic Cryo" — same hop, much higher punch per gram.

### Simcoe (YCR 14)

The original "dank" hop.

- **Origin:** Yakima Chief Ranches, released 2000.
- **Alpha:** 12–14% • **Total oils:** 2.0–2.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 60–65%, farnesene 1–2%
- **Descriptors:** Pine, passionfruit, apricot, dank, earthy, blackcurrant
- **Why it works:** Foundational dual-purpose hop. Bridges between West Coast and NEIPA — the "danker" leg of a NEIPA hop combo.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Centennial, Amarillo, Chinook.
- **Notes:** Like Citra, ages to onion/garlic ("garlicky" is the homebrewer pejorative). Buy fresh.

### Amarillo (VGXP01)

Soft, citrus, lower-intensity but elegant.

- **Origin:** Virgil Gamache Farms, discovered as a wild seedling, released 2003.
- **Alpha:** 8–11% • **Total oils:** 1.5–2.0 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 60%, geraniol 0.5–1%
- **Descriptors:** Orange, peach, apricot, melon, mild floral
- **Why it works:** Softer than Citra — adds roundness without dominating. Whirlpool friendly. Geraniol biotransforms cleanly to β-citronellol → soft citrus.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Centennial, Cascade, El Dorado, Mosaic.
- **Notes:** Often under-loaded in modern recipes. Don't skimp — needs presence.

### Centennial

The "Super Cascade." A classic American C-hop with a NEIPA-friendly profile when used in volume.

- **Origin:** Released 1990, USDA breeding program.
- **Alpha:** 9–11% • **Total oils:** 1.5–2.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 45–55%, humulene 10–18%
- **Descriptors:** Lemon, floral, pine, citrus pith
- **Why it works:** Cleaner, brighter, more "classic American IPA" — but at NEIPA hop loads adds great citrus backbone. More humulene than most NEIPA hops → slight noble character.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Cascade, Simcoe, Amarillo, Mosaic.
- **Notes:** Stable in storage, predictable, available. A workhorse.

### Cascade

The hop that started American craft brewing.

- **Origin:** USDA, released 1972.
- **Alpha:** 4.5–7% • **Total oils:** 0.7–1.4 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 45–60%, humulene 8–15%
- **Descriptors:** Grapefruit, floral, citrus, slight spice
- **Why it works:** Lower alpha and lower total oils than modern NEIPA hops, so it's usually a *supporting* role rather than a lead. Adds classic American citrus.
- **Pairs with:** Centennial, Citra, Amarillo, Chinook.
- **Notes:** Cheap, available everywhere. A good "fill" hop in a 4-hop combo when you want classic citrus.

### Chinook

Big alpha, big resin, big personality.

- **Origin:** Released 1985, USDA breeding program.
- **Alpha:** 12–14% • **Total oils:** 1.7–2.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 35–45%, caryophyllene 9–12%
- **Descriptors:** Pine, grapefruit, resin, spice, dank
- **Why it works:** When you want serious dank/pine in a NEIPA without going full West Coast. Use sparingly in dry hop — can overwhelm.
- **Pairs with:** Simcoe, Citra, Centennial, Mosaic.
- **Notes:** Often used as a clean bittering hop too — its profile is harsher than ideal for big aroma loads. A 10–20% slot in a hop combo.

### Columbus / CTZ (Columbus / Tomahawk / Zeus)

The classic high-alpha workhorse. CTZ is shorthand for three closely-related cultivars often sold interchangeably.

- **Origin:** US, 1990s.
- **Alpha:** 14–18% • **Total oils:** 2.0–3.0 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 30–50%, caryophyllene 10–14%
- **Descriptors:** Dank, black pepper, citrus, resinous
- **Why it works:** Mainly a bittering hop in NEIPA. The high alpha lets you hit IBUs efficiently with a small charge, leaving the aroma load for whirlpool/dry hops.
- **Pairs with:** Most American hops as a base.
- **Notes:** *Don't* dry hop with this in a NEIPA — too aggressive, will fight the juice.

### Idaho 7 (IDHA 1)

Tropical, candy-like, aging gracefully into the modern hop pantheon.

- **Origin:** Jackson Farms (Idaho), released 2015.
- **Alpha:** 13–15% • **Total oils:** 2.0–2.7 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–60%, geraniol moderate
- **Descriptors:** Tropical fruit, pineapple, apricot, papaya, slight resinous edge
- **Why it works:** Reads like a "Citra cousin" but with a heavier resin backbone. Fits naturally in fruit-forward combos.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Strata, El Dorado, Galaxy.
- **Notes:** Production has scaled up — easier to find now than 2018. Holds up in the field.

### El Dorado

Candy-sweet stone-fruit and tropical character.

- **Origin:** CLS Farms (Yakima), released 2010.
- **Alpha:** 14–16% • **Total oils:** 2.5–3.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 55–65%, caryophyllene 6–8%
- **Descriptors:** Tropical fruit, watermelon, pear, candy, cherry
- **Why it works:** Adds a *sweetness perception* that complements drier base recipes. The candy-like character is distinctive.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Idaho 7, Amarillo.
- **Notes:** Lower aroma intensity than Citra/Mosaic — needs to be loaded heavy or paired with louder partners.

### Strata (X-331)

Dank, vivid, polarizing. Either a star or a problem depending on the recipe.

- **Origin:** Indie Hops / OSU breeding (Oregon), released 2018.
- **Alpha:** 11–13% • **Total oils:** 1.7–2.3 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–60%, high in thiol precursors
- **Descriptors:** Cannabis, passionfruit, strawberry, dank, citrus
- **Why it works:** The dank/cannabis character is unmistakable and very of-the-moment. Thiol-rich, so yeast strain matters.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Sabro, Galaxy.
- **Notes:** Use 20–40% of your hop bill if you want it as a feature; less as a complement. Fresh-only — degrades fast.

### Sabro (HBC 438)

Coconut. The hop that smells like a piña colada.

- **Origin:** HBC, released 2018.
- **Alpha:** 12–16% • **Total oils:** 2.5–3.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 40–55%, geraniol high (1–1.5%)
- **Descriptors:** Coconut, tangerine, cream, tropical, hint of cedar
- **Why it works:** The coconut character is wild and unique. Best as 20–30% of a combo — alone it's overwhelming.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Sultana, Strata.
- **Notes:** Crops vary a lot — some lots are subtle, others are full piña colada. Smell before you brew.

### Cashmere (USDA 06277)

Soft, lemony, melon-tinged. An under-rated supporting hop.

- **Origin:** Released 2013, Washington State University breeding program.
- **Alpha:** 7–9% • **Total oils:** 1.2–1.8 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 40–50%, farnesene 6–10%
- **Descriptors:** Melon, lemon, lime, soft tropical
- **Why it works:** Adds soft melon/lemon notes that smooth out spikier hops. Lower oil load → not a lead but a great supporter.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Galaxy, Amarillo, Centennial.
- **Notes:** Often paired with louder hops to round out a combo. 15–25% slot.

### Equinox / Ekuanot (HBC 366)

A hop with two names. Same cultivar, marketing rebrand. Tropical with a citrus edge.

- **Origin:** HBC, released 2014.
- **Alpha:** 13–16% • **Total oils:** 2.0–2.8 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 35–45%
- **Descriptors:** Lime, papaya, lemongrass, herbal, slight pepper
- **Why it works:** Brings a "lime + tropical" leaning that's distinctive — not as juicy as Citra, more savory.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Galaxy, Amarillo.
- **Notes:** Cryo Ekuanot is dramatically more intense — start lower than you think.

### Sultana (formerly Denali)

Pineapple-citrus, cleaner profile, shifting reputation as it spreads.

- **Origin:** Yakima Chief / CLS Farms, released 2014.
- **Alpha:** 14–17% • **Total oils:** 2.5–3.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–60%, farnesene moderate
- **Descriptors:** Pineapple, citrus, pine, slight cedar
- **Why it works:** Tropical without the dank that some NEIPA hops bring. A "clean pineapple" lever.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Sabro, Idaho 7.
- **Notes:** Older bags labeled "Denali" — same hop. Production is scaling.

### Loral (HBC 291)

Floral, peppery, wine-like — different from typical NEIPA tropical.

- **Origin:** HBC, released 2016. Daughter of Glacier × Nugget.
- **Alpha:** 10–12% • **Total oils:** 2.0–2.5 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Caryophyllene 9–12%, humulene 14–18% (high)
- **Descriptors:** Floral, citrus, peppery, dark fruit, herbal
- **Why it works:** The high humulene gives it a noble-hop quality unusual for an American hop. Adds elegance/complexity in small doses.
- **Pairs with:** Citra (lifts the floral side), Mosaic.
- **Notes:** A "complexity hop" — 10–20% of a combo for nuance.

### HBC 472 (now "Krush")

Newer experimental, melon-citrus, gaining traction.

- **Origin:** HBC, experimental in late 2010s.
- **Alpha:** 11–14% • **Total oils:** 2.0–2.5 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Honeydew melon, sweet citrus, soft tropical
- **Why it works:** Unusual melon character — more cantaloupe than typical hop melon notes. Distinctive.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Cashmere, Amarillo.
- **Notes:** Availability varies. Watch for the Krush trade name.

## Australian

### Galaxy

Australia's signature hop. The cleanest, most intense passionfruit in the catalog.

- **Origin:** Hop Products Australia, released 2009.
- **Alpha:** 13–16% • **Total oils:** 2.5–3.5 mL/100g (often higher)
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 35–45%, caryophyllene 4–6%, very high thiol precursors
- **Descriptors:** Passionfruit, peach, citrus, tropical, slight herbal
- **Why it works:** Massive thiol potential → enormous "juicy" character with biotransformation-active yeast. The single most exciting NEIPA hop alongside Citra.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Mosaic, Nelson Sauvin, Vic Secret, Riwaka.
- **Notes:** Expensive and supply is volatile. Galaxy Cryo is exceptional. Don't oxidize — Galaxy goes from passionfruit to onion fast.

### Vic Secret

Cleaner, piney-tropical Galaxy alternative.

- **Origin:** Hop Products Australia, released 2013.
- **Alpha:** 14–17% • **Total oils:** 2.0–2.6 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–60%
- **Descriptors:** Pine, pineapple, tropical, herbal
- **Why it works:** Pairs well with louder fruit-forward hops, adds backbone without dominating.
- **Pairs with:** Galaxy, Citra, Mosaic, Nelson.
- **Notes:** Less supply-constrained than Galaxy, often a good substitute.

### Enigma

Wine-like, raspberry, distinctive.

- **Origin:** Hop Products Australia, released 2015.
- **Alpha:** 14–18% • **Total oils:** 2.0–3.0 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–55%
- **Descriptors:** Raspberry, red currant, pinot gris, white wine, melon
- **Why it works:** Bringing wine/grape/berry notes that almost no other hop does. Distinct in a combo.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Galaxy, Nelson Sauvin, Riwaka.
- **Notes:** Can be polarizing — taste before you commit a big batch.

### Eclipse

Mandarin-orange forward, dry-finishing.

- **Origin:** Hop Products Australia, released 2018.
- **Alpha:** 14–18% • **Total oils:** 1.5–2.5 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Mandarin, orange, pine, slight pineapple
- **Why it works:** Adds a citrus rind / mandarin character. Newer, less proven, but nice.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Galaxy, Idaho 7.
- **Notes:** Limited availability outside Australia.

## New Zealand

### Nelson Sauvin

The hop named after Sauvignon Blanc. Tastes like the wine.

- **Origin:** New Zealand Hops Ltd, released 2000.
- **Alpha:** 12–13% • **Total oils:** 1.0–1.4 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 25–35%, very high in thiol precursors (3MH)
- **Descriptors:** White grape, gooseberry, passionfruit, fresh-cut grass
- **Why it works:** The 3MH thiol level is exceptional — yeast liberates it during fermentation for that signature "Sauv blanc" character. Almost no other hop does this.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Galaxy, Riwaka, Motueka.
- **Notes:** Expensive, limited supply. Worth it. Use with a thiol-active yeast (London III, Hornindal kveik) for max effect.

### Riwaka

Bright, intense grapefruit. The "loud" New Zealand hop.

- **Origin:** New Zealand Hops Ltd, released 1997.
- **Alpha:** 4.5–6.5% • **Total oils:** 0.7–1.2 mL/100g
- **Notable oils:** Myrcene 50–70%
- **Descriptors:** Grapefruit, citrus zest, passionfruit, vivid
- **Why it works:** Low alpha but massive aromatic punch — flavor density is high. Best as whirlpool / dry hop, not bittering.
- **Pairs with:** Nelson Sauvin (the canonical pair), Citra, Motueka.
- **Notes:** Frequently in short supply. Riwaka BBC (lupulin powder) is concentrated and worth seeking.

### Motueka

Lime, citrus, soft tropical.

- **Origin:** New Zealand Hops Ltd, released 2000.
- **Alpha:** 6.5–8.5% • **Total oils:** 0.8–1.4 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Lime, lemon, mojito, mint
- **Why it works:** The "lime" hop. Underused. Adds zest and brightness without dominating.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Nelson Sauvin, Riwaka, Cashmere.
- **Notes:** Lower oil load — load it heavy or use as a complement.

### Wakatu

Soft, lime-and-floral, descended from Hallertau.

- **Origin:** New Zealand Hops Ltd.
- **Alpha:** 6.5–8.5% • **Total oils:** 0.8–1.3 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Lime zest, floral, hint of lemongrass
- **Why it works:** Cleaner, more refined version of Motueka territory. Good in soft NEIPAs that don't want to be loud.
- **Pairs with:** Motueka, Wai-iti, Nelson, Riwaka.
- **Notes:** Limited supply outside NZ.

### Pacific Jade

Citrus and pepper, dual-purpose.

- **Origin:** New Zealand Hops Ltd.
- **Alpha:** 12–15% • **Total oils:** 1.2–1.8 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Citrus, fresh pepper, slight herbal
- **Why it works:** Bittering or whirlpool option with a soft citrus edge — cleaner than CTZ for high-alpha bittering.
- **Pairs with:** Most NZ hops; bittering for any combo.
- **Notes:** Solid, predictable, available.

## German / European

### Hallertau Blanc

Wine-like German aroma hop.

- **Origin:** Hüll, Germany, released 2012.
- **Alpha:** 9–12% • **Total oils:** 1.0–1.5 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** White wine, gooseberry, lemongrass, passionfruit
- **Why it works:** German engineering of a Sauvignon-Blanc-like character. Smoother than Nelson, less intense.
- **Pairs with:** Nelson Sauvin, Citra, Mandarina Bavaria.
- **Notes:** A nice complement when you want a wine character without going full Nelson.

### Mandarina Bavaria

Citrus and tangerine — a more "European" take on American citrus hops.

- **Origin:** Hüll, Germany, released 2012.
- **Alpha:** 7–10% • **Total oils:** 1.5–2.3 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Tangerine, mandarin orange, citrus
- **Why it works:** Cleaner, less dank than American citrus hops. Pairs with German pilsner-base recipes especially well.
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Amarillo, Hallertau Blanc.
- **Notes:** Good for "cleaner" / less-dank NEIPAs.

### Huell Melon

Honeydew melon character. Distinctive.

- **Origin:** Hüll, Germany, released 2012.
- **Alpha:** 6.5–8% • **Total oils:** 1.4–2.0 mL/100g
- **Descriptors:** Honeydew melon, strawberry, apricot
- **Why it works:** The melon character is unusual — pairs with hops that don't already lean melon (Cashmere, HBC 472 already cover that lane).
- **Pairs with:** Citra, Galaxy, Mandarina Bavaria.
- **Notes:** Lower alpha — supporting role only.

## Quick reference: building a 4-hop NEIPA combo

Pick one from each role:

| Role                      | Candidates                                                  |
|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Citrus / juice anchor** | Citra, Galaxy, Nelson Sauvin                                |
| **Dank / depth**          | Mosaic, Simcoe, Strata                                      |
| **Tropical / sweet**      | Sabro, Idaho 7, El Dorado, Mandarina Bavaria                |
| **Bright / supporting**   | Amarillo, Cashmere, Riwaka, Motueka, Centennial             |

Examples:

- **Citra + Mosaic + Galaxy + Amarillo** — the classic juicy quartet
- **Citra + Strata + Sabro + Riwaka** — dank-tropical-coconut-grapefruit, polarizing
- **Galaxy + Nelson Sauvin + Riwaka + Motueka** — Antipodean focus, wine-and-citrus
- **Citra + Mosaic + Idaho 7 + Cashmere** — soft tropical, no surprises
- **Mosaic + Simcoe + Chinook + Centennial** — the West Coast / NEIPA bridge

For exploring varieties one at a time, see [`../recipes/single-hop-experiments.md`](../recipes/single-hop-experiments.md).
