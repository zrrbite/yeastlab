# Stout

The dark counterpoint to the IPA family. **Roast malts, full body, low hop bitterness, and a flavor profile centered on coffee, chocolate, and dark fruit.** Stouts split into many sub-styles — Irish dry stout, oatmeal stout, milk stout, sweet stout, imperial stout, pastry stout — each emphasizing different elements of the same dark-grain framework.

This file is a compact overview of the stout family. Each sub-style could justify its own deep dive.

## The stout family

| Sub-style          | OG          | ABV        | Defining trait                          |
|--------------------|-------------|------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Irish dry stout** | 1.036–1.045 | 3.8–5.0%  | Light body, dry, slight roast bitterness (Guinness archetype) |
| **Sweet stout / milk stout** | 1.044–1.060 | 4.0–6.0% | Lactose for unfermentable sweetness   |
| **Oatmeal stout**  | 1.048–1.065 | 4.2–5.9%  | Flaked oats for silky body              |
| **American stout** | 1.050–1.075 | 5.0–7.0%  | Hop-forward stout (citrusy + roasty)    |
| **Foreign / export stout** | 1.056–1.075 | 6.3–8.0% | Stronger Irish-style; more roast    |
| **Imperial stout** | 1.075–1.115 | 8.0–12.0% | Big, rich, intense                      |
| **Pastry stout**   | 1.080+      | 9.0+%     | Imperial + adjuncts (chocolate, vanilla, etc.) |

## Defining traits across the family

- **Roasted malts** — roasted barley, chocolate malt, black patent malt — provide color and the "stout" character
- **Full body** — even dry stouts feel weighted compared to lagers
- **Low-to-moderate hop bitterness** — 25–60 IBU; bitterness comes from roast more than hops
- **Brown to opaque-black color** — SRM 30–60+
- **Flavor:** roast, coffee, chocolate, dark fruit, sometimes lactose sweetness

## Grain bill — the dark layers

A stout grain bill is **layered** — base malt + roasted grains in different proportions:

| Grain                        | Role                                                   |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Pale malt (2-row, MO)        | Base, 70–85%                                          |
| Roasted barley (un-malted)   | Coffee bitterness, dark color, dry finish (Irish)      |
| Chocolate malt               | Chocolate-coffee character, color                      |
| Black patent malt            | Sharp roast, deep color                                |
| Crystal 60–120L              | Caramel sweetness, body                                |
| Flaked oats                  | Silkiness, body                                       |
| Flaked barley                | Full body, head retention (classic Guinness inclusion) |
| Lactose (added in boil)      | Unfermentable sweetness (sweet/milk stouts only)      |

## Recipe template — Irish dry stout

For 23 L at OG 1.042:

| Ingredient                  | Amount  | Notes                                          |
|-----------------------------|---------|------------------------------------------------|
| Pale malt (Maris Otter)     | 3500 g  | Base                                          |
| Roasted barley              | 350 g   | The "Irish stout" character                   |
| Flaked barley               | 250 g   | Body and head retention (classic)             |
| Hops — Magnum 60 min        | 25 g    | ~32 IBU                                        |
| Hops — Fuggles 5 min        | 15 g    | Light noble character                          |
| Yeast: WLP004 Irish Ale / Wyeast 1084 / Lallemand Nottingham | 11 g dry | English-style ale yeast |

**Mash:** 67 °C × 60 min
**Boil:** 60 min
**Ferment:** 18–20 °C × 7 days
**Cold-condition:** 1 week
**Carbonate:** 2.0–2.4 vol (lower than ales, classic stout level)

## Recipe template — oatmeal stout

For 23 L at OG 1.055:

| Ingredient                  | Amount  | Notes                                          |
|-----------------------------|---------|------------------------------------------------|
| Pale malt                   | 4000 g  | Base                                          |
| Flaked oats                 | 700 g   | The "oatmeal" character — silky body          |
| Crystal 60L                 | 250 g   | Caramel sweetness                             |
| Chocolate malt              | 250 g   | Chocolate, color                              |
| Black patent malt           | 100 g   | Color depth, slight roast bite                |
| Hops — Magnum 60 min        | 22 g    | ~30 IBU                                        |
| Hops — East Kent Goldings 10 min | 25 g | Light hop character                          |
| Yeast: WLP002 English Ale / Lallemand Nottingham | 11 g dry | English-style |

**Mash:** 67 °C × 60 min (warm enough for body, oats need this)
**Boil:** 60 min
**Ferment:** 18–20 °C × 7–10 days
**Carbonate:** 2.2–2.4 vol

## Recipe template — imperial stout (the project)

For 23 L at OG 1.090, ~10% ABV:

| Ingredient                  | Amount  |
|-----------------------------|---------|
| Pale malt (Maris Otter)     | 6800 g  |
| Crystal 80–120L             | 600 g   |
| Chocolate malt              | 600 g   |
| Roasted barley              | 350 g   |
| Black patent malt           | 200 g   |
| Flaked oats                 | 500 g   |
| Hops — Magnum 90 min        | 50 g    |
| Hops — Cascade 30 min       | 30 g    |
| Hops — East Kent Goldings 10 min | 30 g |
| Yeast: WLP013 / Wyeast 1338 / Lallemand Windsor | 11+11 g dry (double pitch for high gravity) |

**Mash:** 65 °C × 75 min (lower for fermentability of high-gravity wort)
**Boil:** 90 min (concentrate wort, develop more melanoidins)
**Ferment:** 19–20 °C, free-rise to 22 °C late, 14+ days
**Diacetyl rest:** 22 °C × 3 days
**Cold-condition:** 4+ weeks
**Carbonate:** 2.0–2.5 vol

## Process notes

### Mash

- **65–67 °C** for most stouts — slightly higher than IPA (favors body)
- Single-infusion fine; no decoction needed

### Boil

- 60–90 min depending on style; longer for higher-gravity stouts

### Hops

- Stouts use **less hop than IPAs**. Bittering at 60 min is the headline; aroma additions are subtle.
- Most stouts use **English noble hops** (East Kent Goldings, Fuggles) or American C-hops (Cascade, Centennial)
- **Don't over-hop a stout** — the roast is doing a lot of work; too much hop creates clash

### Ferment

- **Ale yeast at 18–22 °C**
- **English ale yeasts** suit Irish/oatmeal/sweet stouts
- **Clean American (US-05)** suits American/imperial stouts
- For imperial stouts: bigger pitch, longer fermentation, free-rise

### Cold-condition

- **2–4 weeks for everything bigger than dry stout**
- Imperial stouts can age **months or years** — they actually improve

## What "good stout" tastes like

### Irish dry stout

- **Roasted character forward** — dry, slightly bitter coffee
- **Light body for the color** — surprising lightness
- **Dry finish** — no sweetness
- **Subtle hop bitterness** integrating with roast

### Oatmeal stout

- **Silky, smooth mouthfeel** (the oats)
- **Coffee + chocolate** flavor center
- **Gentle sweetness** — not cloying
- **Medium body**

### Imperial stout

- **Rich, dense, layered** — intense flavor
- **Coffee, dark chocolate, dark fruit** (raisin, prune, fig)
- **Slight alcohol warmth** but not solvent
- **Full body, almost viscous**
- **Long finish**

## Variations

### American stout

Bump the hops — modern American C-hops (Cascade, Centennial, Citra) with both whirlpool and dry-hop additions. Maintains the stout grain bill but adds 30+ IBU of hop aroma. Polarizing but excellent.

### Sweet / milk stout

Add **300–500 g of lactose** in the last 10 min of boil. Lactose doesn't ferment → residual sweetness. Pair with coffee or chocolate flavorings.

### Coffee stout

Add **cold-brew coffee concentrate** (made from 100 g coffee + 1 L water steeped overnight) at packaging. **Don't add coffee to the boil** — bitterness becomes harsh. Cold-brew addition gives clean coffee character.

### Chocolate stout

Add **cocoa nibs** (50–100 g) to secondary fermentation for 2 weeks. Or add cacao powder + sugar mix at packaging.

### Vanilla stout

Add **1–2 vanilla beans** (split lengthwise) to secondary for 1–2 weeks. Or vanilla extract at packaging.

### Pastry stout

The modern phenomenon — imperial stout + sugar + adjuncts (vanilla, cinnamon, lactose, marshmallow, "graham cracker," etc.). Polarizing but dramatic.

### Barrel-aged imperial stout

Imperial stout aged in **bourbon, rum, or wine barrels** for 6–12 months. Picks up vanilla, oak, alcohol heat, fruit notes. Project-level brewing; significant infrastructure (barrel handling, time, space).

## Common stout mistakes

- **Too much roasted barley.** Aggressive bitter / acrid character. 5–7% of grain bill is plenty.
- **Underbittered.** Stout still needs hops; minimum 25 IBU.
- **Wrong yeast.** Don't use a fruity NEIPA yeast in a stout — clashes with the roast.
- **Overcarbonated.** Stouts traditionally lower carb (2.0–2.4 vol); high carb fights the body.
- **Cold-storing too cold.** Imperial stouts can lose character at near-freezing storage. 4–8 °C is fine.

## Why brew stouts

Pros:

- **Forgiving.** Roast malts cover small mistakes.
- **Year-round.** Stout in summer is an underrated combination.
- **Imperial stouts age.** Brew once, drink for years.
- **Adjuncts are fun.** Coffee / vanilla / chocolate / barrel each create something different.
- **Crowd-pleasing.** Even non-stout drinkers respect a good stout.

Cons:

- **Long timelines for imperials.** Less flexibility than ales.
- **Harder to differentiate** when brewing many stouts.
- **Cleanup.** Roast malts stain everything.
