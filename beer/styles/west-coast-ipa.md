# West Coast IPA

The original American IPA. Clear, bitter, dry, **piney-citrusy** — built around the assertive American C-hops (Cascade, Centennial, Chinook, Columbus). Born in California in the late 1980s and 1990s (Sierra Nevada Pale, Stone IPA, Russian River Pliny the Elder), it defined the **American craft beer revolution** for two decades before NEIPAs took over the spotlight.

In 2025–2026 the WCIPA is having a renaissance — drinkers who burned out on opaque haze bombs are returning to the clarity and finish of a well-made WCIPA. This is the **opposite-day version of NEIPA** in almost every dimension, which makes it the perfect counterpart to brew alongside.

## NEIPA vs WCIPA — quick orientation

| Dimension              | NEIPA                          | West Coast IPA                          |
|------------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Appearance             | Hazy, opaque, glowing pale orange | Brilliantly clear, deep gold to amber  |
| Mouthfeel              | Soft, pillowy, full            | Crisp, dry, lean                        |
| Bitterness             | Low–medium perceived           | Medium–high, defining                   |
| Finish                 | Round, smooth                  | Dry, lingering bitterness               |
| Hop character          | Tropical, juicy, soft          | Pine, citrus rind, dank, resinous       |
| Grain bill             | 30%+ wheat/oats                | 100% pale malt + small crystal          |
| Water                  | Cl-forward (Cl:SO₄ ~2:1)       | SO₄-forward (Cl:SO₄ ~1:3)              |
| Yeast                  | Mid-flocculation, fruity esters | Highly flocculant, clean (Chico)       |
| Dry hop                | 2-stage with biotransformation | Often single cold-side, smaller         |

These differences aren't decorative — they reinforce each other. A West Coast yeast won't make a NEIPA. A chloride-heavy water won't make a West Coast.

## Defining traits

- **Brilliant clarity.** Highly flocculant yeast + lower-protein grain bill + cold conditioning → a beer you can read a newspaper through. Haze is a defect, not a style choice.
- **Dry, crisp finish.** Sulfate-forward water sharpens the hops; high attenuation (yeast finishes very dry) leaves no residual sweetness to soften the bitterness.
- **Assertive bitterness.** IBUs commonly **50–80**, perceived as such. The bitterness is the headline.
- **Pine, citrus rind, resin.** The flavor profile of the American C-hops in their natural element. Not "juicy" — *piney*. Grapefruit pith, cat-piss (in a good way), pine sap, dank weed.
- **Pale-to-amber color.** Pilsner-pale to deep gold; some classic recipes pushed amber (e.g., Stone IPA's slight crystal malt). NEIPAs are pale; modern WCIPAs split between pale-and-clean and slightly-deeper-and-traditional.
- **Medium body.** Not as soft as NEIPA, not as thin as a pilsner. Just enough body to support the bitterness.
- **Long shelf life relative to NEIPA.** A WCIPA can age 6–12 weeks gracefully. A NEIPA peaks at 2–4. Hop character fades but doesn't crash; bitterness even softens slightly with time.

## Vital stats

| Parameter            | Typical range             |
|----------------------|----------------------------|
| OG                   | 1.060–1.072               |
| FG                   | 1.008–1.014 (very dry)    |
| ABV                  | 6.0–7.5% (DIPA: 8–10%)    |
| IBU (calculated)     | 50–80                     |
| IBU (perceived)      | High                      |
| Color (SRM)          | 5–10 (pale gold to deep gold/amber) |
| Mash pH              | 5.2–5.4                   |
| Final beer pH        | 4.2–4.4                   |

## Grain bill — pale and clean

The polar opposite of NEIPA. The grain's job is to **stay out of the way** and let the hops dominate.

| Grain                     | %       | Why                                                |
|---------------------------|---------|----------------------------------------------------|
| 2-row pale malt           | 90–100% | Base. Clean, neutral, supports without sweetness.  |
| Vienna or Munich          | 0–8%    | Optional — adds slight malt complexity, depth      |
| Light crystal (15–40L)    | 0–5%    | Optional — slight body, light caramel. Use sparingly |
| Carapils / dextrin        | 0–3%    | Optional — body without color or sweetness         |

**Avoid:**

- **Wheat / oats** — defeats the clarity goal
- **Dark crystal (60L+)** — too sweet, fights the bitterness
- **Roast malts** — wrong style entirely

The classic Sierra Nevada Pale Ale (the WCIPA progenitor) is pretty much **100% pale 2-row** with a tiny touch of crystal. Plenty of modern WCIPAs do the same.

## Hops — pine, citrus, dank

A few categorical differences from NEIPA hop selection:

- **You want pine, citrus rind, dank, herbal.** Not tropical fruit / juicy / soft.
- **You can use bittering-heavy hops** (Chinook, Columbus, Centennial) for both bittering AND aroma — they have plenty of both.
- **Cryo Hops® are useful but less critical** — polyphenol astringency is less of a concern than in massively-dry-hopped NEIPA.
- **Dry hop is usually smaller** — total hopping rate often 6–10 g/L vs NEIPA's 12–18 g/L.

### Classic WCIPA hop combinations

- **Cascade + Centennial + Columbus** — the original Sierra Nevada / Stone IPA territory. Pine, grapefruit, herbal.
- **Simcoe + Centennial + Chinook** — pine-forward, dank, classic.
- **Citra + Simcoe + Centennial** — modern WCIPA leaning slightly more citrusy.
- **Mosaic + Simcoe + Cascade** — dank/blueberry/pine; bridges to modern hop characters.
- **Chinook + Cascade + Amarillo** — pine + grapefruit + soft orange.

For a WCIPA, **lean into dank/pine/resin hops** — these don't get the prominence they deserve in NEIPA recipes. Chinook, Simcoe, Columbus shine in WCIPA.

## Water — sulfate-forward

The defining water move. **Burton-on-Trent style** water (high sulfate, hard) is where this style was inspired in spirit, though American brewers tuned to their own profile.

| Ion                | Target (ppm)              |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Calcium (Ca²⁺)     | 100–150                   |
| Magnesium (Mg²⁺)   | 5–15                      |
| Sodium (Na⁺)       | 5–20 (low)                |
| Chloride (Cl⁻)     | 50–80 (low)               |
| Sulfate (SO₄²⁻)    | **200–350** (the headline) |
| **Cl : SO₄ ratio** | **~1 : 3 to 1 : 4**       |

The sulfate sharpens the hop bitterness — makes it feel **drier, more linear, more "piney."** Without it, even a heavily-hopped beer feels round and soft (which is exactly what NEIPA wants and exactly what WCIPA doesn't).

For brewers used to NEIPA water: **your CaCl₂ → CaSO₄ swap is the entire mental model.** The salts you load up for soft NEIPA water you back off for WCIPA; the sulfate (gypsum) you avoid in NEIPA you push hard in WCIPA.

See [`../water.md`](../water.md) for a NEIPA water reference; the WCIPA equivalent inverts the chloride/sulfate balance.

## Yeast — clean and flocculant

NEIPA wants ester-throwing, mid-flocculant yeast. WCIPA wants the opposite:

- **Highly flocculant** → drops bright cleanly, supports clarity
- **Clean fermentation profile** → minimal esters, no banana, no clove, no peach. The hops are the headline; yeast should disappear.
- **High attenuation** → drier finish, supports the crisp profile
- **Tolerates aggressive bittering** without compounding harshness

### Top picks

- **WLP001 California Ale / Wyeast 1056 American Ale / Safale US-05** ("Chico") — the Sierra Nevada strain. The default. Clean, neutral, moderately flocculant, 75–82% attenuation. Available everywhere. **Workhorse for WCIPA.**
- **Imperial A07 Flagship** — close cousin to Chico, slightly cleaner ferment in some hands.
- **Lallemand BRY-97** — dry yeast, slightly cleaner than US-05, slower start, very high attenuation.
- **WLP051 California Ale V** — slightly more flocculant than 001, similar character.
- **WLP007 Dry English Ale** — for an English-leaning WCIPA. More flocculant, drier finish, slight English character.

**Avoid:**

- London Ale III, Vermont Ale, Verdant IPA — these are NEIPA strains. Wrong character, wrong flocculation.
- Belgian / Saison strains — too estery, wrong style.
- Kveik — too tropical/fruity for WCIPA, fights the pine character.

See [`../yeast-strains.md`](../yeast-strains.md) for the broader strain reference.

## Process — the WCIPA pipeline

### 1. Mash low for fermentability

- **Mash temp: 64–66 °C / 147–151 °F** — lower than NEIPA (which mashes 67 °C). Lower mash temp favors **β-amylase**, producing more fermentable sugars → drier finish.
- 60 min mash.

### 2. Boil with substantial bittering

- **60 min boil** with bittering hop addition.
- WCIPA bittering charge is **substantial** — 30–50 IBUs from the boil itself, often using high-alpha hops (Magnum, Warrior, Columbus, Chinook).
- Some recipes do FWH (first-wort hopping) — adding hops at the start of mash → smoother, more refined bitterness.

### 3. Whirlpool

- **At 80–90 °C / 175–195 °F** — hotter than NEIPA's 82 °C target.
- Some WCIPA recipes whirlpool **right at flameout (95+ °C)** — closer to a kettle hop. More isomerization, more bitterness — fits the style.
- Whirlpool dose: 3–6 g/L (smaller than NEIPA).

### 4. Cool, oxygenate, pitch Chico

- Chill to **18 °C / 64 °F**.
- Oxygenate well (8–12 ppm DO).
- Pitch Chico at 18 °C.

### 5. Ferment clean and dry

- **Days 1–2:** 18–19 °C
- **Days 3–6:** Free-rise to 20–22 °C, normal fermentation
- **Day 6–8:** FG approached. WCIPA targets very dry — 1.008–1.012 typical.
- **No active-ferm dry hop in classic WCIPA.** Biotransformation is a NEIPA move; WCIPA is about clean hop expression, not yeast-modified.

### 6. Cold-side dry hop (single charge)

- After FG, drop a single dry hop at **fermentation temp or slightly cool (15–18 °C)** for **3–5 days**.
- Total dry hop: **3–6 g/L** — much less than NEIPA.
- Some classic WCIPAs barely dry hop at all (Sierra Nevada Pale Ale traditionally didn't); the modern style does.

### 7. Cold-condition for clarity

- Crash to **0–2 °C** for **5–10 days** before packaging.
- This is the clarity step. Yeast and hop debris drop out. **Never skip this for a WCIPA** — clarity is part of the style.
- Optionally: gelatin fining or biofine for extra clarity. Some purists object; many great WCIPAs use it.

### 8. Carbonate higher than NEIPA

- **2.5–2.8 vol CO₂** — slightly higher carbonation than NEIPA. Lifts the dry, crisp character.

### 9. Closed transfer

- Same low-DO discipline as NEIPA. WCIPAs oxidize too — they just hide it less than NEIPA (cardboard is more obvious in a clear, bitter beer than in a hazy juicy one). See [`../process/packaging.md`](../process/packaging.md).

## What "good" looks and tastes like

**Visual:** **Brilliantly clear**, deep gold to amber. Holds a tight white head with good lacing down the glass.

**Aroma:** **Pine sap, grapefruit pith, dank cannabis, citrus rind, slight resin, sometimes mint or herbal notes.** Not tropical fruit-forward (that's NEIPA). Should be aromatic but more focused/linear than NEIPA's broad fruit-bomb.

**Flavor:** **Bitter, then bitter, then a touch of malt, then bitter again.** Pine and citrus rind dominate. Should have **forward bitterness** that's clean and well-integrated, not harsh or vegetal.

**Mouthfeel:** **Crisp, dry, lean.** Carbonation prickles. Light-medium body. Not thin, but definitely not pillowy.

**Finish:** **Bone dry. Lingering bitterness.** The bitterness should keep coming back as you drink.

## Common failure modes

| Symptom                              | Likely cause                                                  |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hazy / cloudy                        | Insufficient cold conditioning, wrong yeast, too much wheat/protein in grain |
| Sweet / cloying finish               | Mash temp too high, attenuation too low, yeast didn't finish, or accidentally added crystal |
| Soft / round / not piney enough      | Sulfate too low — needs more gypsum                          |
| Harsh / unpleasant bitterness        | Old hops, old isomerized iso-alpha, water alkalinity too high, mash pH too high |
| Tropical / juicy character           | NEIPA hops or NEIPA yeast — wrong recipe                      |
| Diacetyl                             | Yeast quit too soon — extend warm rest                       |
| Astringent                           | Over-extracted bittering, water too alkaline, polyphenol crash |
| Cardboard / sherry                   | Oxidation. Less hidden in WCIPA than NEIPA — fix DO discipline |

## The modern WCIPA renaissance

In 2024–2026 the West Coast IPA is firmly back as a serious style category. Drivers:

- **NEIPA palate fatigue.** Drinkers grew tired of opaque haze bombs that all tasted similar.
- **Better technique.** Modern WCIPAs benefit from low-DO packaging, better hop varieties (mixing classic Cs with Citra/Mosaic/Simcoe for a **modern WCIPA** style), and more refined water chemistry.
- **Flocculant haze frustration.** Drinkers can see what they're drinking; they like it.
- **Drinkability.** WCIPAs are easier to drink multiple of than heavy NEIPAs.

The **modern WCIPA** category bridges traditional WCIPA technique with **newer hops** — a beer that's still clear and dry but uses Citra alongside Centennial. Russian River's Pliny the Elder was the model; many modern WCIPAs riff on that template.

## Sub-styles to know

- **Classic WCIPA** — Sierra Nevada / Stone / Russian River archetype. Pine + grapefruit, traditional Cs, brilliantly clear.
- **Modern WCIPA** — same technique, **modern hops** (Citra, Mosaic, Galaxy alongside Centennial/Simcoe). Crisper than NEIPA, brighter than classic.
- **Cold IPA** — see [`comparison.md`](comparison.md). Sometimes treated as a sub-style of WCIPA, sometimes its own thing. Cold-fermented (lager yeast, cold ferm), clear, dry, hop-forward.
- **West Coast DIPA** — bigger version. 8–10% ABV, more hop, more body. See [`../recipes/west-coast-dipa.md`](../recipes/west-coast-dipa.md).
- **Imperial IPA** — same territory as DIPA but sometimes more malt-forward.
- **Session IPA** — 4–5% ABV, similar profile but lighter. Tricky to pull off without the body and bitterness clashing.
- **Pale Ale** — closely related but lower IBU (30–45), lower ABV (5–6%). Sierra Nevada Pale Ale is the historical WCIPA prototype.
