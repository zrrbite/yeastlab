# Cold IPA

The most interesting style innovation of the post-NEIPA era. **A clear, dry, hop-forward beer brewed with lager yeast at lager temperatures, then dry-hopped like an IPA.** Not a pilsner, not a WCIPA — its own thing, sometimes called "the lager brewer's IPA" or "an IPL with attitude."

Pioneered by **Wayfinder Brewing** (Portland, OR) around 2018, with Kevin Davey publishing the recipe approach. The style spread quickly because it solved a problem: NEIPAs were hop-saturated but heavy; WCIPAs were dry but bitter. Cold IPA delivers **lager-like crispness with IPA-level hop expression**.

## What's actually different

Compared to the IPA family:

- **Lager yeast** at **lager temperatures** (12–15 °C / 54–59 °F) — produces a dramatically cleaner fermentation profile than ale yeast
- **Pilsner malt base + adjuncts (rice or corn)** — drier and lighter than typical IPA grain bills
- **Soft, sulfate-leaning water** — supports hop expression but with less mineral edge than WCIPA
- **Modern hop bill** — Citra, Mosaic, Simcoe, Nelson, Strata — the same hops as NEIPA, used differently
- **Cold conditioning weeks (lager standards)** — produces brilliant clarity
- **Higher carbonation than ales** — 2.7–3.0 vol typical

The result: you get the **clean, lifted, brilliant** fermentation profile of a lager combined with **the assertive hop character of an IPA**. The hops have nowhere to hide; the beer has nowhere to hide.

## Vital stats

| Parameter        | Target              |
|------------------|---------------------|
| OG               | 1.060–1.072        |
| FG               | 1.008–1.014 (very dry, lager-attenuation) |
| ABV              | 6.5–7.5%           |
| IBU (calculated) | 50–70              |
| Color (SRM)      | 3–5 (very pale)    |
| Mash pH          | 5.3                |
| Final beer pH    | 4.2–4.4            |
| Carbonation      | 2.7–3.0 vol CO₂    |

## Defining traits

- **Brilliantly clear, very pale.** Lighter and cleaner than WCIPA. Looks more like a pilsner with attitude than an IPA.
- **Lager-clean fermentation profile.** No ester character. The hops are unmasked.
- **Dry, crisp finish.** Adjuncts and lager attenuation produce a very dry beer.
- **Hop-forward but elegant.** The hops express themselves clearly because there's no yeast character or grain heaviness to compete.
- **Higher carbonation.** 2.7–3.0 vol is part of the style — produces a "spritzy" mouthfeel.
- **Lighter body.** Despite 7% ABV, mouthfeel can feel like a 5% beer.

## NEIPA vs WCIPA vs Cold IPA — quick

| Dimension              | NEIPA              | Modern WCIPA        | Cold IPA                    |
|------------------------|--------------------|--------------------|------------------------------|
| Yeast                  | Ale (London III)   | Ale (Chico)        | **Lager (W34/70 etc.)**      |
| Ferm temp              | 18–22 °C           | 18–22 °C           | **12–15 °C (cold!)**         |
| Adjuncts               | Wheat / oats       | Pale + light crystal | **Rice / corn (often)**     |
| Color                  | Pale-orange hazy   | Pale-gold clear    | **Very pale, very clear**    |
| Mouthfeel              | Soft, full         | Crisp, lean        | **Crisp, very lean**         |
| Cold conditioning      | Brief              | 1 week             | **2–4 weeks (lager spec)**   |
| Carbonation            | 2.4–2.6 vol        | 2.6–2.8 vol        | **2.7–3.0 vol**              |
| Brew time start-finish | ~3 weeks           | ~4 weeks           | **5–7 weeks**                |

## Grain bill — pale and dry

| Grain                          | %       | Why                                                |
|--------------------------------|---------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Pilsner malt                   | 75–85%  | Base. Light, clean, neutral.                       |
| Flaked rice or flaked corn     | 15–20%  | **Dry out the finish, lighten the body, classic Cold IPA move** |
| Optional: 2-row pale           | 0–10%   | Slight body if needed                              |
| Carapils                       | 0–2%    | Minimal body if needed (skip in true Cold IPA)     |

The **rice or corn adjunct** is the secret. It:

- Adds fermentable sugar without adding malt body or flavor
- **Dries out the finish** dramatically
- Makes the beer **lighter than the ABV implies**
- Doesn't compete with the hops

This is the same trick used in American macro lagers (Budweiser, etc.) — historically derided in craft circles, but reborn here as a deliberate quality choice. The adjuncts in Cold IPA aren't "cheap" — they're **a flavor decision** to maximize hop expression by minimizing grain interference.

## Water — soft, sulfate-leaning

| Ion                | Target (ppm)              |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Calcium (Ca²⁺)     | 80–110                    |
| Magnesium (Mg²⁺)   | 5–15                      |
| Sodium (Na⁺)       | 5–15                      |
| Chloride (Cl⁻)     | 50–80                     |
| Sulfate (SO₄²⁻)    | 130–180                   |
| **Cl : SO₄ ratio** | **~1 : 2 to 1 : 3**       |

Sulfate-leaning like WCIPA, but **less aggressive** than WCIPA's 1:9 inversion. The beer is already dry from the adjuncts and lager attenuation; you don't need extreme sulfate to push dryness.

## Yeast — lager strains

This is the defining style move. You want a **clean lager strain that can ferment slightly warm** without producing diacetyl or sulfur off-flavors.

### Top picks

- **Saflager W-34/70 (dry)** — the most common modern lager yeast. Works at 12–18 °C. Easy availability. Great default for Cold IPA.
- **Wyeast 2001 Pilsner Urquell H Strain / WLP800 Pilsner Lager** — slightly more flocculant; classic lager character.
- **Saflager S-23 (dry)** — slightly fruitier than W-34/70; works well in Cold IPA.
- **Imperial L17 Harvest** — purpose-built for "fast lagers" / IPL-style brewing.
- **Mexican lager strains** (WLP940, etc.) — produce a very clean, slightly sulfury fermentation; some Cold IPA brewers prefer these for a cleaner result.

### Yeast pitch

Lager fermentation needs a **bigger pitch** than ale fermentation because cold fermentation slows yeast metabolism:

- **Liquid:** 2–3× the yeast cell count of an equivalent OG ale (so a 2.5 L starter, or two packs)
- **Dry W-34/70:** **22 g (2 packs)** for 23 L

Underpitching is the most common Cold IPA mistake. Cold + underpitch = stuck fermentation, off-flavors, weeks of trouble.

## Process — lager fermentation, IPA dry hop

### 1. Pitch cool

- **Pitch yeast at 10–12 °C / 50–54 °F** into oxygenated wort.
- Alternative "fast lager" approach: pitch at 18 °C briefly, then drop to 12 °C within 4–6 hours. Reduces lag without compromising profile.

### 2. Ferment at lager temperatures

- **Days 1–2:** 12 °C
- **Days 3–10:** 13–15 °C — slightly warmer than traditional lager (which would hold 9–12 °C). Keeps fermentation reasonably fast for an IPA timeline.
- **Total fermentation: 10–14 days** to FG.

### 3. Diacetyl rest

- **At ~80% attenuation, raise to 18–20 °C for 2–3 days** to clean up any diacetyl. Critical for lager-style fermentation; lager yeast can produce notable diacetyl that needs to be cleaned up before crashing.

### 4. Cold-side dry hop (cool, not cold)

- **At 12 °C**, drop the dry hop under CO₂ purge.
- **Hold 4–7 days.** Slightly longer than NEIPA (3 days) because cold extraction is slower.
- Dose: **5–8 g/L total** (less than NEIPA but more than WCIPA).

### 5. Cold lager (the long crash)

- **Crash to 0–1 °C / 32–34 °F**.
- **Hold 2–4 weeks** — this is where the lager character locks in. Longer is better.

The cold lager step is what makes Cold IPA *taste* lager-like. Skip it and the beer will taste like a sloppy lager-yeast IPA. Don't skip it.

### 6. Optional: gelatin or biofine for clarity

Recommended for Cold IPA. The clarity goal is high.

### 7. Carbonate slightly higher than other IPAs

- **2.7–3.0 vol CO₂** — part of the spritzy mouthfeel.

## Hop bill — modern hops, IPA quantities

Cold IPA uses **modern hops** (the same ones used in NEIPA) but in **smaller total doses** because the lager fermentation lets every gram of hop express clearly. Less is more.

### Suggested bill for a 7% Cold IPA, 23 L batch

| Stage                      | Hops                                | Grams | Notes                          |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------|--------------------------------|
| Bittering (60 min)         | Magnum 14% AA                       | 12 g  | Clean bittering, ~25 IBU       |
| Whirlpool (85 °C, 25 min)  | Citra + Mosaic + Simcoe equal parts | 80 g  | ~12 IBU, plenty of aroma       |
| Cold-side dry hop (12 °C, 5 days) | Citra + Mosaic + Strata          | 100 g | The aroma payload              |

Total hops: ~190 g for 23 L (~8 g/L) — substantially less than NEIPA's 14 g/L but enough.

### Hop choice considerations

- **Modern fruit-forward hops shine here.** Citra, Mosaic, Galaxy, Nelson, Strata.
- **Avoid heavy dank/pine hops.** Chinook and Columbus can produce a Cold IPA that tastes like a thin pilsner trying to be a WCIPA. Stick with "modern" varieties.
- **Single-hop Cold IPAs are excellent.** The lager fermentation lets a single variety express more clearly than in any other style.
- **Less is more.** A 6 g/L Cold IPA can taste louder than a 14 g/L NEIPA because nothing is hiding.

## What "good" looks and tastes like

**Visual:** **Brilliantly clear, very pale gold/straw.** Looks more like a pilsner than an IPA at first glance.

**Aroma:** **Lifted, bright, fruit-forward** — but not as broad as NEIPA. The hop character is clearer and more focused. Less "juicy/jammy"; more "fresh/lifted."

**Flavor:** **Crisp, dry, hop-forward.** The hops express clearly without competing with malt or yeast. **Bone-dry finish** with prominent hop aroma in the mouth.

**Mouthfeel:** **Crisp, spritzy, light.** The high carbonation and dry finish make this feel like a 5% beer despite being 7%.

**Finish:** **Very dry, with lingering hop character.** No malt sweetness. No yeast character. Just bitterness fading into hop aroma.

## Common failure modes

| Symptom                              | Likely cause                                                  |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Tastes like a sloppy NEIPA           | Cold conditioning too short; yeast didn't drop                |
| Sulfur (rotten egg) character        | Lager yeast still working; needs longer cold conditioning     |
| Diacetyl (slick butter)              | Skipped or shortened the diacetyl rest                       |
| Stuck fermentation (high FG)         | Underpitched, fermentation too cold, or wort under-aerated   |
| Tastes thin / boring                 | Hop bill too light, OR adjunct ratio too high (>20% rice/corn) |
| Too sweet                            | Mash temp too high, or insufficient adjuncts                  |
| Hazy / not clear                     | Insufficient cold conditioning, or yeast didn't drop. Add gelatin |
| Stale / cardboard                    | Oxidation. Cold IPA hides this less than NEIPA does           |

## Sub-styles and variations

- **Cold DIPA** — bigger version, 8–10% ABV. Use more hops, more rice/corn for drinkability, longer fermentation.
- **Cold IPL (India Pale Lager)** — sometimes used interchangeably with Cold IPA, sometimes refers to a slightly different style with different proportions.
- **Single-hop Cold IPA** — pick one hop, showcase it. The style favors this approach.
- **"Hazy Cold IPA"** — contradiction; usually a marketing thing.

## Why Cold IPA matters

The style answered a question the IPA category had been asking: **"Can we have hop-forward beer that's actually drinkable in volume?"** NEIPAs are saturated; WCIPAs are bitter. Cold IPA is **clean, dry, and hop-forward at the same time** — a profile no previous style achieved.

For brewers, Cold IPA is also a **technique-builder**: the lager fermentation requires patience, temperature control, and discipline. If you can make a clean Cold IPA, you can probably make a clean pilsner — and that's a meaningful skill.
