# IPA style comparison

A quick-reference table for the modern American IPA family. Useful when designing a new recipe — knowing which knob controls which style helps you avoid drifting between intentional categories.

## The big three (and Cold IPA)

| Dimension              | NEIPA / Hazy              | Modern WCIPA              | Classic WCIPA              | Cold IPA                  |
|------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Appearance**         | Hazy, opaque, glowing pale orange | Brilliantly clear, pale gold | Clear, gold-amber       | Brilliantly clear, very pale |
| **Mouthfeel**          | Soft, pillowy, full       | Crisp, lean              | Crisp, lean              | Very crisp, very dry, lager-like |
| **Bitterness perceived** | Low–medium              | Medium–high              | High                     | High                      |
| **Finish**             | Round, slightly sweet     | Dry                       | Bone dry                 | Extra dry, lager-clean    |
| **Hop character**      | Tropical, juicy, soft     | Pine + tropical (modern Cs + Citra) | Pine, citrus rind, dank | Bright citrus, floral, lifted |
| **Grain bill**         | 30%+ wheat / oats         | 100% pale + small crystal | 100% pale (often)        | Pilsner malt + rice/corn (light adjuncts) |
| **Water Cl:SO₄**       | 2:1 (chloride-forward)    | 1:3 (sulfate-forward)     | 1:4 (very SO₄-forward)   | 1:3 (sulfate-forward, soft)|
| **Yeast**              | London III / Conan / Verdant | Chico (US-05) or modern | Chico                    | **Lager yeast** (W34/70, 2007, etc.) |
| **Ferm temp**          | 18–22 °C                  | 18–22 °C                 | 18–22 °C                 | 12–15 °C (lager temps)    |
| **Bittering target**   | 30–55 IBU                 | 50–70 IBU                | 60–80+ IBU               | 50–70 IBU                 |
| **Whirlpool temp**     | 82 °C                     | 82–95 °C                 | 95+ °C (often)           | 82–90 °C                  |
| **Active-ferm dry hop**| ✓ (biotransformation)     | usually no                | no                       | no (lager doesn't biotransform much) |
| **Cold-side dry hop temp** | 13 °C                | 18 °C                    | 15–18 °C                 | 12 °C (cold)              |
| **Total hopping rate** | 12–18 g/L                 | 8–14 g/L                 | 6–10 g/L                 | 8–12 g/L                  |
| **Cold conditioning**  | Brief (clarification only) | 5–10 days at 0–2 °C      | 7–10+ days at 0–2 °C     | Weeks at 0 °C (lager)     |
| **Carbonation**        | 2.4–2.6 vol               | 2.6–2.8 vol              | 2.6–2.8 vol              | 2.7–3.0 vol               |
| **Peak freshness**     | 2–4 weeks                 | 2–6 weeks                | 4–8 weeks                | 4–10 weeks                |
| **Style philosophy**   | Soft, juicy, full         | Bright, dry, hop-forward | Bitter, clean, classic   | Lager precision + IPA hop |

## The narrative version

### NEIPA

The current "default" for many young craft drinkers. **Soft, juicy, hazy, full.** Designed to feel **smooth and approachable** despite the hop intensity — chloride-forward water, oat/wheat for body, mid-flocculant fruity yeast (London III/Conan), aggressive layered dry hopping with biotransformation. Built for **freshness over longevity** — peak at 2–4 weeks, faded by 8.

### Modern WCIPA

The renaissance style. **Clear and dry like classic WCIPA, but with newer hops.** Cascade + Citra + Mosaic + Centennial-type combos, brewed with sulfate-forward water and Chico, cold-conditioned to brilliance. Often described as "the WCIPA your dad would brew today" — cleaner than NEIPA, more interesting than the original WCIPA template.

### Classic WCIPA

The original American IPA. **Pine, grapefruit pith, dank, very bitter, very dry, very clear.** The Cs in their natural element (Cascade, Centennial, Chinook, Columbus, with Simcoe added). Sierra Nevada Pale Ale → Stone IPA → Pliny the Elder is the lineage. **The bitterness is the star.**

### Cold IPA

The 2020s post-NEIPA innovation. **Brewed with lager yeast at lager temperatures, then dry-hopped like an IPA.** The result is an IPA with **lager-like cleanliness and crispness** — the cleanest possible base for showcasing hops. Often uses adjuncts (rice, corn) to dry it out further. Pioneered by Wayfinder Brewing in Portland (Cold IPA was their term).

Cold IPA is genuinely distinct from WCIPA — different yeast, different fermentation profile, different mouthfeel. Some treat it as a sub-style of WCIPA; most modern beer judges treat it as its own category.

## Decision matrix — which to brew

If you want to brew an IPA and aren't sure which:

- **You like soft, juicy, fruit-forward beers** → NEIPA
- **You miss the original American IPA character** → Classic WCIPA
- **You want clear and bright with modern hops** → Modern WCIPA
- **You like crisp lager-like beers** → Cold IPA
- **You want the most drinkable hop-forward beer possible** → Cold IPA or Modern WCIPA
- **You want something to age 4+ weeks** → Classic WCIPA (NEIPA fades; Cold IPA holds; classic WCIPA actually improves slightly)

## Brewing them all

If you're going to brew across the family, **stagger them**:

- **Brew NEIPA when you have ~3 weeks** — don't queue it; drink it fresh.
- **Brew WCIPA when you can give it ~4 weeks** — the cold conditioning is part of the style.
- **Brew Cold IPA when you have a fermenter for 4–6 weeks** — lager fermentation is slow.

A fun bench-marking exercise: **brew the same hop bill across NEIPA, Modern WCIPA, and Cold IPA**. Same Citra + Mosaic + Simcoe across all three. You'll learn enormous amounts about how grain, water, and yeast change what hops "are."
