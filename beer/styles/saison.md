# Saison

The most fermentation-driven style in beer. **A Belgian / French farmhouse ale built around extremely active yeast** that throws complex esters, phenols, and dries out below 1.005 — well past where any clean ale yeast would stop. The result is a beer with **dramatic yeast character** that can be elegant, funky, peppery, or wild depending on choices.

Saison is also the **most flexible** style in the catalog. There's no single Saison recipe — saisons range from 4% to 8%+ ABV, from extra-dry to slightly malty, from bone-clean to mixed-fermentation funky. The unifying thread is the yeast.

## Defining traits

- **Dry, dry, dry.** Saison yeast attenuates 85–90%+, leaving very little residual sugar. Final gravity often below 1.005.
- **Yeast character forward.** Pepper, clove, fruity esters, slight earthy/horsy character — all from the yeast.
- **High carbonation.** 3.0+ vol CO₂ traditional. Fluffy, prickly mouthfeel.
- **Pale gold to deep gold color.** Light and refreshing.
- **Moderate alcohol** historically (5–6%), though "modern saison" pushes 6–8%.
- **Hops are present but secondary.** Saison drinkers focus on yeast; hops are supportive.

## Vital stats

| Parameter            | Typical range          |
|----------------------|------------------------|
| OG                   | 1.048–1.080 (wide range) |
| FG                   | **1.000–1.008** (very dry) |
| ABV                  | 5.0–8.5%               |
| IBU                  | 25–45                  |
| Color (SRM)          | 4–8 (gold)             |
| Mash pH              | 5.2–5.4                |
| Final beer pH        | 4.0–4.4                |
| Carbonation          | **3.0+ vol** (high)    |

## Grain bill

A saison grain bill is **lean and clean**:

| Grain                     | %       | Why                                        |
|---------------------------|---------|--------------------------------------------|
| Belgian or German pilsner | 70–85%  | Base; clean, slightly sweet                |
| Wheat (malted or flaked)  | 0–20%   | Body, slight haze; classic addition        |
| Sugar (table or candi)    | 5–15%   | **Critical** — drys out the finish further |
| Optional: Munich          | 0–10%   | Slight maltiness                           |
| Optional: Vienna          | 0–10%   | Toasty edge                                |

The **sugar addition** is essential — it ferments to nothing, leaving the beer drier without the malt heaviness that grain alone would provide. Many saisons use 10%+ sugar.

## Yeast — the defining choice

This is where saisons live and die. Three main yeast families:

### Saison yeast (clean, peppery)

- **Wyeast 3724 Belgian Saison / WLP565 Belgian Saison I** — DuPont strain. Famous for **stalling** at ~1.020 then needing warm temp (28+ °C) to finish. Frustrating but produces dry, peppery, classic saison.
- **WLP566 Belgian Saison II** — easier to ferment; fewer stalls.
- **Wyeast 3711 French Saison** — modern favorite. Doesn't stall; ferments dramatically (88% attenuation), peppery, easy.
- **Lallemand Belle Saison (dry)** — quick, clean, slightly milder character. Easy entry-level saison yeast.

### Brett blends (funky, complex)

- **Brettanomyces bruxellensis** added alongside saison yeast → barnyard, leather, sour fruit notes
- Some saisons are **100% Brett-fermented** for very different character
- See [`mixed-fermentation.md`](mixed-fermentation.md) for the broader category

### Hybrid / modern

Some breweries use **kveik strains** (especially Hornindal) as "saison-adjacent" yeast for high-temp, tropical-ester results.

## The DuPont stall problem

If you use Wyeast 3724 / WLP565, expect this:

- Fermentation starts vigorously
- Around days 5–7, BG drops to ~1.020
- **Fermentation stalls** — yeast dies off at lower temperatures
- **Solution:** raise temperature to **28–32 °C / 82–90 °F** for several days. The yeast will resume and ferment to ~1.000.

Modern saison brewers either:

- **Use a different strain** (3711 or BSI which don't stall)
- **Pitch warm and free-rise** (start at 22 °C, let it climb naturally to 30+)
- **Have patience** with the stall + heat method

## Process

### 1. Mash

- **Temp 64–66 °C / 147–151 °F** — favors fermentability for the dry finish
- **60 min mash**

### 2. Boil

- **60–75 min boil**
- Add **sugar in last 10 min** of boil

### 3. Hops

Saisons typically use **traditional European noble hops** for character that complements yeast:

- **Saaz, Hallertau, Tettnang, Styrian Goldings** — earthy, herbal, peppery
- **Some modern saisons use American hops** (Citra, Mosaic) for tropical / wine character — these "Hoppy Saisons" are essentially their own substyle

A typical saison hop schedule:

- **60 min:** Saaz or Hallertau, ~25 IBU
- **15 min:** Saaz, ~5 IBU
- **5 min:** Saaz, aroma punch

### 4. Ferment warm

- **Pitch at 22 °C / 72 °F** (a common saison error is pitching too cool)
- **Let temp rise** during fermentation — to 28–32 °C is fine for saison strains, even encouraged
- Ferment 1–3 weeks; don't crash early

### 5. Carbonate high

- **3.0+ vol CO₂**
- Bottle conditioning is traditional (more carbonation than kegging usually allows easily)

## Recipe template — classic farmhouse saison

For **23 L** at OG 1.060:

| Ingredient                | Amount  |
|---------------------------|---------|
| Belgian pilsner malt      | 4500 g  |
| Wheat malt or flaked      | 600 g   |
| Munich (optional)         | 200 g   |
| Cane sugar                | 400 g (added at 10 min in boil) |
| Hops (Saaz):              |         |
| - 60 min                  | 30 g    |
| - 10 min                  | 15 g    |
| - 5 min                   | 10 g    |
| Yeast: Wyeast 3711 French Saison | 1 starter |

**Mash:** 65 °C, 60 min
**Boil:** 60 min
**Ferment:** pitch 22 °C, free-rise to 28 °C
**Carbonation:** 3.0 vol

Drop temp to ~22 °C only at the very end; ferment for **2 weeks minimum**.

## What "good saison" tastes like

- **Pepper-forward** — black pepper, white pepper, slight clove
- **Fruity esters** — pear, citrus, sometimes banana
- **Bone dry finish** — should not feel sweet at all
- **Slightly tart** but not sour (unless mixed-ferment)
- **Effervescent** — high carbonation with fluffy mouthfeel
- **Subtle yeast funk** — barnyard, hay, earth — depends on strain
- **Hops audible but not loud** — a saison is yeast-dominated

## Variations

### Brett saison

Add a **Brettanomyces bruxellensis** culture (Wyeast 5112 / WLP650) at the end of primary fermentation. **Age 3–6 months** in secondary or in bottle. Develops barnyard, leather, fruit, dry character. The "long aged saison" tradition.

### Hoppy saison

Use **modern American hops** (Citra, Nelson Sauvin, Galaxy) in addition to / instead of European hops. Excellent — saison yeast's dryness pairs beautifully with hop-forward flavors. **Nelson Sauvin specifically is a saison superstar.**

### High-ABV saison ("saison brett")

Push OG to 1.075–1.085. More malt, more sugar. Result: 8%+ ABV saison with all the same dryness and character. Excellent as a base for Brett aging.

### Grisette

Lower-ABV (~3.5–4.5%) sister style. Same yeast and approach, lighter grain bill. Historical Belgian "miner's beer" — easy drinking, refreshing.

## Food pairings

Saisons are **arguably the best food beer in existence** because:

- **Dry, doesn't compete with food**
- **Carbonation cleanses palate**
- **Pepper notes complement food spices**
- **Versatile alcohol levels**

Pair with: charcuterie, fish, seafood, summer salads, spicy food (Thai, Indian), grilled vegetables, soft cheeses, fruit desserts.

Less ideal: hearty stouts-and-mac food, very rich/fatty cuisine.

## Common saison mistakes

- **Fermenting too cool.** Saison yeast loves heat. Don't ferment at 18 °C.
- **Crashing too early.** Stall at 1.020 is yeast-style — wait it out, raise temp, give it time.
- **Using wrong yeast.** Without saison yeast, you don't have a saison. Don't use US-05.
- **Too low carbonation.** Saison needs 3.0+ vol — higher than ales typically.
- **Over-hopping.** Saison's character is yeast-forward; lots of hops fight that. Keep IBU 25–45.
