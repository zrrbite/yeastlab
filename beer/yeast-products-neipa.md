# NEIPA yeast — liquid vs dry product comparison

Two paths to the same fermentation: **liquid yeast** (vials and packages from Wyeast / White Labs / Imperial / GigaYeast) and **dry yeast** (sachets from Lallemand, Fermentis). For NEIPA specifically, both produce excellent beer — but they differ in convenience, cost, freshness, strain availability, and consistency.

This file is the practical comparison for **NEIPA brewers choosing yeast products**. Strain-specific notes (London Ale III character, Conan character, etc.) are in [`yeast-strains.md`](yeast-strains.md).

## TL;DR

- **Liquid yeast** — wider strain selection, often considered "more authentic" for traditional NEIPA strains (London Ale III, Conan), but requires a starter, costs more, and has shelf-life constraints.
- **Dry yeast** — radically more convenient, longer shelf life, no starter required, **and modern dry NEIPA strains (especially Lallemand Verdant IPA) are now competitive in flavor with liquid alternatives.** For most NEIPA brewers, dry yeast is the practical default.

If you're starting out: **buy Lallemand Verdant IPA dry, brew with it for several batches, then experiment with liquid strains** if you want to.

## Liquid yeast for NEIPA

### Major manufacturers

- **Wyeast** — vials of liquid yeast in 100 mL "smack packs"
- **White Labs (WLP)** — pourable vials
- **Imperial Yeast** — modern entry, popular among hazy-IPA brewers; pure pitch packs
- **GigaYeast** — boutique, modern strains, smaller market share
- **East Coast Yeast** — small-batch, rare strains, hard to source outside US East Coast

### Top NEIPA liquid strains

| Strain                       | Manufacturer        | Character                                  |
|------------------------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| **London Ale III (Wyeast 1318)** | Wyeast        | The original NEIPA workhorse. Soft, fruity, mid-low flocculation, ~71–75% attenuation |
| **WLP066 London Fog**        | White Labs          | Same lineage as 1318. Slightly different fermentation behavior |
| **Imperial A38 Juice**       | Imperial Yeast      | Modern London III variant. Excellent for NEIPA |
| **GigaYeast GY054**          | GigaYeast           | London III variant; widely-used in modern NEIPAs |
| **Vermont Ale / Conan (Imperial A04 Barbarian)** | Imperial | The Heady Topper strain. Distinct peach character |
| **WLP095 Burlington Ale**    | White Labs          | Closely related to Conan |
| **Imperial A24 Dry Hop**     | Imperial Yeast      | Modern New England-leaning strain |
| **Lallemand New England (liquid version not common)** | — | (Lallemand mainly dry) |
| **WLP051 California Ale V**  | White Labs          | More flocculant cousin of WLP001; less ester |
| **Wyeast 1098 British Ale**  | Wyeast              | Older British strain; more flocculant for cleaner NEIPAs |

### Pros

- **Wider strain diversity** — many distinct yeast cultures available
- **Often the "true" version** of historic strains (London III, Conan)
- **Larger initial cell count per package** (typically 100 billion vs ~6 billion in dry)
- **Some strains exist only as liquid** (most boutique/rare strains)

### Cons

- **Requires a starter** (usually) for sufficient cell count
- **Expensive** — typically $9–$15 per vial/pack
- **Shelf life:** 6+ months refrigerated, but quality declines after 3 months
- **Production date matters** — buy fresh; old liquid yeast = stuck fermentation
- **Supply variable** — popular strains can be out of stock seasonally
- **Temperature-sensitive in shipping** — needs cold-chain logistics

### Liquid yeast workflow

For a NEIPA at 23 L, OG 1.065 with 1 vial of London Ale III:

1. **Buy fresh** (within 60 days of production date)
2. **Plan a starter:**
   - Make 1.5–2 L of starter wort (1.040 OG)
   - Pitch the vial into starter wort
   - Stir plate (best) or shake intermittently for 18–24 hours
   - Refrigerate the starter for 24+ hours; decant the spent wort, leaving ~200 mL of yeast slurry
3. **Pitch the yeast slurry** into your brew wort

Without a starter: an unrefreshed liquid pack is **underpitched** for any high-OG ale, leading to stuck fermentation, off-flavors, and sometimes sluggish biotransformation.

### Cost per batch (liquid)

For a single 23 L NEIPA: **$10–15 for yeast + $2–3 for starter ingredients** = **~$13–18 per batch**.

## Dry yeast for NEIPA

### Major manufacturers

- **Lallemand** (Canada) — the leading craft-friendly dry yeast manufacturer. Many specialty strains.
- **Fermentis** (France) — historic; Safale and Saflager lines. More mainstream.
- **Mangrove Jack's** — UK-based; smaller selection but good NEIPA option.

### Top NEIPA dry strains

#### Lallemand strains (the headliners)

- **Verdant IPA** — **the modern NEIPA dry yeast**. Released around 2020. Soft tropical character, mid-flocculation, excellent biotransformation. Released in 11g sachets.
- **New England (LalBrew)** — purpose-built for hazy IPA. Soft, fruity, slightly different from Verdant.
- **Voss Kveik (LalBrew)** — Norwegian kveik strain in dry form. Excellent for very-warm-fast NEIPA fermentation.
- **Belle Saison** — dry saison. Used for hazy saison hybrids occasionally.
- **Nottingham** — too flocculant for NEIPA (drops bright too fast); avoid for hazy IPA but good for cleaner styles.

#### Fermentis (Safale) strains

- **Safale US-05** — the dry workhorse. Slightly more clean / less ester than NEIPA-specific yeasts. Good for "modern WCIPA" leaning NEIPAs.
- **Safale S-04** — English ale yeast. Highly flocculant; not ideal for NEIPA.
- **Safale BE-256** — Belgian; not a NEIPA strain.

#### Mangrove Jack's

- **M44 American West Coast** — solid US-05 alternative.
- **M76 Bavarian Lager** — for Cold IPA brewing.

### The Verdant IPA story

Verdant IPA is **the most significant recent dry yeast release for hop-forward beers.** Released by Lallemand around 2019–2020 in collaboration with Verdant Brewing (UK), the strain produces:

- Soft tropical / peach esters
- Mid-flocculation (haze stays)
- ~75–80% attenuation
- Excellent biotransformation activity
- Hop-creep tolerance above average

It's broadly considered to **rival or match** the traditional London III liquid for NEIPA character — at one-third the cost, with no starter needed, and with multi-year shelf life.

The release of Verdant IPA changed the practical advice for NEIPA brewers. **For most home brewers, Lallemand Verdant IPA in dry form is now the default recommendation.**

### Pros

- **Convenient.** No starter required.
- **Long shelf life.** Refrigerated, dry yeast is good for **2+ years** from manufacture date.
- **Cheap.** Typically $4–6 per 11g sachet.
- **Reliable.** Quality control on dry yeast is very good; consistent fermentation expected.
- **No supply chain issues** (mostly) — sachets ship and store easily.
- **Direct pitch** — open the sachet, sprinkle on wort. Done.

### Cons

- **Smaller strain library** than liquid — maybe 15–20 craft-relevant dry strains vs hundreds of liquid options
- **No truly rare or boutique strains** — only mainstream options
- **Fewer "authentic" historic strains** — if you specifically want the original London III, you're using liquid
- **Slight character differences** — even the best dry strains may not perfectly match their liquid equivalents

### Dry yeast workflow

For a NEIPA at 23 L, OG 1.065:

1. **Buy fresh** (check date; under 1 year old preferred)
2. **Pitch directly** — sprinkle 11g (one sachet) of Verdant IPA on the wort surface, stir gently to incorporate
3. **Or rehydrate** (optional but recommended): mix dry yeast with 100 mL of water at 25–28 °C, wait 15 min, then pitch
4. **For higher gravity** (>1.075), pitch 22g (two sachets)

That's it. No starter, no stir plate, no refrigeration logistics.

### Cost per batch (dry)

For a single 23 L NEIPA: **$5 for one Verdant IPA sachet** = **~$5 per batch**.

For a 23 L DIPA at 1.080 OG: **$10 for two sachets** = **~$10 per batch** (still cheaper than liquid + starter).

## Side-by-side comparison

| Factor                      | Liquid yeast              | Dry yeast (Lallemand)        |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Cost per NEIPA batch**    | $13–18                    | **$5–10**                    |
| **Strain diversity**        | High (100+ NEIPA-relevant) | Limited (5–8 NEIPA-relevant) |
| **Shelf life refrigerated** | 3–6 months optimum        | **2+ years**                 |
| **Starter required?**       | **Yes** (most cases)      | No                           |
| **Storage**                 | Fridge                    | Fridge or pantry             |
| **Lead time**               | Order, ship cold, plan starter | **Pitch on brew day**     |
| **Character authenticity**  | Higher for traditional strains | Modern strains are competitive |
| **Supply reliability**      | Variable                  | Highly consistent            |
| **Best for**                | Specific strain pursuit, max character | Convenience, cost, broad-appeal NEIPAs |

## When to choose liquid

- **You want a specific strain** that isn't available dry (boutique strains, specific Trillium/Hill Farmstead clones, etc.)
- **You're chasing a specific brewery's character** that uses a known liquid strain
- **You're doing serious side-by-side brewing** where strain authenticity matters
- **You have time and a stir plate** to make starters routinely
- **Cost isn't a constraint**

## When to choose dry (Lallemand Verdant IPA)

- **You're starting out** with NEIPAs — Verdant is forgiving and reliable
- **You want convenience** — no starter, predictable
- **You brew sporadically** — dry yeast keeps in your pantry
- **You want competitive flavor** at a fraction of the cost
- **You're scaling up production** for friends or sale — dry yeast is logistically much easier
- **Supply chain matters** — dry yeast is reliably available

## A pragmatic recommendation

**For most home NEIPA brewers, Lallemand Verdant IPA dry is the right default.**

Reasons:

- **Quality is now fully competitive** with traditional liquid strains
- **Cost is ~1/3 of liquid + starter**
- **Convenience is dramatically higher**
- **Reliability eliminates a fermentation variable**

Use liquid when you have a specific reason — chasing a clone of a particular brewery, doing strain comparison experiments, or wanting access to boutique / rare strains.

## Yeast pitch rate calculator

For 23 L NEIPA:

| OG       | Liquid pitch       | Dry pitch (Verdant IPA) |
|----------|--------------------|-------------------------|
| 1.050    | 1 vial (no starter) | 11 g (1 sachet)        |
| 1.060    | 1 vial + 1 L starter | 11 g (1 sachet)       |
| 1.070    | 1 vial + 2 L starter | 11 g (1 sachet)       |
| 1.080    | 2 vials + 2 L starter | 22 g (2 sachets)     |
| 1.090+   | Multiple starters     | 22 g + extra (3 sachets if you can) |

For DIPAs and high-gravity NEIPAs, **always over-pitch** rather than under-pitch — the cost difference is small, the impact on fermentation health is significant.

## Yeast freshness — for both

Whether liquid or dry:

- **Buy fresh.** Liquid: under 60 days from production. Dry: under 1 year.
- **Store cold.** Both benefit from refrigeration.
- **Don't refreeze** dry yeast that was thawed (rare issue but worth noting).
- **Smell-check** dry yeast on opening — should smell like bread / yeast, not off / chemical.
