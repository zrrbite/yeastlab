# Brioche

The most enriched of breads — high butter, high egg, high sugar, soft and golden. **A different beast from lean dough.** Brioche taught generations of French bakers everything they know about handling fat and gluten in the same dough.

This recipe uses commercial yeast — sourdough brioche exists but the ratios and timeline are quite different. Stick with commercial yeast for predictable, golden, café-style results.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Mix + butter incorporation | 25 min    | Room temp   |
| First rise             | 1.5 h         | 22–25 °C    |
| **Cold rest (critical)** | **8–24 h**  | 4 °C        |
| Shape                  | 15 min        | Room temp (cool) |
| Final proof            | 2.5–3 h       | 22–25 °C    |
| Bake                   | 25–35 min     | 175 °C      |

The cold rest is essential. Brioche dough at room temp is a sticky butter mess; cold, it handles like normal bread.

## Formula

For **1 large loaf** (in a 9×5 pullman or 1.5 lb loaf pan):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour                      | 100%     | 500 g |
| Whole milk                       | 30%      | 150 g |
| Eggs (medium)                    | 30%      | 150 g (3 eggs) |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 10 g  |
| Sugar                            | 12%      | 60 g  |
| **Unsalted butter (very soft)**  | **40%**  | **200 g** |
| Active dry yeast                 | 1.5%     | 7.5 g |
| Egg wash (1 egg + 1 tsp water)   | —        | for top |

**Total dough:** ~1080 g

> **40% butter is a moderate brioche.** Classic French brioche can go to 80% butter (brioche à tête, brioche Nanterre); 40% is the "approachable home brioche" target — rich enough to be unmistakably brioche, lean enough to handle.

## The build

### 1. Mix the dough (no butter yet)

In a stand mixer with dough hook (highly recommended for brioche — hand-kneading is possible but punishing):

- 150 g warm milk (~28 °C)
- 60 g sugar
- 7.5 g yeast → bloom 5 min
- 3 eggs (150 g), beaten
- 500 g bread flour
- 10 g salt

Mix on low until shaggy. Knead on medium-low for **6–8 min** until smooth and elastic.

### 2. Add butter (the long step)

This is the heart of brioche. **Add butter in 4 batches**, ~50 g each, while the mixer runs on medium-low:

1. Add 50 g butter, knead until incorporated (~3 min)
2. Add another 50 g, knead until incorporated (~3 min)
3. Add another 50 g, knead (~3 min)
4. Add the final 50 g, knead until **fully incorporated and smooth** (~5 min)

Total kneading with butter: **~15 minutes**. The dough will look broken at first — chunks of butter, soft mess — then come together as a glossy, elastic, slightly sticky mass.

**The dough is done when:**

- It's smooth and shiny
- It pulls cleanly from the bowl walls (mostly)
- It has a slight sheen from the incorporated butter
- It's stretchy without tearing

### 3. First rise (1.5 hours)

- Lightly oil bowl, place dough back, cover
- **Rise 1.5 hours** at room temp until **roughly doubled**
- Don't fold or knead during this rise

### 4. Cold rest — critical step (8–24 hours)

- Cover dough tightly, place in fridge **overnight (8+ hours)**

This step is non-negotiable. Cold brioche dough:

- **Handles** instead of becoming a butter mess
- **Develops more flavor** during the long cold ferment
- **Absorbs the butter** more fully into the dough structure
- **Shapes cleanly** when cold

Going beyond 24 hours is fine — the dough holds 36–48 hours in the fridge if needed. Past that, fermentation becomes uncontrolled.

### 5. Shape

Brioche shapes:

#### Pullman / loaf

- Tip cold dough onto lightly floured bench
- Press into a rough rectangle
- Roll up tightly into a log, sealing seam
- Place seam-side down in a buttered loaf pan
- For a "stripey" cross-section: divide dough into 3 equal pieces, ball each, place side-by-side in pan

#### Brioche à tête (the classic mushroom-shaped roll)

- Divide cold dough into 12 equal pieces
- Pinch off ¼ from each piece — that's the "head"
- Form the larger ¾ into a ball, place in buttered fluted brioche tin
- Form the small piece into a teardrop, push the pointed end into the center of the larger ball
- Use brioche-shaped tin for proper form

For a first try: **just do the loaf**. The brioche à tête is a project.

### 6. Final proof (2.5–3 hours)

- Cover lightly with plastic wrap or a tea towel
- **Proof at room temp 2.5–3 hours** until the loaf is **noticeably risen** (1.5–2× the dough volume)

Cold brioche dough takes longer to come to life than typical bread. Be patient.

### 7. Egg wash and bake

- **Brush top thoroughly with egg wash** (beaten egg + water)
- **Bake at 175 °C / 350 °F for 25–35 minutes** until **deep golden brown**
- Internal temp: **88–92 °C / 190–198 °F**
- If top browns too fast, tent with foil for the last 10 min

### 8. Cool

- **Cool in pan 10 min**, then turn out onto a rack
- **Cool completely** before slicing — brioche needs the structure to set

## Variations

### Higher-butter brioche (60–80%)

Increase butter to 300–400 g. Add proportionally more egg (4 eggs instead of 3). Same process but expect more cold-rest time and more delicate handling. Result: dramatically richer, almost cake-like.

### Brioche burger buns

After cold rest, divide into 8 pieces (~135 g each), shape into balls, proof in a buttered baking dish, bake at 190 °C for 18–22 min. Egg wash with sesame seeds. The internet's favorite burger buns.

### Cinnamon brioche / brioche cinnamon rolls

After cold rest, roll dough into a 30×40 cm rectangle. Spread with **softened butter + cinnamon-sugar (60 g brown sugar + 1 tbsp cinnamon)**. Roll up tightly, slice into 12 rolls, place in pan, proof, bake. Glaze with **cream cheese icing** when warm.

### Chocolate brioche

Add **150 g of chocolate chips** during the last fold of butter. Distribute through the dough. Especially good as buns.

### Savory brioche (cheese & herb)

Reduce sugar to 20 g, add **100 g grated gruyere + 10 g chopped fresh thyme** during the last butter fold. Excellent for sandwiches or French toast in a savory direction.

## Common issues

- **Dough never comes together.** Butter wasn't soft enough — must be very soft (almost mayonnaise consistency) before adding.
- **Greasy or oily bread.** Butter wasn't fully incorporated. Knead longer, in smaller batches.
- **Dense, didn't rise.** Yeast was old, OR rise temp too cold. Brioche likes warm rises.
- **Burned crust before insides done.** Oven runs hot, OR pan was dark (absorbs more heat). Foil tent early.
- **Sticky dough impossible to shape.** Skipped the cold rest. **Always cold rest brioche** before shaping.
- **Crumb is dry/crumbly the next day.** Probably overbaked. Internal temp 90 °C is enough.

## Storage

- **Best fresh** — within 24 hours of baking
- **Day 2:** still good, slightly drier crumb
- **Day 3+:** make French toast or bread pudding (its highest calling)
- **Freezes well** — slice, freeze, toast straight from freezer

## Why this is the gateway drug

Brioche teaches you:

- **Handling enriched dough** — fat and gluten in coexistence
- **The value of the cold rest** for shape and flavor (DEAM principles in commercial-yeast clothing)
- **Patience with kneading** — full butter incorporation takes time
- **Visual / tactile cues** — when butter is "in" vs "still chunky"
- **The egg wash discipline** — proper coverage = proper color

Once you can make brioche, **enriched doughs in general** become accessible — challah, Hokkaido milk bread, German stollen, panettone (the project of projects).
