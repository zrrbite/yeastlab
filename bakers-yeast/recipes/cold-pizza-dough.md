# Cold-fermented pizza dough (commercial yeast)

The commercial-yeast counterpart to [`../../sourdough/recipes/pizza-ny.md`](../../sourdough/recipes/pizza-ny.md). Uses **active dry yeast or instant yeast** with a **24–72 hour cold rest** to develop complex flavor without needing a sourdough starter. **The result rivals sourdough pizza dough at a fraction of the maintenance cost.**

The cold rest is the magic. Same DEAM principles apply — amylase liberates sugar at fridge temps, gluten develops passively, dough becomes extensible — even with commercial yeast.

## Why cold-ferment with commercial yeast

- **No starter to maintain.** Just buy yeast packets.
- **Predictable timing.** Commercial yeast is reliable across fermentation conditions.
- **DEAM principles still apply.** Cold rest still drives flavor and extensibility.
- **The result tastes like good pizza dough.** Side-by-side with sourdough at 48-hour cold rest, the difference is subtle.
- **Faster total timeline.** No need for the cold-fed levain prep step.

This is the **"Tuesday night pizza" recipe.** Mix Sunday night, eat Tuesday or Wednesday.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Mix                    | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Bulk                   | 30 min        | Room temp   |
| Ball + cold rest       | 24–72 h       | 4 °C        |
| Final proof            | 1.5–3 h       | Room temp   |
| Stretch + bake         | 8 min each    | 260–290 °C  |

## Formula

For **4 × 280 g balls** (14" pies):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour (12.5–13% protein)   | 100%     | 700 g |
| Water                            | 62%      | 434 g |
| Salt                             | 2.5%     | 17.5 g|
| Olive oil                        | 3%       | 21 g  |
| Sugar                            | 1%       | 7 g   |
| **Active dry yeast**             | 0.3%     | 2 g   |

**Total dough:** ~1180 g → 4 × 295 g balls

> **Why these numbers.** 62% hydration handles cleanly for stretching. 2.5% salt is slightly higher than sourdough — supports the long cold rest by slowing yeast. **Tiny amount of yeast (0.3%)** is the trick — fermentation runs slow over 48 hours rather than blowing through in 4 hours. Sugar gives the yeast immediate fuel and helps browning at home-oven temps.

## The build

### 1. Mix (15 min)

In a large bowl or stand mixer:

- 434 g warm water (~30 °C / 86 °F)
- 7 g sugar dissolved in water
- Sprinkle 2 g active dry yeast on top, let bloom 5 min (foamy on top)
- Add 21 g olive oil
- Add 700 g bread flour and 17.5 g salt
- Mix until shaggy
- Knead by hand or on stand mixer hook for **6–8 minutes** until **smooth, elastic, slightly tacky**

The dough should pull cleanly from the bowl walls.

### 2. Brief bulk (30 min)

- Lightly oil bowl, place dough back in, cover
- Rest at room temp **30 minutes** — just enough for yeast to begin

### 3. Divide and ball

- Tip dough onto lightly floured bench
- **Divide into 4 equal pieces** (~295 g each — use a scale)
- **Ball each tightly** with surface tension (cup-and-drag technique)
- Place each in a lightly oiled, sealed container with **2× headroom**

### 4. Cold rest (24–72 hours)

**Straight into the fridge** at 4 °C.

- **24 hours minimum** — usable, less developed
- **48 hours** — sweet spot for most pies
- **72 hours** — peak flavor, slightly slacker dough
- **96+ hours** — protease starts softening gluten too much; over-fermented territory

The cold rest is doing what it does in DEAM:

- **Amylase liberates sugar** → better browning, slight sweetness
- **Gluten develops passively** → easier to stretch
- **Yeast slowly ferments** → flavor compounds accumulate
- **Schedule flexibility** — bake any night Mon–Wed for a Sunday-night mix

### 5. Final proof (1.5–3 h)

- **Take balls out of fridge 1.5–3 hours before baking**, depending on kitchen temp
- They should look puffy, jiggle when nudged
- Slightly under-proofed is better than over for pizza — you want puff in the rim

### 6. Stretch and top

- Heavy dust of bench flour (50/50 bread flour + semolina)
- Slide a ball out gently
- Press flat from center outward, leaving a 1.5 cm rim untouched
- Stretch by hand to **14"** (~35 cm) — thinner in middle, slightly thicker rim
- Top:
  - **Sauce:** crushed San Marzano + salt + oregano + drizzle olive oil
  - **Cheese:** shredded **low-moisture mozzarella** (NOT fresh mozzarella for this style — too wet)
  - Layer cheese first, sauce in dollops on top — keeps cheese from sogging

### 7. Bake

- **Preheat oven to max** (260–290 °C / 500–550 °F) with **stone or steel on upper-middle rack** for **45 min minimum**
- Optional: switch to **broiler** for the last 2 min of preheating
- **Launch on a peel** dusted with semolina
- **Bake 5–7 min** until bottom is set and rim starts to color
- **Switch to broiler** for the last 60–90 sec to char the rim and brown cheese
- Slide out, slice, eat

## Variations

### Pan pizza (Detroit/Sicilian style)

Same dough, but at 70% hydration. After cold rest, push dough into a heavily oiled rectangular pan. Top edge-to-edge with cheese (the rim of cheese against the pan is the Detroit trademark). Bake at 260 °C for 12–15 min.

### Thin and crispy ("New York Bar style")

Use 60% hydration. Cold rest 72 hours. Roll thin (don't hand-stretch — use a rolling pin). Bake very hot, very fast.

### Whole wheat pizza dough

Replace 100 g of the bread flour with **whole wheat flour** (so 600 g bread + 100 g whole wheat). Different character — nuttier, denser, more fermentation-active.

### Sourdough hybrid

Add **50 g of cold sourdough starter** alongside the commercial yeast. Reduce commercial yeast to 1 g. Best of both worlds — flavor depth from the starter, reliability from the yeast.

## Comparison to sourdough pizza

| Aspect              | Sourdough (pizza-ny.md) | This recipe                    |
|---------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Flavor depth        | Slightly more complex   | Very close — DEAM principles bridge the gap |
| Convenience         | Need active levain      | Just buy yeast                 |
| Reliability         | Variable (starter health) | Highly consistent             |
| Maintenance         | Weekly starter feeding  | None                           |
| Total time          | 3+ days (counting levain prep) | 24-72 hr cold + active steps |
| Skill required      | Sourdough fluency       | Beginner-friendly              |

**Honest assessment:** if you don't already have a sourdough starter going, the commercial-yeast version is the better choice for pizza. The flavor difference at 48-hour cold rest is small enough that the convenience wins.

If you do have a starter, do the sourdough version sometimes for the slightly deeper flavor and the satisfaction.

## Common issues

- **Dough won't hold a circle when stretching.** Under-proofed (cold), or balls weren't shaped tightly enough. Surface tension matters.
- **Dough is too slack.** Over-fermented — used too much yeast, or rest too long. Drop yeast next time, or shorter rest.
- **Crust is pale after baking.** Oven not hot enough, OR sugar added at zero (the sugar in this recipe helps browning).
- **Crust is tough.** Dough cold from fridge — not warm enough for stretching. Let warm 2+ hours.
- **Bottom soggy.** Stone not hot enough, or too much sauce. Preheat 45+ min, sauce in dollops not a layer.

## Why DEAM principles transfer

This recipe is **DEAM applied to commercial yeast.** Same principles:

- **Cold-rest enzymatic priming** — amylase frees sugar at fridge temps regardless of yeast species
- **Long, slow fermentation** — both wild and commercial yeast benefit
- **Schedule flexibility** — the dough waits for you, not the other way around
- **Less yeast + more time** — the universal "more flavor" lever

Once you understand the DEAM framework, you can apply it to **any yeast-leavened dough** — sourdough, commercial yeast, hybrid. The principles transfer.
