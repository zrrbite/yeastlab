# Same-day pita (commercial yeast)

A 3-hour weeknight pita using commercial yeast. **Faster than the sourdough version** (which still requires a prepped levain), this is the recipe when you want pita tonight without planning ahead.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Mix                    | 10 min        | Room temp   |
| Bulk                   | 1 h           | 22–25 °C    |
| Divide + bench rest    | 30 min        | Room temp   |
| Roll + final rest      | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Bake                   | 90 sec each   | 280 °C      |

About 3 hours total — most of which is dough resting.

## Formula

For **8 pitas** (each ~80 g):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour                      | 95%      | 380 g |
| Whole wheat flour                | 5%       | 20 g  |
| Water                            | 60%      | 240 g |
| Olive oil                        | 3%       | 12 g  |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 8 g   |
| Sugar                            | 1%       | 4 g   |
| Active dry yeast                 | 1%       | 4 g   |

**Total dough:** ~668 g → 8 × ~80 g balls

## The build

### 1. Mix (10 min)

- 240 g warm water (~30 °C / 86 °F)
- Add 4 g sugar, sprinkle 4 g yeast on top, let bloom 5 min
- Add 12 g olive oil
- Add 380 g bread flour + 20 g whole wheat + 8 g salt
- Mix until shaggy
- Knead 5–7 min by hand or with stand mixer until smooth and elastic

### 2. Bulk (1 hour)

- Lightly oil bowl, place dough back, cover
- **Rise 1 hour** at room temp until doubled

### 3. Divide and bench rest (30 min)

- Divide into 8 equal pieces (~80 g each)
- Pre-shape into tight balls
- Cover, rest 30 min — **critical step**, lets gluten relax for rolling

### 4. Roll and final rest (15 min)

- Roll each ball into a **3 mm thick disc, ~16 cm diameter**
- Roll evenly — uneven thickness = uneven puff
- Place on parchment paper or floured surface
- Cover, rest 15 min

### 5. Bake (90 sec each)

#### Setup

- **Pizza stone or steel on highest rack**, preheated **45 minutes minimum** at **max oven temp (typically 280–290 °C / 535–550 °F)**
- Optional: broiler on for last 5 min of preheating

#### The bake

1. Slide one pita onto the hot stone
2. Wait 30 seconds — should start bubbling
3. At ~45–60 sec, pita should puff into a dramatic balloon
4. At 90 sec, pull it out
5. Wrap in a clean towel as you bake the next one

If they don't puff: stone wasn't hot enough (#1 cause), or pita rolled too thick.

### Stove-top alternative

- Preheat dry cast-iron skillet on max heat 10 min
- Slap pitas on, 30 sec per side, then flip and 30 sec more
- Wrap in towel as you go

## Variations

### Whole wheat pita

Bump whole wheat to 30%. Slightly denser pocket but more flavor.

### Garlic-herb pita

Brush rolled discs with garlic-infused olive oil before baking. Sprinkle za'atar after baking.

### Stuffed pita

Roll discs slightly larger (~18 cm), spread filling on half, fold over, seal edges, bake. Cheese-stuffed flatbread, instantly.

## Same as sourdough version?

Compared to [`../../sourdough/recipes/pita.md`](../../sourdough/recipes/pita.md):

- **Faster** (3 hours vs 4 hours)
- **No levain prep** required
- **Slightly less flavor depth** but the difference is subtle in pita (which gets a lot of its character from char + warmth)
- **More reliable** if your starter is sluggish

For weeknight pita: this version. For Saturday-with-time pita: the sourdough version.

## Common issues

Same as sourdough pita — see [`../../sourdough/recipes/pita.md`](../../sourdough/recipes/pita.md) for full troubleshooting.

The most common is **stone not hot enough**. Preheat longer than you think; verify with an oven thermometer.
