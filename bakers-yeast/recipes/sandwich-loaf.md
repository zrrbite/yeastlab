# Soft sandwich loaf (pullman / pain de mie)

The gold standard of homemade sandwich bread — soft, even crumb, square cross-section, tender enough for kids' lunches but flavorful enough for adults' avocado toast. **Bakes in a pullman pan (with a sliding lid) for that perfect square shape**, but also works in a regular loaf pan for a dome-topped version.

A milk-and-butter loaf with commercial yeast. Easy, reliable, dramatically better than store-bought.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Mix                    | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Bulk                   | 1.5 h         | 22–25 °C    |
| Shape                  | 10 min        | Room temp   |
| Final proof            | 1 h           | 22–25 °C    |
| Bake                   | 35–40 min     | 190 °C      |

About 4 hours total. Makes one 1.5-lb loaf.

## Formula

For **1 sandwich loaf** in a 9×5 (or pullman):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour                      | 100%     | 500 g |
| Whole milk (warm)                | 50%      | 250 g |
| Water (warm)                     | 15%      | 75 g  |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 10 g  |
| Sugar                            | 5%       | 25 g  |
| Unsalted butter (softened)       | 6%       | 30 g  |
| Active dry yeast                 | 1.5%     | 7.5 g |

**Total dough:** ~898 g

> **Why these numbers.** The milk + water combination gives the soft, slightly sweet crumb without the heaviness of all-milk dough. **6% butter** is enough for tenderness without going into brioche territory. **5% sugar** feeds the yeast and adds a touch of sweetness; less than discard rolls (this is sandwich bread, not dessert).

## The build

### 1. Mix (15 min)

In a stand mixer with dough hook (or by hand):

- 250 g warm milk + 75 g warm water (~30 °C)
- Add 25 g sugar, sprinkle 7.5 g yeast, let bloom 5 min
- Add 500 g bread flour and 10 g salt
- Mix to shaggy
- Add 30 g softened butter
- **Knead 8 minutes** on medium-low (stand mixer) or **10–12 min by hand** until **smooth, elastic, slightly tacky**

### 2. First rise (1.5 hours)

- Lightly oil bowl, place dough in, cover
- **Rise 1.5 hours** at room temp until **doubled**

### 3. Shape

For a **regular dome-topped loaf**:

1. Tip dough onto lightly floured bench
2. Press into a rough rectangle ~25 × 35 cm
3. Fold one short edge in by 1/3
4. Fold the other short edge over to overlap
5. Roll up tightly from the now-shorter side (the "spiral" gives even crumb)
6. Pinch seam closed, place seam-side down in **buttered loaf pan**

For a **pullman-style square loaf**:

- Same shaping, but use a 9×4×4 pullman pan with sliding lid
- After shaping, place dough in pan, slide lid on **fully closed** before proofing — yes, fully closed
- The lid forces the dough to expand into a perfectly square cross-section

### 4. Final proof (1 hour)

- **For dome top:** cover with a tea towel; proof until the dough rises **1 cm above the rim of the pan** (~1 hour)
- **For pullman:** lid stays on; proof until you can't quite slide the lid open (about 1 hour) — the dough has filled the pan

### 5. Bake (35–40 min)

- Preheat oven to **190 °C / 375 °F**
- **For dome top:** brush gently with milk for color; bake 35–40 min until deep golden
- **For pullman:** lid stays on the entire bake; bake 40–45 min
- Internal temp: **88–94 °C / 190–200 °F**

### 6. Cool

- **Turn out of pan immediately** after baking — leaving it in the hot pan makes the crust gummy
- **Cool completely** on a rack (at least 1 hour) before slicing
- **Slice with a serrated knife** — sandwich bread cuts cleanly when cool

## Variations

### Whole wheat sandwich loaf

Replace **150 g of bread flour with whole wheat** (so 350 g bread + 150 g whole wheat). Slightly denser but more flavorful. Perfect for tuna salad or peanut butter.

### Honey oat sandwich loaf

- Replace sugar with **40 g honey** (or maple syrup)
- Add **60 g rolled oats** to the dry ingredients
- Sprinkle additional oats on top before baking (after a brush of milk)

### Multigrain sandwich loaf

Add a "soaker" the night before:

- 100 g mixed seeds (sunflower, sesame, flax, pumpkin)
- 100 g hot water
- Soak overnight

Mix into dough alongside reduced water (200 g instead of 250). Adds nutty, hearty character.

### Milk bread (Hokkaido / shokupan style)

Make a **tangzhong** (water roux):

- 25 g flour + 125 g water, cooked to 65 °C, cooled
- Subtract these amounts from the main recipe (475 g flour, 125 g water)
- Add the tangzhong to the dough
- Result: **dramatically softer**, fluffier, longer-lasting bread (the gelatinized starch retains moisture)

This is the technique that makes Hokkaido milk bread so distinctively soft. **Highly recommended.**

### Cinnamon raisin

Roll dough into a 30×40 cm rectangle. Brush with **butter + cinnamon-sugar (40 g brown sugar + 1 tsp cinnamon)** + sprinkle **80 g of raisins**. Roll tightly, place in pan, proof, bake. The kitchen smells like a bakery.

## Storage

- **At room temp in a paper bag or bread box:** 3 days
- **Slice and freeze:** indefinite. Toast straight from freezer for fresh-tasting bread.
- **Don't refrigerate** — refrigerator-stored bread stales faster than room-temp.

## Why DEAM principles still apply

Even with commercial yeast, you can apply DEAM thinking:

- **Long bulk over multiple shorter rises** = more flavor
- **Cold rest after first rise** (4 hours in fridge before shaping) = even more flavor — particularly useful if you want to mix one day and bake the next
- **Lower yeast + more time** = better bread

For a **next-level sandwich loaf**, after step 2 (first rise), **cold-rest the dough 8–12 hours in the fridge** before shaping. The result tastes more developed and slightly tangier — closer to sourdough character without needing a starter.

## Common issues

- **Dense bread.** Underproofed — wait until dough rises 1 cm above pan rim. OR yeast was dead — bloom test next time.
- **Crumb is too crumbly when slicing.** Probably overbaked. Internal temp 92 °C is enough.
- **Crust is too pale.** Underbaked or oven runs cool. Check temp; consider milk wash for more color.
- **Loaf collapsed in oven.** Overproofed. Catch it earlier.
- **Crumb has big holes.** Underkneaded — gluten not developed enough.
- **Loaf is gummy in the middle.** Underbaked. Internal temp matters more than time.

## When to make this vs sourdough

- **You want quick, reliable sandwich bread:** this recipe.
- **You want maximum flavor depth, have time:** sourdough country loaf.
- **You want soft crumb specifically for sandwiches:** this recipe (sourdough sandwich loaves are tougher).
- **You're feeding kids:** definitely this.
