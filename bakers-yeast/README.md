# Baker's yeast

Commercial *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* bread — fast, predictable, and good for things sourdough is overkill for: pizza dough on a Tuesday, last-minute pita, dinner rolls, sandwich loaves.

The chemistry is the same as sourdough (see [`../theory/fermentation.md`](../theory/fermentation.md)), but without LAB the flavor lever is smaller and the timing lever is bigger. You're trading complexity for control.

## Why use baker's yeast

- **Predictable timing.** A 2-hour rise is a 2-hour rise.
- **Mild flavor base.** Lets toppings and add-ins lead (think pizza, brioche cinnamon rolls).
- **No starter to maintain.** Useful for occasional baking.
- **Reliability.** Skill required is lower; fewer ways to fail.

## Where you can still steal from sourdough

- **Cold rest** still lets amylase and protease work — a 24-hr cold pizza dough is dramatically better than a same-day one. **DEAM principles transfer.**
- **Lower yeast + more time** is almost always better-tasting than the opposite.
- **A poolish or biga preferment** overnight gives you most of the flavor depth of sourdough with commercial yeast.
