# Sourdough discard rolls

Soft, golden, dinner-roll-style buns made from **sourdough discard** (the starter you'd otherwise throw away during feeding). Same-day, made in 4 hours from start to finish, dramatically tasty for something built around what's normally trash.

These aren't true sourdough rolls — they use a small amount of **commercial yeast** alongside the discard. The discard provides flavor, the commercial yeast provides reliable lift. **Best of both worlds for a weeknight bake.**

## The discard problem

Every time you feed your starter, you discard most of what was there before adding fresh flour and water. That discard is **flavorful, slightly tangy, full of LAB acid and yeast byproducts** — but **not active enough to leaven bread reliably on its own.**

Most beginners throw discard away. **Don't.** It bakes into amazing things — pancakes, crackers, focaccia, pizza dough, and these rolls. The flavor it adds is part of why it exists.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Mix                    | 10 min        | Room temp   |
| Bulk                   | 1.5 h         | 22–25 °C    |
| Divide + shape         | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Final proof            | 45 min        | Room temp   |
| Bake                   | 18–22 min     | 200 °C      |

About 3 hours start to warm-rolls-on-the-table.

## Formula

For **12 dinner rolls** (each ~80 g):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour                      | 100%     | 500 g |
| Water                            | 50%      | 250 g |
| Whole milk (warm)                | 15%      | 75 g  |
| Sourdough discard (any)          | 30%      | 150 g |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 10 g  |
| Sugar                            | 5%       | 25 g  |
| Butter (softened)                | 8%       | 40 g  |
| Active dry yeast                 | 0.7%     | 3.5 g |
| Egg (for glaze)                  | —        | 1 large |

**Total dough:** ~1050 g → 12 × ~85 g rolls

> **Why these numbers.** 50% water + 15% milk = ~65% effective hydration with milk's slight sweetness and fat. **30% discard** is the upper end of what won't make the dough sluggish — discard is mostly water and starch, not yeast power. **Active dry yeast at 0.7%** does the heavy lifting for reliable rise. Sugar and butter give it the soft, slightly sweet, dinner-roll character.

## The build

### 1. Mix (10 min)

In a large bowl or stand mixer:

- 75 g warm milk + 250 g warm water (~30 °C / 86 °F)
- Sprinkle 3.5 g active dry yeast on top, let bloom 5 min
- Add 150 g sourdough discard, 25 g sugar, 10 g salt → stir
- Add 500 g bread flour
- Mix until shaggy
- Add 40 g softened butter
- **Knead 5–8 minutes** by hand or with dough hook on medium-low until **smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky**

The dough should come together cleanly off the bowl walls when developed.

### 2. Bulk (1.5 h)

- Lightly oil the bowl, place dough back in, cover
- **Rise at room temp 1.5 hours**, until doubled

If your kitchen is cold (<20 °C), give it 2–2.5 hours.

### 3. Divide and shape

1. **Tip dough onto lightly floured bench**
2. **Divide into 12 equal pieces** (~85 g each — use a scale)
3. **Shape each into a tight ball:**
   - Cup the dough piece in your palm against the bench
   - Drag it gently in a circular motion to build surface tension
   - You'll feel it tighten into a smooth, taut ball

### 4. Final proof (45 min)

- **Place balls in a buttered 9×13 baking dish or sheet pan**, spaced about 1 cm apart so they touch when risen
- Cover with a tea towel
- **Proof 45 min** at room temp until **doubled and touching**

### 5. Egg wash and bake

1. **Beat 1 egg with 1 tbsp water** in a small bowl
2. **Brush the tops of all rolls** generously
3. Optional: sprinkle with **flaky salt, sesame seeds, poppy seeds, or everything-bagel seasoning**
4. **Bake at 200 °C / 400 °F for 18–22 min** until **deep golden brown**

Internal temp ~88–92 °C / 190–198 °F when done.

### 6. Cool briefly, eat warm

- **Cool in pan 5 min**, then transfer to a rack
- **Best within an hour** of baking — the soft, warm, slightly sweet character is the point

## Variations

### Garlic-herb pull-apart rolls

After shaping, brush the bottom of the pan with **garlic-infused olive oil + chopped fresh rosemary**. Roll the balls briefly through the oil before placing. Bake as normal. Pull apart while warm.

### Cheese rolls

Push **a cube of sharp cheddar (or gruyere)** into the center of each ball before final proof. Cheese melts into the roll during baking.

### Sweet rolls (cinnamon style)

Reduce salt to 6 g, increase sugar to 50 g, add 1 tsp cinnamon to the dry mix. Brush with **butter + cinnamon-sugar** before baking. Borderline cinnamon rolls — without the spiral.

### Burger buns

Make 8 larger pieces (~130 g each) instead of 12. Shape into slightly flattened balls. Bake same way. Brush with butter when fresh out of the oven for extra softness.

### Hot dog buns

Shape pieces into elongated cylinders (~100 g each), 8 buns total. Place in a pan touching to encourage tall, soft sides. Same bake.

## Other discard recipes (quick mention)

If you have lots of discard piling up, beyond rolls:

- **Sourdough pancakes** — equal parts discard + flour + milk + an egg. Cook like pancakes. Tangy, fluffy.
- **Sourdough crackers** — discard + a little flour + olive oil + salt + herbs. Roll thin, bake at 180 °C until crisp. Excellent.
- **Discard pizza dough** — 200 g discard + 300 g flour + 180 g water + 8 g salt + small commercial yeast. Same-day pizza.
- **Discard waffles** — like pancakes but in a waffle iron. Crispy outside, tangy inside.
- **Discard biscuits / scones** — replace some of the buttermilk with discard.

## Why DEAM enables this

DEAM's once-a-week feeding rhythm produces **less discard than daily-feeding** approaches — so when you do have discard, it's worth using rather than throwing away. The discard from a cold-fed levain has spent days developing flavor; it's especially good for these rolls.

If you're following the [course](../course/), you'll have discard from week 1 (building the starter) and from weekly maintenance. Use it.

## Common issues

- **Rolls didn't rise.** Yeast was old/dead, or water too hot (killed the yeast). Bloom in 30 °C water; should foam in 5 min. If not, replace yeast.
- **Rolls are too dense.** Underproofed at final, or kneaded too short. Knead 8 min; proof until doubled.
- **Rolls are tough.** Overworked the dough, or overbaked. Knead until smooth, not until tough. Internal temp 90 °C is enough.
- **Rolls spread instead of rising up.** Surface tension wasn't enough during shaping. Practice the cupped-palm shaping technique.
- **Tops burned before insides cooked.** Oven runs hot or rack too high. Use middle rack; check at 18 min.

## Why these are great

A pan of warm sourdough discard rolls solves several problems at once:

- **Uses discard productively** instead of trash
- **Same-day** — no overnight cold rest needed
- **Beginner-friendly** — commercial yeast = predictable, easier than pure sourdough
- **Crowd-pleasing** — soft, golden, slightly sweet rolls are universally beloved
- **Builds bread-baking skills** without the patience requirement of pure sourdough

A perfect Tuesday-night recipe. Rolls in the oven by 6 PM, on the table for dinner.
