# Baguettes (DEAM)

French-style baguettes built on a **3-day-old cold-fed levain straight from the fridge**, a long early cold rest, and a short bulk. The kind of dough you can start the night before and finish on a slow afternoon.

This is the worked example behind one of the cover photos in the original DEAM doc. Apply the [method](../methods/double-enzymatic-activation.md) — this is the recipe-shaped version of it.

## At a glance

| Phase                    | When                | Time     | Temp        |
|--------------------------|---------------------|----------|-------------|
| Pre-ferment wake-up      | Morning of bake     | ~1 h     | Room temp   |
| Final mix + cold rest    | Same morning        | 8–14 h   | 4–6 °C      |
| Warm up + bulk + folds   | Afternoon of bake   | 3–4 h    | 22–26 °C    |
| Shape + bench rest       | Late afternoon      | 20–30 min| Room temp   |
| Final proof              | Before bake         | 30–60 min| Room temp (or cold proof, see below) |
| Bake                     | Evening             | 18–22 min| 250 °C → 230 °C with steam |

You can split this across two days: cold-rest overnight, bulk and bake the next day after work.

## Formula (baker's percentages)

For **3 baguettes (~280 g each)** — adjust as you like.

| Ingredient            | Bakers % | Grams |
|-----------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour (~12–13% protein) | 95% | 475 g |
| Whole wheat or rye    | 5%       | 25 g  |
| Water                 | 72%      | 360 g |
| Salt                  | 2.0%     | 10 g  |
| Cold-fed levain (100% hydration) | 12% | 60 g |

**Total dough weight:** ~930 g → 3 × 310 g pieces (a touch over 280 g final, accounting for handling loss).

> **Why these numbers.** 72% hydration is forgiving for hand-shaping baguettes. 12% levain is the DEAM low-inoculation lever — slower, more flavor, more flexibility. 5% wholegrain in the dough adds enzymes and a faint nutty edge without making the crumb dark.

## The build

### 1. Levain — already prepped

Use a cold-fed levain that's been in the fridge **2–4 days**. See [`../starter.md`](../starter.md) for the weekly schedule. Pull it straight from the fridge — no warm-up needed.

### 2. Pre-ferment wake-up (morning of bake, ~1 h)

- In a large bowl: 360 g water + 60 g cold-fed levain → stir until the levain dissolves.
- Add ~150 g of the bread flour. Mix to a thick batter (poolish-style, ~100% hydration).
- Sprinkle the salt on top of the remaining 350 g of flour in another bowl. Set aside.
- Cover the wake-up mix. Wait ~1 h, until you see active bubbling.

### 3. Final mix + early cold rest (8–14 h, 4–6 °C)

- Add the salted flour to the wake-up mix. Use the rye/whole wheat as part of this.
- Mix by hand or with a spatula until shaggy and no dry flour remains. Don't try to develop gluten yet — the cold rest will do most of that.
- Let it rest 20 min at room temp, then do **one** stretch-and-fold to bring the dough together.
- Cover and put it straight into the fridge for the **early cold rest**.

> This is the enzymatic priming phase. Amylase is freeing maltose; protease is barely active because the pH hasn't dropped. You'll come back to a smooth, extensible dough that's already half-developed.

### 4. Bulk + folds (3–4 h, 22–26 °C)

- Pull the dough out. Let it warm up for 30–45 min.
- **Fold 1** (~30 min in): firm stretch-and-fold to build foundation strength.
- **Folds 2–4** (every 45 min – 1 h): gentle coil folds.
- Watch the dough, not the clock. Done when it's puffy, jiggly, holds bubbles at the edges, and has gained ~30–50% in volume. **At 75 °F (24 °C) on primed dough, 3 h is realistic.**

### 5. Pre-shape, rest, shape

- Tip the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Divide into 3 equal pieces (~310 g each).
- Pre-shape into loose cylinders. Bench rest **20–30 min** seam-side-up.
- Final shape into baguettes: gently degas, fold the top down, roll up tight, seal the seam, then roll out from center to ends to lengthen. Don't squeeze — let the dough do the stretching.
- Transfer seam-down to a floured couche or parchment with pleats between the loaves.

### 6. Final proof (two options)

**Option A — same evening:** 30–60 min at room temp. Ready when a poke springs back slowly.

**Option B — overnight cold proof:** straight into the fridge, uncovered or loosely covered. Bake cold the next morning. Better blistering and a deeper crust color (see the [late cold rest notes in `../starter.md`](../starter.md)). Shorten the early cold rest to 8–10 h if you go this route — protease activity stacks across both colds.

### 7. Bake (18–22 min)

- Preheat oven to **250 °C** with a baking stone or heavy tray, plus a steam pan on the bottom rack.
- Boil water for steam.
- Transfer baguettes to a peel or inverted tray on parchment. **Score** with 3–4 long, shallow, overlapping diagonal slashes per loaf — about 30° from the centerline.
- Slide onto the stone. Pour boiling water into the steam pan, close immediately.
- **Bake with steam: 10 min at 250 °C.**
- Vent the steam (open the door briefly), drop to **230 °C, bake another 8–12 min** until deep golden.
- Cool on a rack at least 30 min before tearing in.

## Tuning levers

- **Want darker crust + more blister?** Use the overnight cold proof. The chilled dough surface dries slightly, traps gas, and blisters when steam hits.
- **Want sweeter crust?** Lengthen the early cold rest to 14 h+. More residual sugar after fermentation.
- **Want more open crumb?** Bump hydration to 75–78% (need confidence with sticky dough handling). Use a stronger flour.
- **Want more tang?** Lower the levain to 10% and stretch the cold proof to 18+ hours. Stiffer levain (50–65% hydration) also helps.

## Troubleshooting

- **Crumb is gummy.** Underbaked, or amylase paradox (too much — see [`../../theory/enzymes.md`](../../theory/enzymes.md)). Bake longer or shorten the cold rest.
- **No oven spring.** Over-proofed final, or under-fermented bulk. The classic poke test: should spring back slowly, not pop right back or stay dented.
- **Pale crust.** Underbaked, or sugar all consumed by fermentation. Shorten bulk slightly or bake hotter to start.
- **Tight, dense crumb.** Under-fermented, over-shaped (degassed too aggressively), or hydration too low for your flour.
