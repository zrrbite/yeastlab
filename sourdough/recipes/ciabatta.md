# Sourdough ciabatta

The "slipper bread" — Italian, soft, full of irregular alveoli, with a crackling crust. **Ciabatta is what high-hydration dough wants to be when it grows up.** Demands wet-hand technique and confidence; pays back with bread that looks like a magazine photo.

DEAM-style ciabatta uses a **stiffer cold-fed levain** (60–70% hydration) for more acetic-acid character, paired with a **wetter final dough** (80–85% hydration) for the open crumb.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Pre-ferment wake-up    | 1 h           | Room temp   |
| Mix + autolyse         | 30 min        | Room temp   |
| Cold rest              | 8–12 h        | 4 °C (overnight) |
| Bulk + folds           | 3–4 h         | 22–24 °C    |
| Divide + minimal shape | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Final proof            | 45–60 min     | Room temp   |
| Bake                   | 22–25 min     | 240 °C      |

## Formula

For **2 large ciabatta loaves** (each ~440 g):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour (high protein)       | 100%     | 500 g |
| Water                            | 82%      | 410 g |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 10 g  |
| Olive oil                        | 2%       | 10 g  |
| Cold-fed levain (~70% hydration) | 12%      | 60 g  |

**Total dough:** ~990 g → 2 × ~440 g loaves

> **Why these numbers.** 82% hydration is **on the high end** for hand-handling but it's what gives ciabatta its dramatic open crumb. The stiffer-than-usual levain (~70% hydration) pushes more acetic acid character — the slight tang that's part of classic ciabatta. Olive oil keeps the crust slightly tender despite the high heat.

## Stiffer levain prep

If your normal cold-fed levain is **100% hydration** (1:1 flour:water), make a **stiff version specifically for ciabatta**:

- 25 g existing starter + 50 g flour + 35 g water (1:2:1.4 ratio = ~70% hydration)
- Pinch of whole wheat / rye
- Cover, ferment 6–8 h at room temp
- **Refrigerate when active** — use within 5 days

Or skip this and use your normal cold-fed levain at 100% — the ciabatta will be slightly less tangy but still excellent.

## The build

### 1. Pre-ferment wake-up (1 h)

- 410 g water + 60 g levain → stir to dissolve
- Add 200 g of the bread flour → stir to a thick batter
- Set remaining 300 g flour + 10 g salt + 10 g olive oil aside
- Cover, wait 1 h until visibly bubbling

### 2. Mix + autolyse

- Add the salted flour and olive oil to the wake-up
- Mix by hand until shaggy and no dry flour remains — **don't try to develop gluten yet**
- Cover, rest 30 min

### 3. Cold rest (8–12 h)

- Quick stretch-and-fold (just to gather the dough)
- Cover and into the fridge **overnight**

This is the DEAM enzymatic phase. By morning the dough should be smooth, somewhat puffed, and visibly more developed than when you mixed.

### 4. Bulk + folds (3–4 h)

- Pull dough from fridge, let warm 45 min
- **Wet your hands** (always, for ciabatta)
- 1 stretch-and-fold to bring it together
- Wait 45 min
- 1 coil fold → wait 45 min
- 1 coil fold → wait 45 min
- 1 final gentle coil fold

By end of bulk the dough should be:

- **Puffy and jiggly** with **visible bubbles** at the surface and edges
- Gained ~50–80% volume (more than typical bread dough — ciabatta wants the gas)
- **Soft and stretchy**, almost batter-like

If it's still tight, give it another 30–45 min.

### 5. Divide and "non-shape"

This is the fun part. Ciabatta is **deliberately not shaped** in the traditional sense.

1. **Heavy dust of flour on the bench**
2. **Generously dust the top of the dough** while it's still in the bowl
3. **Tip the dough out gently** — try not to deflate
4. **Use a bench scraper** to divide into 2 equal pieces — long rectangles, like loose slippers (hence "ciabatta" — Italian for slipper)
5. **Lift each piece carefully** and place on a piece of parchment paper, **stretching gently** to roughly 25 cm × 10 cm rectangles

Don't tuck. Don't shape. Don't tighten. Ciabatta wants to be **loose, gassy, irregular**.

### 6. Final proof (45–60 min)

- Cover loosely with a tea towel
- Rest 45–60 min at room temp until **noticeably puffed and jiggly**

### 7. Bake (22–25 min)

#### Setup

- **Pizza stone or baking steel on the middle rack**, preheated to **240 °C / 465 °F** for 30+ min
- **Steam tray on the bottom rack** with hot water, OR a small cast-iron skillet you can pour boiling water into right before loading

#### The bake

1. **Slide each ciabatta on its parchment** onto the hot stone using a peel
2. **Pour 100 mL boiling water** into the steam tray, **shut the door immediately**
3. **Bake 12 min with steam**
4. **Carefully open the oven, remove the steam tray** (use a long oven mitt)
5. **Bake another 10–13 min** until **deeply golden** and the crust **crackles** when squeezed gently

Internal temp should be ~95–98 °C / 205–208 °F when done.

### 8. Cool

**At least 30 min on a rack.** Slice with a serrated knife — soft crumb, big irregular holes, crackling crust.

## What "good ciabatta" looks like

- **Crust:** golden, crackling, slightly chewy
- **Crumb:** very open with **dramatic irregular holes** — some thumb-sized
- **Texture:** soft, slightly elastic, with audible crackle when you tear it
- **Flavor:** mildly tangy, complex, slightly nutty
- **Mouthfeel:** moist but not gummy

## Variations

### Olive ciabatta

Add **120 g of pitted, halved Kalamata or Castelvetrano olives** during the last fold. Distribute by gentle folding. Phenomenal with olive oil and crusty cheese.

### Herb ciabatta

Add **15 g chopped fresh rosemary + 10 g chopped fresh thyme** during the last fold. Pairs incredibly with cured meats.

### Garlic ciabatta

Add **6 cloves of finely minced garlic + 5 g salt-cured anchovies (chopped)** during the last fold. Don't fight me on the anchovies — they melt into the bread and add umami without "fishy."

### Tomato ciabatta

Use **half-and-half water and homemade tomato passata** for the dough (so 200 g passata + 210 g water). Adds a subtle tomato character and gorgeous orange tint to the crumb.

## Common issues

- **Crumb is tight, not open.** Under-fermented at bulk. Bulk longer next time. Watch for big visible bubbles at the surface.
- **Loaf collapsed when transferred to the oven.** Over-proofed or over-handled. Be more confident with the transfer; less hesitation.
- **Crust is pale.** Underbaked, or oven not hot enough. Bake longer; verify oven temp.
- **Crust didn't crackle.** Insufficient steam. Use more boiling water, or skip removing the steam tray.
- **Dough was too sticky to handle.** Either hydration too high for your flour (drop to 78%), or you didn't flour the bench enough. Flour generously — ciabatta is the one bread where you don't need to fight stickiness.
- **Bread feels gummy.** Underbaked. Internal temp must hit ~95 °C.

## Why DEAM ciabatta works

The cold rest phase does for ciabatta what it does for baguettes:

- **Builds enzymatic flavor depth** that 4-hour same-day ciabatta can't match
- **Develops gluten passively** so the very high hydration becomes manageable
- **Prevents over-aggressive bulk** by giving the dough a head start

A same-day ciabatta needs aggressive folding and lots of patience to reach the structure DEAM gives you for free overnight.
