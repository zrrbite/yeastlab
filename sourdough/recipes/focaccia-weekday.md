# Weekday focaccia

This process leverages a busy schedule. On weekends you can simply cut some of the cold-retard periods to suit the day.

Spread across **1–2 days**.

## Day 1 — morning or evening (after work)

- Take some fresh cold-fed levain.
- Mix dough as usual at night; store cold until you get home from work the next day.
- (Or do it in the morning for a shorter cold rest. Either is fine.)

## Day 2 afternoon — when home from work

- Take dough out, let it reach room temp (~1 h). Give it one stretch.
- **Laminate** and add pre-infused garlic oil and rosemary oil to each layer.
- Ferment as usual for **3–4 hours total at room temp**, with **3 coil folds**.
- Transfer to a flat / wide container, well oiled (use garlic oil etc.), for final rest (30 min – 1 h), then into the fridge.

## Day 3 — whenever you bake

- Take the dough out **2–3 hours before baking** to let it do its rise and become bubbly. (If you don't have time, just ferment longer the day before.)
- Pour dough out onto flat baking paper or paper in a tray. Not worth fighting stickiness with excessive oil.
- Add garlic oil and rosemary oil on top. Be generous with flaky salt. **Fresh rosemary is the most fragrant.**
- Pop any large bubbles or they'll just get burnt.
- Press oil into the dough with fingers. Final final rise: ~30 min.
- **Bake 10–15 min with steam** (`varm luft` / convection).
- Optional: take it out and baste more oil if needed. Easier to get an even coating once the crust has set.
- **Bake another 10–15 min without steam** (over/under heat).

## Notes

- This is a framework, not a recipe. Use any base focaccia hydration you like (75–85% works well with strong flour).
- The key DEAM moves are present: cold-fed levain, long cold rest, low effort. The rest is just toppings and timing.
- Garlic-rosemary oil keeps in the fridge for weeks. Make a big batch.
