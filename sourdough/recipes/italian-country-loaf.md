# Italian-style country loaf

The "no shaping, no scoring, no BS" loaf featured in the original DEAM document — a rustic Italian-style boule that maximizes flavor and structure with **minimum shaping effort**. The kind of loaf you make when you have time for the bake but not for technique.

Built on a **4-day-old cold-fed levain** for deep tang, with **4 hours of bulk** and a single fold to bring it together. The result is a deeply-flavored, irregularly-crumbed, beautifully-imperfect loaf.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Pre-ferment wake-up    | 1 h           | Room temp   |
| Mix + cold rest        | 8–14 h        | 4 °C (overnight) |
| Bulk                   | 4 h           | 22–24 °C    |
| Single fold + light shape | 5 min      | Room temp   |
| Bench rest             | 30 min        | Room temp   |
| Bake                   | 45 min        | 245 °C      |

A bake that fits into a relaxed weekend morning.

## Formula

For **1 large country loaf** (~880 g):

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour                      | 90%      | 450 g |
| Whole wheat or rye               | 10%      | 50 g  |
| Water                            | 75%      | 375 g |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 10 g  |
| Cold-fed levain (100% hydration) | 12%      | 60 g  |

**Total dough:** ~945 g → 1 loaf

> **Why these numbers.** 75% hydration is the rustic country-loaf sweet spot — open crumb without being unmanageable. The 10% whole wheat or rye adds depth, enzyme activity, and the slight gray-brown color that tells everyone "this is a real bread." 12% inoculation, slightly lower than baguettes, encourages the longer flavor-development window.

## The build

### Use a more mature levain

For this recipe, **use a cold-fed levain that's been in the fridge 3–5 days** (not 1–2). The longer fridge time builds more acetic acid character, giving the loaf a slightly tangier, deeper flavor. This is one of the deliberate moves of this recipe.

### 1. Pre-ferment wake-up (1 h)

- 375 g water + 60 g levain (3–5 day old) → stir to dissolve
- Add 150 g of the bread flour → stir to a thick batter
- Set remaining 300 g flour + 50 g whole wheat + 10 g salt aside
- Cover, wait 1 h until visibly bubbling

### 2. Mix and cold rest (overnight)

- Add the salted flour mix to the wake-up
- Mix by hand 1–2 min until shaggy and no dry flour remains
- Cover, rest 30 min at room temp
- 1 stretch-and-fold (4 lifts)
- Cover and into the fridge **overnight (8–14 hours)**

### 3. Bulk fermentation (4 h)

- Pull dough from fridge, let warm 45–60 min at room temp
- 1 stretch-and-fold (4 lifts) at the start
- Wait 1 h
- 1 coil fold
- Wait 1 h
- 1 coil fold
- Wait 1 h
- 1 final coil fold
- Total bulk: ~4 hours from removing from fridge

By end of bulk:

- Dough should be **noticeably puffed** (gained 50%+)
- **Visible bubbles** at the surface and edges
- **Smooth and stretchy** when handled
- **Slightly tangy aroma** — the older levain working

### 4. The "non-shape" — fold and seal

This is the punch-line of "no shaping, no scoring, no BS":

1. **Lightly flour the bench**
2. **Tip the dough out gently**
3. **Fold it once over itself** — top-to-bottom, like closing a book
4. **Seal the seam** with the side of your hand
5. **Place seam-side down** on a piece of parchment paper
6. **Bench rest 30 min** uncovered

That's the entire shape. No tucking, no tightening, no stitching the bottom. The dough is allowed to relax into its natural form.

### 5. Bake (45 min)

#### Setup

- **Dutch oven (4–6 qt) with lid** in oven, preheating at **245 °C / 475 °F** for **30–45 min**

#### The bake

1. **Lift the loaf with parchment** into the screaming-hot Dutch oven
2. **Replace the lid immediately**
3. **Bake 22 min covered** — steam from the dough creates the oven-spring environment
4. **Remove lid. Drop temp to 225 °C / 440 °F.**
5. **Bake another 22–25 min** until **deep golden-brown**

No scoring needed. The loaf will **crack open naturally** along its top — irregular, unique, beautiful in its imperfection. **This is part of the style.**

### 6. Cool

**At least 45 min on a rack** before slicing. Patience is part of the recipe.

## What "good country loaf" looks like

- **Crust:** dark golden-brown, **with one or more natural cracks** across the top from oven spring
- **Shape:** somewhere between round and oval — irregular, organic
- **Crumb:** open with mixed hole sizes; not as wild as baguettes or ciabatta, more "honeycomb"
- **Flavor:** **deeper and tangier than typical** — the older levain shows
- **Mouthfeel:** chewy, slightly elastic, with a satisfying tear

## Why this style works

The Italian-style country loaf relies on **three things working together:**

1. **The older levain** (3–5 days cold) brings more acetic acid and more depth than a fresh levain
2. **The long enzymatic cold rest** primes the dough for a confident, fast bulk
3. **The minimal shaping** lets the dough express its natural structure — irregular crumb, organic shape, no over-tightened gluten

It's a loaf designed for **bakers who care about flavor more than aesthetics**. Or for bakers who don't have time for shaping. Or for bakers who realize after bake #20 that "perfect shape" doesn't actually make the bread taste better.

## Variations

### More tangy

- Use a 4–5 day levain
- Push the cold rest to 16–18 hours
- Bulk slightly cooler (20–22 °C)

### More flavor / more rustic

- Bump whole grain to 20–25%
- Add a small amount of rye specifically (not just whole wheat) — rye has higher enzyme activity

### Larger loaf

- Scale up to 750 g flour total → ~1450 g dough → 1 large boule
- Bake 50–55 min total

### Two smaller loaves

- Divide before final shape
- Bake either in two Dutch ovens, or one after another (the second loaf benefits from a slightly cooler oven, ~225 °C)

## Common issues

- **Crust didn't crack open / no oven spring.** Either over-proofed at bulk (too much) or under-proofed (not enough gas). Watch the dough — should look puffy and jiggly before final shape.
- **Crumb is too tight.** Bulk too short, or inoculation too low for this timing. Bulk an extra 30–60 min.
- **Loaf collapsed during transfer.** Over-proofed at bench. Cut bench rest to 20 min.
- **Tastes too sour.** Levain was too old (>5 days), or cold rest too long. Use a younger levain or shorter cold rest.
- **Tastes too mild.** Levain was too young (<2 days), or cold rest too short. Use an older levain.
