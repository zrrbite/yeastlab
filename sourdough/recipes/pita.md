# Sourdough pita

Soft, puffy, pocketed flatbreads — the kind that fill up like balloons in a screaming-hot oven, then deflate into perfect sandwich pockets. **Sourdough pita uses the same DEAM-prepared cold-fed levain as the rest of the workflow**; the differences are the recipe ratios and the bake.

Bake-day ready in **3 hours from a prepped levain.**

## At a glance

| Phase                  | Time          | Temp        |
|------------------------|---------------|-------------|
| Pre-ferment wake-up    | ~1 h          | Room temp   |
| Mix + first rest       | 30 min        | Room temp   |
| Bulk + folds           | 2 h           | 22–26 °C    |
| Divide + shape balls   | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Bench rest             | 20 min        | Room temp   |
| Roll + final rest      | 15 min        | Room temp   |
| Bake                   | 90 sec each   | 280 °C+     |

Total: ~4 hours from start to first pocket.

## Formula

For **8 pitas** (each ~80 g), enough to feed 4 hungry people:

| Ingredient                       | Bakers % | Grams |
|----------------------------------|---------:|------:|
| Bread flour                      | 95%      | 380 g |
| Whole wheat flour                | 5%       | 20 g  |
| Water                            | 60%      | 240 g |
| Salt                             | 2%       | 8 g   |
| Olive oil                        | 3%       | 12 g  |
| Cold-fed levain (100% hydration) | 12%      | 48 g  |

**Total dough:** ~708 g → 8 × ~85 g balls

> **Why these numbers.** 60% hydration is the pita sweet spot — wet enough to stretch thin without tearing, dry enough to handle. The 5% whole wheat adds enzymatic activity and a touch of nutty depth. Olive oil keeps the crumb soft after the dramatic puff-and-collapse cycle.

## The build

### 1. Pre-ferment wake-up (~1 h)

Pull a [cold-fed levain](../starter.md) (1–4 days old) from the fridge.

- 240 g water + 48 g levain → stir to dissolve
- Add 100 g of the bread flour → stir to a thick batter
- Set remaining 280 g flour + 20 g whole wheat + 8 g salt + 12 g olive oil aside
- Cover, wait ~1 h until visibly bubbling

### 2. Mix final dough

- Add the salted-flour mix and olive oil to the wake-up
- Mix by hand 3–5 min until smooth and cohesive — pita dough wants more development than bread dough
- Cover, rest 30 min

### 3. Bulk + folds (~2 h)

- 1 stretch-and-fold at 30 min
- 1 coil fold at 60 min
- 1 coil fold at 90 min
- Done when dough is smooth, slightly puffed, gained ~30% volume

### 4. Divide and pre-shape (15 min)

- Tip dough onto lightly floured bench
- Divide into 8 equal pieces (use a scale — ~85 g each)
- Pre-shape each into a tight ball: pinch seam underneath, give it a tight tuck
- **Bench rest 20 min covered** — critical step; lets gluten relax for rolling

### 5. Roll and final rest

- Heavy dust of flour on bench
- Roll each ball into a **thin disc, ~3 mm thick, ~16 cm diameter**
- Roll **evenly** — uneven thickness means uneven puff
- Place on parchment paper or a floured surface
- **Rest 15 min covered** — lets the gluten relax slightly so the dough doesn't snap back when it hits the heat

> **The thickness test:** if you can lift a rolled disc by an edge and it droops slightly under its own weight without tearing, you're at the right thickness. Too stiff = too thick = won't puff. Too floppy = too thin = will puff but tear.

### 6. Bake (90 sec each)

The bake is what makes pita pita. **You need maximum heat and maximum heat retention.**

#### Setup

- **Pizza stone or steel on the highest rack**, heated **45 minutes minimum** at **max oven temp (typically 280–290 °C / 535–550 °F)**
- Optional: turn on broiler for the last 5 minutes of preheating to push stone temp higher

#### The bake

1. **Slide one pita onto the hot stone** using a peel.
2. **Wait 30 seconds.** It should start to bubble and lift.
3. **At ~45–60 sec**, the pita should puff into a **dramatic balloon** — full, round, pocket forming inside.
4. **At 90 sec**, pull it out. Edges should be lightly colored, not brown.
5. **Wrap in a clean towel** as you bake the next one — keeps them soft.

If a pita doesn't puff:

- **Stone wasn't hot enough** (most common cause)
- Pita rolled too thick, or too thin
- Edges sealed unevenly during rolling — air escapes before puffing
- Dough was too cold — should be at room temp when it hits the stone

## Stove-top alternative (no stone, no oven)

If you don't have a baking stone:

- Use a **dry cast-iron skillet on max heat** for 10 minutes to preheat
- Slap pitas on one at a time, **30 sec per side, then flip and 30 sec more**
- They'll puff dramatically — sometimes even better than oven-baked
- Wrap in towel as you go

This is actually how pita is often made in homes across the Middle East — the oven version is a Western adaptation.

## Storage

- **Eat warm** if at all possible — best within 2 hours of baking
- Store in a **paper bag at room temp** for 1–2 days; reheat briefly under broiler or in a dry skillet
- Freeze excess immediately — pita freezes well; thaw and reheat for almost-fresh results

## Variations

### Whole wheat pita

Bump whole wheat to 30%. Slightly denser pocket but more flavor. Excellent for dipping.

### Garlic-herb pita

Brush rolled discs with garlic-infused olive oil before baking. Sprinkle za'atar after baking.

### Stuffed pita (the cheat code)

Roll discs slightly larger (~18 cm), spread filling (cheese + herbs, or chopped olives) on half, fold over, seal edges, bake. Result: instant cheese-stuffed flatbread. Very satisfying.

### Smaller / "tea" pitas

Make 12 smaller (~55 g) balls instead of 8 larger ones. Bake 60 sec each. Great for appetizers or kids.

## Common issues

- **Pitas don't puff.** Stone not hot enough (#1 cause). Use an oven thermometer to verify temp. Crank up. Use broiler.
- **Pitas tear when rolling.** Dough wasn't rested enough; gluten too tight. Bench rest after dividing, then rest again after rolling.
- **Pitas burn before puffing.** Stone too hot, or dough too thin. Or your oven is closer to broiler than you think.
- **Pitas puff but pockets don't form.** Possibly under-fermented; the dough lacks gas. Bulk longer next time.
- **Pitas are tough/stiff after cooling.** Probably overbaked, or rolled too thick. 90 seconds is the target; longer dries them out.

## Why DEAM works for pita

Cold-fed levain pita has:

- **More flavor** than commercial-yeast pita (which can taste flat)
- **Faster bulk** because the levain is already primed (3 hours vs the 6+ for non-DEAM sourdough pita)
- **Same starter and rhythm** as your other DEAM bakes — no special starter needed

The whole pita workflow drops cleanly into your weekly DEAM rhythm. **Pull a levain Tuesday morning, eat pita Tuesday evening.**
