# Neapolitan pizza dough (DEAM)

Soft, tender, slightly chewy with a puffed leopard-spotted *cornicione* (rim). Built for a **60–90 second bake at 430–500 °C** in a wood, gas, or pellet oven. Sourdough version of a classic Naples-style dough, primed by a long cold rest so a small levain does big flavor work.

If you don't have a 450 °C+ oven, see the [NY-style recipe](pizza-ny.md) instead — it's tuned for home-oven temperatures and will give you a better result than a half-Neapolitan.

## What "Neapolitan" actually means here

- **Soft, supple dough.** Lower hydration than open-crumb sourdough — usually 60–65%. Higher would just slack out at these bake temps.
- **00 flour.** Very fine grind, moderate-to-high protein (W 280–320 ish for long ferments). Caputo Cuoco, Pizzeria, or Chef's Flour are all classics. Don't substitute bread flour — it browns differently.
- **No oil. No sugar.** Dough should taste like flour, fermentation, salt.
- **Very high heat.** The whole point of the recipe is the 60–90 sec leopard char from a screaming-hot deck.
- **Hand-stretched.** No rolling pin. Preserve the gas in the rim.

## At a glance

| Phase                  | When            | Time     | Temp        |
|------------------------|-----------------|----------|-------------|
| Pre-ferment wake-up    | Day before      | ~1 h     | Room temp   |
| Final mix              | Day before      | 15 min   | Room temp   |
| Bulk + folds           | Day before      | 2–3 h    | 22–24 °C    |
| Ball + cold rest       | Day before → bake day | 24–48 h | 4 °C |
| Final proof            | Bake day        | 2–4 h    | Room temp   |
| Bake                   | Showtime        | 60–90 s  | 430–500 °C  |

The cold rest is where the magic happens — that's the long DEAM-style enzymatic priming phase, just done after balling instead of before bulk.

## Formula (baker's percentages)

For **6 × 250 g balls** (10–12" pies):

| Ingredient            | Bakers % | Grams |
|-----------------------|---------:|------:|
| Tipo 00 flour (W 280–320) | 100% | 875 g |
| Water                 | 62%      | 543 g |
| Salt                  | 2.8%    | 24 g  |
| Cold-fed levain (100% hydration) | 8% | 70 g |

**Total dough weight:** ~1512 g → 6 × 250 g balls.

> **Why these numbers.** 62% hydration is the Neapolitan sweet spot — soft enough to stretch, firm enough to hold a topping at high heat. 2.8% salt is intentional, both for flavor and to slow the ferment over the long cold rest. 8% levain is *very* low — possible only because the cold rest is long enough for enzymes to do most of the priming work.

## Levain

Use a cold-fed levain that's been in the fridge **2–4 days**. See [`../starter.md`](../starter.md). 8% is a tiny inoculation; the levain needs to be alive but doesn't need to be at peak.

## The build

### 1. Pre-ferment wake-up (~1 h, day before bake)

- Combine **all the water** with the **70 g levain**. Stir until dissolved.
- Add **~150 g of the flour**. Stir to a thick batter. Cover.
- Let it sit ~1 h until you see active bubbling.

### 2. Final mix (15 min)

- Add the remaining flour and the salt to the wake-up mix.
- Mix by hand or in a stand mixer with a dough hook on low for 5–7 min, until the dough is smooth and pulls cleanly from the bowl. Should be tacky but not sticky.

### 3. Bulk + folds (2–3 h at 22–24 °C)

- Cover and bulk on the counter. **2 light coil folds** spaced 45 min apart in the first 90 min — this is enough for low-hydration dough.
- Done when puffy and ~30% larger.

### 4. Ball and cold rest (24–48 h at 4 °C)

- Divide into **6 × 250 g pieces**. Ball each one tightly — pinch the seam underneath, give it a tight tuck on the bench.
- Place in an oiled (very lightly) sealed container with at least 2× headroom. A pizza ball box is ideal; a wide tupperware works.
- **Straight into the fridge for 24–48 h.**

> This is the enzymatic phase. Amylase keeps liberating maltose at 4 °C, building sugar reserves and complex flavor. The cold also keeps the dough soft and easy to stretch later. **48 h is the sweet spot** — past 72 h protease starts to get loud and the dough goes slack.

### 5. Final proof (2–4 h at room temp on bake day)

- Pull the balls out **2–4 h before baking**, depending on how cold your kitchen is.
- They should look puffy and relaxed, jiggle when you nudge the container, and the dough should yield to a gentle press without snapping back hard.
- Don't over-proof at this stage — they'll deflate when you stretch them and you'll lose the puff in the rim.

### 6. Stretch

- Heavy dust of semolina or 00 on the bench.
- Slide a ball out **gently** — don't degas the rim. Press flat from the center outward with fingertips, leaving a 1 cm puffed rim untouched.
- Stretch by lifting and turning over knuckles, or by hand on the bench with a gentle pull-and-rotate. Aim for **10–12" / 25–30 cm**, ~3–4 mm thick in the center.

### 7. Top

- Less is more. Classic margherita: thin layer of crushed San Marzano tomato (raw, just salt), a handful of torn fresh mozzarella (drained, blotted), a few basil leaves *after* baking, drizzle of EVOO before going in.
- Heavy toppings will steam the dough and you'll lose the puff. Trust the simplicity.

### 8. Bake (60–90 s at 430–500 °C)

- Preheat your oven to its max for **at least 45 min** with the stone or steel fully heated.
- Launch the pie on a peel dusted with semolina.
- **At 480–500 °C: 60–75 sec.** Rotate once with the peel after ~30 sec for even charring.
- **At 430–450 °C: 90 sec.**
- The crust should have the iconic *leopard spots* — small dark patches across a puffy rim. Mozzarella should be molten with a few small char marks. Bottom should be flexible but spotted.

If you don't have a pizza oven, this dough will *survive* a home oven on max + steel preheated to 290 °C, but the bake will be longer (4–6 min) and you won't get the leopard. Honestly: use the [NY-style recipe](pizza-ny.md) for home ovens.

## Tuning levers

- **More leopard / blistering.** Push to 48 h cold rest. Bump levain to 10% if your starter is sluggish. Bake hotter.
- **More flavor depth.** 48 h cold rest, 10–15% rye in the levain feed.
- **Softer, more digestible crust.** Stay closer to 60% hydration. Don't over-salt.
- **Crisper bottom.** Hotter stone, slightly drier balls (back off to 60% hydration).
- **Bigger pie balls (350 g, ~14").** Same percentages, scale up.

## Troubleshooting

- **Rim doesn't puff.** Over-proofed, over-handled when stretching, or oven not hot enough. Check final-proof timing first.
- **Wet/soggy center.** Too much sauce or too-wet mozzarella. Drain mozzarella on paper towel for 30 min before. Crushed tomatoes only — no canned sauce.
- **Tough, chewy crust.** Under-proofed, or too much flour worked into the dough during stretching. Use semolina to dust, not 00.
- **Tears when stretching.** Under-proofed (cold), or over-fermented (slack). Cold dough is supposed to feel firm — that's fine. Just give it more time at room temp.

## Why DEAM works for Neapolitan

The classic commercial Neapolitan recipe uses a *biga* or commercial yeast and a 24-hour bulk. Sourdough versions normally need a long cold rest anyway because the LAB needs time to do its work. DEAM's principles fit exactly:

- **Low inoculation** matches the Neapolitan-style biga's slow ferment.
- **Long cold rest** does the enzymatic priming you need for that puff and char at high heat.
- **No oil/sugar** lets the fermentation flavor speak — exactly what you want.
