# Troubleshooting

Most beginner sourdough problems come from **a small number of root causes**. This file is organized by **symptom** — what you actually observed — with the most likely causes and fixes.

If your bake didn't work, find the closest symptom below.

## Starter problems

### "Starter won't get bubbly / no rise"

Most common cause: **kitchen too cold.** Wild yeast wakes up slowly below 20 °C / 68 °F.

Fixes:

- Move starter to a warmer spot (top of fridge, near sunny window, oven with light on)
- Use slightly warmer water (~30 °C / 86 °F) for feeding
- Add a tiny bit more rye/whole wheat flour at the next feed
- **Be patient** — sometimes starters take 10–14 days

Other possibilities:

- Flour too old / chlorinated
- Water too chlorinated (let it off-gas overnight, or use spring water)
- Starter is **fine but you can't see the rise** — try marking the level on the jar with a rubber band

### "Starter smells terrible (like vomit, cheese, or rotting)"

If days 1–4: **normal**, this is the early bacteria phase. Wait it out.

If days 5+: 

- Could be unwanted bacteria dominated. Discard mostly, refresh aggressively (1:5:5 ratio: 10 g starter + 50 g flour + 50 g water), feed twice daily for 2 days.
- If it doesn't recover after 2 days of aggressive refresh: **start over**. Sometimes faster than fixing.

### "Starter has gray/brown liquid on top (hooch)"

That's just **alcohol from late fermentation**. Means the starter is hungry. **Stir it back in and feed**. Hooch is fine, not bad.

### "Starter has fuzzy mold (pink, green, blue, white-fuzzy)"

**Throw it out and start over.** Don't try to salvage moldy starter — risk of off-flavors and (rarely) mycotoxins.

(Hooch is gray-brown liquid, not mold. Mold has texture and color and looks fuzzy.)

### "Starter rose huge then collapsed before I could use it"

Healthy yeast — it peaked early and ran out of food. Either:

- Feed sooner (every 8 hours instead of every 12)
- Use a higher feeding ratio (1:5:5 instead of 1:1:1) to give it more food
- Use cooler water if your kitchen is warm

### "Starter floats one day, sinks the next"

The float test is **inconsistent** — depends on starter age, hydration, recent stir activity. **Don't rely on it solely.** Use volume increase + bubble structure + smell + timing as your real indicators.

## Dough problems

### "Dough is sticky / slack / hard to handle"

Most likely:

- **Hydration too high** for your flour or skill level — drop hydration 5%
- **Cold rest too short** — gluten didn't have time to develop; longer cold rest helps
- **Too much salt mixed unevenly** — salt clumps make weak spots; mix evenly

For wet doughs: **wet your hand** during folds (water doesn't stick to wet skin). Use a bench scraper instead of fingers.

### "Dough is tight / dense / won't rise"

Most likely:

- **Too cold** — fermentation slows below 18 °C. Move dough to a warmer spot.
- **Inoculation too low** — try 20% instead of 10–15% for next bake
- **Salt too high** — keep at 1.8–2.2%
- **Underfermented** — give it more time at bulk

### "Dough is over-fermented (slack, sticky, smells alcoholic)"

Bulk went too long, or temperature too warm. Crash it cold immediately and **bake even if it's not perfect** — at this stage, going longer just makes it worse. Some loaves you eat humbly, some you turn into focaccia.

Next time:

- Watch the dough, not the clock
- Aim for **30–50% rise** and "puffy and jiggly"
- If your kitchen is warm, shorten the bulk

### "Dough rose great but collapsed when shaped"

Over-proofed at bulk — gluten degraded. Shape **earlier** next time (when dough has gained 30%, not 100%).

## Bake problems

### "Crumb is gummy / underbaked"

Most likely:

- **Underbaked** — use a thermometer, target 96–98 °C internal
- **Sliced too soon** — wait 45 minutes after pulling from oven
- **Over-fermented** — gluten broke down; structure can't hold steam properly

### "Crust is too pale"

- Underbaked
- Not enough sugar in the dough — try a longer cold rest next time (more amylase activity → more residual sugar → more browning)
- Oven runs cool — get an oven thermometer

### "Crust is too dark / burnt"

- Oven runs hot — drop temp by 10–20 °C
- Overbaked — pull a couple of minutes earlier
- Use a lower rack
- Sugar overload from a very long cold rest; shorten the cold rest

### "Crumb is dense / tight"

- **Under-fermented** — most common cause. Give bulk more time.
- **Inoculation too low** for the timing
- **Dough too cold** during fermentation
- **Over-shaped** — degassed too much when shaping

### "Crumb has huge holes only in the middle, dense everywhere else"

Classic over-proof at final / under-fermented at bulk combination. The middle fermented more than the edges. Next time: **better shaping** for even gas distribution + don't let the final proof go too long.

### "Bread is sour but I want it less sour"

Several levers:

- Use the levain **earlier** (1–2 days from prep, not 4–5)
- Reduce cold proof time
- Bulk warmer (lactic acid is smoother than acetic; warm bulk favors lactic)
- Use 1:3 or 1:5 starter feeding ratios instead of 1:1 (less acid build between feeds)

### "Bread is bland — I want it more sour"

- Use the levain **older** (4–5 days from prep)
- Lengthen the cold proof
- Bulk slightly cooler
- Use a stiff levain (50–65% hydration) for more acetic acid character

## Loaf problems

### "Loaf has no oven spring"

Most likely:

- **Over-proofed at final** — too tired to spring. Test with the poke test (poke the dough; should spring back slowly, not pop right back nor stay dented)
- **Under-fermented at bulk** — not enough gas to expand
- **Oven not hot enough** — need at least 230 °C / 445 °F for spring
- **Not enough steam** — Dutch oven with lid traps steam; if not using Dutch oven, add a steam tray

### "Loaf split unevenly along the score"

- Score deeper next time (you want the dough to "open" along the score)
- Or you under-scored — try a longer slash with a sharper blade
- Sometimes loaves just split unpredictably; ugly oven spring is still oven spring

### "No 'ear' on the score"

The classic crispy lifted edge along a scored cut comes from:

- A **single, decisive** score (one slash, deep)
- **30–45° angle** on the blade (not perpendicular to dough)
- Hot, steamy oven for the first 10 min
- Underproofed slightly — over-proofed dough doesn't ear

It takes practice. Beginners' loaves rarely ear well; that's normal.

### "Bread has a thick, tough crust"

- Underbaked (counterintuitive — thick crust often means it didn't fully crisp/dry)
- Over-baked (too long, too hot) — drier crust gets thick
- Try the foil-tent trick: cover loosely with foil for the last 10 min if it's coloring too fast

## Schedule problems

### "Life intervened — can I save my dough?"

Almost always: **put it in the fridge.** Cold slows fermentation 5–10×. A dough that's been in the fridge for 6 extra hours is probably fine.

If the dough is pre-bulk: cold rest doesn't hurt.
If the dough is mid-bulk: it'll keep slowly fermenting; pull it out when you can and continue.
If the dough is shaped: cold proof in the fridge, bake when ready.

### "I can't bake today — should I bake tomorrow?"

Yes. Cold-proof in the fridge overnight (or for up to 24 hours total). Bake straight from the fridge — actually produces *better* results than same-day baking due to surface drying and Maillard substrate concentration.

### "I forgot to feed my starter for 2 weeks"

It's almost certainly fine. In the fridge, sourdough starters can survive 2–4+ weeks unfed. To revive: take it out, discard most, do an aggressive refresh (1:5:5), feed twice daily for 1–2 days. It'll come back.

If it's been months: it might be dead, but try the same revival first. If no signs of life after 3 days of feeding, start over.

### "I bake once every 2–3 weeks — what about my starter?"

Just keep it in the fridge. Feed it once a week with a 1:1:1 (no need to bulk-feed). Or skip a week and just refresh before your next bake. Starters are robust.

## When to start over

You almost never have to. Even a "ruined" starter, dough, or loaf can usually be salvaged. Cases where starting over is genuinely faster:

- **Moldy starter** — yes, start over
- **Starter that's been smelly for 7+ days with no improvement** — yes, faster to restart
- **Dough with visible mold** (very rare) — start over
- **Dough you're certain is way over-fermented** — bake it as flatbread or focaccia rather than waste the flour, but skip the boule

Almost everything else can be baked, eaten, and learned from.
