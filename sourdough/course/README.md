# Sourdough course — DEAM for beginners

A 4-week introduction to sourdough using the **Double Enzymatic Activation Method**. The goal is not "you understand all the chemistry" — that comes later if you want it. The goal is: by the end of week 4, you have **a healthy starter, a confident weekly rhythm, and at least three real bakes** under your belt, with the framework to keep going.

Built for **total beginners** — no prior baking experience assumed. If you already bake sourdough on a different method, skim weeks 1–2 and start at week 3.

## Why DEAM for beginners

Most sourdough courses teach the **traditional** method: feed your starter daily, time everything around when the starter peaks, build levains the morning of bake, bake same-day or next-day. This works, but it's high-touch and unforgiving for someone learning around a job and a life.

DEAM **inverts the workflow**:

- You feed your starter once a week, not daily.
- You build levains in advance and store them cold for days.
- You let enzymes do most of the work passively while you're not in the kitchen.
- The schedule bends to your life, not the other way around.

For a beginner, this means **fewer chances to mess up by mistiming a feed** and **more flexibility** when life intervenes. You don't have to be home when the dough peaks — that's the whole point.

The full method is documented in [`../methods/double-enzymatic-activation.md`](../methods/double-enzymatic-activation.md). This course gets you there step by step.

## How to use this course

- **Don't skip weeks.** Each week builds on the previous. Week 2 needs the starter from week 1; week 3 needs you to have baked once.
- **Read each week's file before the week begins.** Then come back to it as you work through the steps.
- **Take notes.** A small notebook with dates, dough temperatures, what you observed, what worked. By week 4 your notes will be more useful than any recipe.
- **Don't aim for perfect.** Aim for *done*. Your first bake will not look like a magazine photo. That's normal. Bake it anyway, eat it, take notes, do the next one.
- **It's OK to fail a bake.** Most beginner sourdough disasters are recoverable as flatbread, croutons, or breadcrumbs. Even an outright bad bake teaches you something.

## Time commitment

- **Week 1:** ~5 minutes/day for 7 days (feeding starter)
- **Week 2:** ~3 hours active time across 2–3 days (your first bake)
- **Week 3:** ~2 hours active time across 2 days (cold-fed levain bake)
- **Week 4:** ~3 hours active time across 2–3 days (tuned bake + reading)

Most of the time is **passive** — dough resting, fermenting, baking. Active hands-on time is small.

## What you'll need

See [`00-prerequisites.md`](00-prerequisites.md) for the full list. Headline:

- A **kitchen scale** (essential — sourdough is measured by weight, not volume)
- **Bread flour** + a small bag of **whole wheat or rye**
- A **mixing bowl, jar, oven**, and any pot you can put in the oven
- That's basically it. You don't need a banneton, a bench scraper, or a Dutch oven to start.

## What this course is NOT

- **Not** a comprehensive bread-baking education. We focus on DEAM, one method, well.
- **Not** a chemistry course — though [`../../theory/`](../../theory/) is there if you want to dive deeper after week 4.
- **Not** a recipe book. We give you three recipes here. The point is the method, not the recipes.
- **Not** about Instagram-perfect loaves. Open crumbs and ear-scoring come with practice. We focus on *bakes you actually want to eat*.
