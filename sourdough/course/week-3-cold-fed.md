# Week 3 — Cold-fed levain rhythm

You've baked once. Time to step up to the **proper DEAM workflow** — weekly prep, cold-fed levains, and your second bake.

This week's bake is a **boule** (round loaf) — the iconic sourdough shape. With a Dutch oven (or DIY equivalent) you'll get oven spring, a real crust, and a satisfying open crumb.

## What you'll do this week

1. **Sunday (or any flexible day):** prep 2–3 cold-fed levains for the week
2. **Bake day (Wednesday-ish):** wake one up, mix, cold rest, bake

The setup time on Sunday is **15 minutes**. The bake day pulls a fully-ready levain from the fridge — no scramble, no daily feeds, no waiting for peak.

## What you'll need

- Your starter (out of fridge, fed once to wake it up — see "Pre-Sunday")
- Bread flour (a few hundred g)
- Whole wheat or rye (for super food)
- A few **small jars** with loose lids for cold-fed levains
- Optional but recommended: a **Dutch oven** (4–6 qt) or a **cast-iron skillet + roasting pan inverted as a lid** (DIY equivalent)

## Pre-Sunday — refresh your starter

The day before levain prep:

1. **Take starter from fridge.**
2. **Discard all but 25 g.**
3. **Feed:** 50 g flour + 50 g water + a pinch of whole wheat / rye.
4. **Cover. Leave at room temp 8–12 hours** (overnight).

By Sunday morning the starter should be **active, bubbly, peaked**.

## Sunday — levain prep

This is the new rhythm. **Once a week**, in 15 minutes, you set up the levains for your next 2–3 bakes.

### Step 1: Prep 3 small jars

Three small jars, each with a loose lid. Label them with the date.

### Step 2: Build levains

In each jar:

- **25 g of your active morning starter**
- **50 g bread flour**
- **50 g water**
- **A small pinch of whole wheat / rye** (~3 g — the "super food")

That's a 1:2:2 ratio. Stir each well. Cover loosely.

### Step 3: First fermentation

Leave the jars on the counter for **4–6 hours**, until each one shows visible signs of activity (bubbles, slight rise).

### Step 4: Refrigerate

When the levains are clearly active (bubbles, maybe ~30–50% rise), **put them in the fridge.**

That's it for Sunday. The levains will continue fermenting slowly in the cold — building flavor, developing complexity, ready to use anytime in the next 5 days.

> **What's happening:** in the fridge, yeast slows down dramatically but **enzymes (especially amylase) keep working**. The levain becomes sugar-rich and flavorful while you sleep. By bake day, it's primed and waiting.

### Optional: midweek stir

Every 1–2 days, **briefly stir the cold levains** with a clean spoon. This adds oxygen, keeps yeast healthy, and prevents premature collapse. Takes 10 seconds per jar.

## Bake day — pull a levain

Pick any day this week (e.g., Wednesday or Thursday, when the levains are 3–4 days old).

### Morning: pre-ferment wake-up

1. **Take one levain from the fridge.** No need to warm it up first.
2. **Get a large mixing bowl.**
3. **Add 350 g water** (room temperature).
4. **Add the entire levain** (~120–125 g) to the water. Stir to dissolve.
5. **Add 200 g of bread flour** (about half the total). Stir to a thick batter (poolish-style).
6. **In a separate bowl**, mix **300 g bread flour + 9 g salt**.
7. **Cover the wet mix.** Let sit **45 minutes – 1 hour.**

After an hour, the wet mix should show **clear bubbling and slight rise** — the cold-fed levain is waking up vigorously, fed by the fresh flour and water.

### Late morning: mix final dough

1. **Add the salted flour mix** to the active pre-ferment.
2. **Mix with one hand** for 1–2 minutes until shaggy and no dry flour remains. Don't try to develop gluten — the cold rest will do it.
3. **Cover. Rest 30 min** at room temp.
4. **One stretch-and-fold:** wet hand, lift one side, fold over the top, rotate, repeat 4 times.
5. **Cover. Into the fridge** for **8–14 hours** (overnight is perfect).

This is the **enzymatic rest**. The dough sits cold, gluten develops passively, amylase frees sugar, the dough preps itself for fermentation.

### Next morning: bulk fermentation

After the cold rest:

1. **Pull the dough from the fridge.**
2. **Let it warm up** at room temp for **45–60 minutes.**
3. **Do another stretch-and-fold** — same technique as before, 4 folds.
4. **Cover. Rest 45 min.**
5. **Coil fold:** lift the dough from the middle and let the sides fold under as you set it down. Rotate. Do this 2–3 times. (Easier than it sounds; YouTube "coil fold sourdough" if confused.)
6. **Cover. Rest 1 hour.**
7. **One more coil fold round.**
8. **Cover. Rest 1 hour.**

By now the dough should look **puffy, jiggly, and gassy.** Bubbles visible at the edges. Volume increase ~30–60%.

### Total bulk: 3–4 hours from removing from fridge

If your dough is bubbly, smooth-topped, jiggly, and has gained noticeable volume: it's done with bulk. **Move to shape.**

If it's still tight and sluggish, give it another hour and re-check. Watch the dough, not the clock.

## Shape

This is where focaccia diverges from boule.

### Pre-shape

1. **Lightly flour your bench** (counter).
2. **Tip the dough out gently** — try not to deflate it.
3. **Tuck the edges under** to form a loose round. Don't squeeze.
4. **Bench rest 20–30 minutes**, seam-side up.

### Final shape

1. **Lightly flour the top of the round.**
2. **Flip it over** so the floured side is down.
3. **Pull the bottom edge up** over the dough.
4. **Stretch the right side**, fold over the center.
5. **Stretch the left side**, fold over the center.
6. **Roll it from top to bottom**, sealing the seam underneath.
7. **Place seam-side down** in a banneton lined with floured cloth, OR a flour-dusted bowl with a tea towel.

### Final proof

Two options:

- **Counter proof, 1–2 hours**: Quick, less depth of flavor. Bake the same day.
- **Cold proof, 8–18 hours overnight**: Deeper flavor, better blistering, easier to score. Bake next morning straight from the fridge.

For your second bake, **try the overnight cold proof.** It's the DEAM way and produces dramatic results.

## Bake

### Preheat with the Dutch oven inside

1. **Place your Dutch oven (with lid)** in the oven.
2. **Preheat to 245 °C / 475 °F** for **30–45 minutes** to fully heat.

### Score and load

1. **Take the dough out of the proofing vessel** onto a piece of parchment paper.
2. **Score the top** with a sharp blade — a single deep slash, or an X. Don't stress the pattern; oven spring matters more than aesthetics.
3. **Lift the parchment-with-dough** and lower it into the hot Dutch oven.
4. **Replace the lid.**

### Bake covered, then uncovered

- **Bake 20 min with lid on** (the trapped steam is what gives oven spring and crust shine)
- **Remove lid. Drop temp to 225 °C / 440 °F.**
- **Bake another 20–25 min** until **deep golden-brown**.

### Total bake: ~45 minutes

When the loaf is **deeply colored, sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom**, and reads **96–98 °C / 205–208 °F** internally (if you have a thermometer): **done**.

## Cool

**Resist the urge to slice immediately.** Let it cool on a rack for at least **45 minutes**. Inside the loaf, starches are still setting. Slicing into a hot loaf gives you gummy crumb.

## What to log

In your notebook:

- Levain age (how many days from prep to bake)
- Time and temp of the cold rest
- Dough temp at end of bulk
- Time at each fold stage
- Visual / smell observations during bulk
- Bake details
- How it tasted
- One thing to try differently

## What to expect from this bake

Compared to your week 2 focaccia:

- **More complex flavor** — the cold-fed levain has been developing for days
- **More noticeable sourness** — your starter is older and LAB is more active
- **Better oven spring** — Dutch oven, plus better-developed dough
- **A real crust** — crackling, deep brown, blistered if you cold-proofed
- **Open crumb** — bigger holes, more lift than focaccia

It will look like a real sourdough boule. Possibly the best bread you've ever made at home.

## Common week 3 issues

### "Levain didn't activate when I pulled from fridge"

Either the levain sat too long in the fridge (>5 days), OR your pre-ferment temperature was too cold. Solutions: use a warmer water at pre-ferment, OR feed the levain a small "lunch" (20 g flour + 20 g water) the day before bake.

### "Dough was too slack / sticky during folds"

The cold rest may have been too long, OR the bulk too warm. Next time, try a shorter cold rest (8 hr instead of 14) or cooler bulk temp.

### "Bread has tight/dense crumb"

Most likely: under-fermented at bulk. The dough wasn't bubbly enough when you shaped. Next time, watch for more visible bubbles + a 50%+ rise before shaping.

### "Bread overflowed the Dutch oven / collapsed"

Over-proofed at bulk OR over-shaped. Next time: pre-shape lightly, final-shape with conviction.

### "Crust is too pale"

Either underbaked (longer next time) or your cold rest was too short (less sugar built up = less browning).

### "Crust is too dark / burnt"

Oven runs hot. Use the lower rack next time, or drop temp by 10–20 °C.
