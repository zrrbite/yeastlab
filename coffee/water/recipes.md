# Coffee water recipes

There are two ways to get good coffee water: **buy it** (commercial mineral packets / concentrates) or **make it** (RO + your own salts). Both work. The DIY route is cheaper at scale and gives you control to dial profiles for specific brew methods or beans.

For the *why* behind each ion, see [`theory.md`](theory.md). For everything else: this is the recipe page.

## Quick reference table

| Recipe / Standard         | Hardness (ppm CaCO₃) | Bicarbonate (ppm) | Best for                          |
|---------------------------|----------------------|-------------------|------------------------------------|
| **SCA Standard**          | 50–175               | 40                | General all-purpose target         |
| **Hendon "Espresso" / TWW Espresso** | ~150       | 0                 | Espresso, no scale                 |
| **Colonna-Dashwood / Hendon "Bright"** | ~70 (mostly Mg) | 0      | Filter, brightness-forward         |
| **Lotus 1L Pour-Over**    | ~120                 | trace             | Filter, balanced                   |
| **Third Wave Water "Classic"** | ~130            | trace             | Filter, all-purpose                |
| **Third Wave Water "Espresso"** | ~110           | 0                 | Espresso                           |
| **"Rao Water" (Scott Rao)** | ~150              | 0                 | Espresso, body-forward             |

The headline distinction between recipes is **bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)**:

- **Zero bicarbonate** = brighter coffee, more acid expression, **no scale buildup in espresso machines**.
- **Some bicarbonate (40–80 ppm)** = mellower, less acidic perception, more "balanced" cup. Will scale espresso machines over time.

For espresso, **most modern recipes go zero-bicarbonate** to protect the machine. For pour-over and filter, a little bicarbonate is fine and some prefer it.

## SCA Water Standard (the baseline)

The Specialty Coffee Association published a water quality standard that became the industry reference:

| Property                | Acceptable range                |
|-------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Total dissolved solids  | 75–250 ppm (target ~150)         |
| Calcium hardness        | 17–85 ppm CaCO₃ (target ~51)     |
| Total alkalinity        | 40 ppm CaCO₃ (~40 ppm bicarbonate) |
| pH                      | 6.5–7.5 (target 7.0)             |
| Sodium                  | 10 ppm (low)                     |
| Chlorine                | 0                                |
| Total chlorides         | 0                                |

The SCA standard is **conservative and machine-safe** but doesn't necessarily produce the most expressive coffee. Most modern third-wave recipes diverge from SCA on **bicarbonate** (cutting it to zero for brightness and machine longevity) and on **magnesium-to-calcium ratio** (favoring more magnesium for flavor extraction).

## DIY: building water from RO

The approach: start with reverse-osmosis water (~0 TDS), add precise amounts of mineral salts to dose specific ions, mix, use.

### What you need

- **Reverse osmosis water** — from a home RO system (best), distilled water from a grocery store (works), or RO refill kiosks (most US grocery stores).
- **Three salts:**
  - **Magnesium sulfate (Epsom salt, MgSO₄·7H₂O)** — adds Mg²⁺ and SO₄²⁻
  - **Calcium chloride (CaCl₂·2H₂O)** — adds Ca²⁺ and Cl⁻
  - **Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃, baking soda)** — adds Na⁺ and HCO₃⁻ (only in recipes that include bicarbonate)
- **Precision scale** — accurate to 0.01 g. Coffee water doses are tiny (often < 0.5 g per liter). A jewelry scale works.
- **Concentrated stock solutions** (recommended) — instead of weighing salts each time, make a 100× concentrate of each, then dose by milliliters. See "concentrate method" below.

### Recipe: Hendon "Espresso" / TWW Espresso style

Brightness-forward, zero-bicarbonate, espresso-machine-safe.

**Per 1 L of RO water, add:**

- 0.10 g magnesium sulfate (Epsom salt)
- 0.04 g calcium chloride dihydrate
- 0 g sodium bicarbonate (none)

This gives roughly:

- Mg²⁺ ~10 ppm
- Ca²⁺ ~10 ppm
- Total hardness ~50 ppm CaCO₃ equivalent
- HCO₃⁻ 0 ppm

Slightly different from "TWW Espresso" exact formulation but in the same family.

### Recipe: Colonna-Dashwood / Hendon "Bright Filter"

Magnesium-forward, zero-bicarbonate, brightness and complexity.

**Per 1 L of RO water:**

- 0.18 g magnesium sulfate
- 0.07 g calcium chloride dihydrate
- 0 g sodium bicarbonate

Approximately:

- Mg²⁺ ~18 ppm
- Ca²⁺ ~18 ppm
- Total hardness ~75 ppm CaCO₃
- Mg : Ca ratio ~1 : 1 by mass (Mg : Ca higher than typical tap water)
- HCO₃⁻ 0 ppm

**Use for filter / pour-over / AeroPress.** The Mg-forward profile is associated with brighter acidity and clearer flavor expression.

### Recipe: SCA Standard target

The SCA-recommended profile, with bicarbonate.

**Per 1 L of RO water:**

- 0.13 g magnesium sulfate
- 0.07 g calcium chloride dihydrate
- 0.07 g sodium bicarbonate

Approximately:

- Mg²⁺ ~13 ppm
- Ca²⁺ ~18 ppm
- HCO₃⁻ ~50 ppm
- Hardness ~80 ppm CaCO₃

Use for general all-purpose brewing. The bicarbonate buffers the cup's acidity slightly, giving a "rounder" perception.

### Recipe: Rao Water (Scott Rao)

A higher-mineral espresso recipe favoring body and crema.

**Per 1 L of RO water:**

- 0.30 g magnesium sulfate
- 0.10 g calcium chloride dihydrate
- 0 g sodium bicarbonate

Approximately:

- Mg²⁺ ~30 ppm
- Ca²⁺ ~25 ppm
- Hardness ~150 ppm CaCO₃
- HCO₃⁻ 0 ppm

Higher mineral content → more body. Some find this profile too "heavy" for filter; it shines in espresso.

## The concentrate method (recommended)

Mixing salts directly into 1 L bottles is tedious. Instead, make **two concentrated stocks** that you dose by mL.

### Two-bottle concentrate (zero-bicarbonate recipes)

For a Colonna-Dashwood / Hendon Bright recipe, batch up:

**Bottle A — magnesium concentrate (1 L):**

- 18 g magnesium sulfate (Epsom)
- Top to 1000 mL with RO water

**Bottle B — calcium concentrate (1 L):**

- 7 g calcium chloride dihydrate
- Top to 1000 mL with RO water

To make 1 L of brewing water:

- Add **10 mL of Bottle A** + **10 mL of Bottle B** to 980 mL RO water
- Shake or stir
- Done

For a 5 L batch:

- 50 mL of A + 50 mL of B + 4900 mL RO
- Or simplify: pour 50 mL of each into a 5 L jug, top with RO

This scales linearly. Mix 5 L of brewing water in 30 seconds once you have the concentrates.

### Storage

- Concentrate bottles last **6+ months at room temp** if sealed. They'll cloud / precipitate if very concentrated; shake before use.
- Brewing water (post-mix) is **best used within a day or two** — it's just slightly mineralized water, no preservative.

## Commercial alternatives

If DIY isn't appealing, several products do this for you:

### Third Wave Water (TWW)

Pre-measured mineral packets to add to a gallon (3.78 L) of distilled water.

- **Pros:** very easy, several profiles (Classic, Espresso, Light Roast, etc.), available at most coffee shops.
- **Cons:** more expensive per liter than DIY (~$0.50–$1.00/gallon vs ~$0.05/gallon for DIY).
- **Caveat:** can't tweak the recipe to your taste / specific bean.

### Lotus Coffee Water

Concentrate-based system from Empirical Water (formerly Lotus).

- **Pros:** premixed concentrates in dropper bottles, multiple profiles, well-respected by competition baristas.
- **Cons:** moderate cost, dropper bottles need precision dosing.
- **Profile:** generally more refined / specialty-coffee oriented than TWW.

### Aquacode

Newer entrant, similar concentrate model.

### Commercial RO + remineralization systems

For a coffee shop or serious home setup:

- **Reverse osmosis** + **remineralization cartridge** preset to a specific profile.
- Cost: $500–$2000+ for the install. Worth it if you're brewing many liters per week.
- Brands: Optipure, Everpure, BWT.

## Picking a recipe for your brew method

| Brew method      | Recommended starting recipe                | Why                                           |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Espresso         | Hendon Espresso / TWW Espresso / Rao Water | Zero bicarbonate (machine longevity), variable mineral level for body |
| Pour-over / V60  | Colonna-Dashwood Bright Filter             | Magnesium-forward, brightness, clarity        |
| AeroPress        | Colonna-Dashwood Bright Filter or SCA      | Either works; depends on preference           |
| French press     | SCA Standard                               | Some bicarbonate softens the heavy body       |
| Cold brew        | SCA Standard                               | Long extraction, bicarbonate helps balance    |
| Moka pot         | Hendon Espresso / TWW Espresso             | Zero bicarbonate, similar concerns to espresso |

## A note on calculation tools

If you want to design custom profiles:

- **Brewminate** (web tool) — handles coffee water calculations.
- **Lotus's mineral calculator** — simple, profile-focused.
- **Spreadsheets** — once you know that 1 g MgSO₄·7H₂O in 1 L of water yields ~25 ppm Mg, you can derive any recipe.
