# Coffee water

A finished cup of coffee is **98–99% water**. The minerals in that water determine what gets extracted from the grounds, how the cup tastes, and how forgiving the brewer is. There's no aspect of coffee where small changes pay off more.

## Contents

- **[`recipes.md`](recipes.md)** — water profiles and DIY mineral recipes. SCA standard, third-wave brewing community formulations (Lotus, Third Wave Water), Colonna-Dashwood / Hendon "Hendon Water," and how to make them yourself from RO + salts.
- **[`theory.md`](theory.md)** — why each ion matters. Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, HCO₃⁻, Na⁺, Cl⁻ — what each does to extraction, taste, and machine longevity.

## TL;DR — what to actually do

If you're not going to read further:

1. **Don't use straight tap water** unless yours happens to fall in the right range (most doesn't). Hard water or chlorinated water makes mediocre coffee, hard.
2. **Don't use straight RO or distilled water.** Without minerals, water under-extracts and the coffee tastes flat and weak.
3. **Use a recipe.** Either a commercial product (Third Wave Water, Lotus, Aquacode) or DIY from RO + salts.
4. **Match the recipe to the brew method.** Espresso wants slightly different water than pour-over. See [`recipes.md`](recipes.md).
5. **If you have an espresso machine: water also affects scaling.** Pick a recipe with low or zero bicarbonate to extend machine life.
