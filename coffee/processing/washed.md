# Washed (wet) processing

The default. Most specialty coffee globally is washed. The hallmark is a **clean, bright, terroir-driven cup** where you taste the bean and the origin, not the processing.

## What happens

The cherry's pulp is mechanically removed, the mucilage is fermented off (briefly), the bean is washed clean, then dried. Fermentation is **shorter and more controlled** than in any other method.

## Step by step

### 1. Cherry harvest and sorting

Ripe red cherries hand-picked or stripped. Producer sorts to remove unripe (green), overripe (black), and defective cherries. Typically same-day delivery to the wet mill.

### 2. Float separation

Cherries dumped into water tanks. **Floaters** (under-ripe, hollow, defective) skimmed off; **sinkers** (dense, ripe, good) proceed.

### 3. Pulping

Cherries fed through a pulper — a mechanical grinder that **separates skin and pulp from the bean** while leaving the **mucilage** (the sticky sugar layer between bean and skin) attached.

The output: beans coated in slimy, sticky mucilage. The skins/pulp are removed and often composted back to the farm.

### 4. Fermentation (the key step)

Mucilage-coated beans go into **fermentation tanks** (concrete, plastic, or stainless) for **12–48 hours** at ambient temperature.

What's happening:

- **LAB** (lactobacilli) produce lactic acid → drops pH from ~5.5 to ~4.0
- **Yeast** produce ethanol and aromatic esters
- **Pectinase enzymes** (microbial and intrinsic to the cherry) break down the pectin in mucilage → mucilage liquefies
- Mucilage becomes loose and washable

Two sub-styles:

- **Dry fermentation** — beans + mucilage in a tank, no added water. Hotter, more aggressive fermentation. Common in Ethiopia.
- **Wet fermentation** — beans + mucilage submerged in water. Slower, more even fermentation. Common in Central America, Colombia.

Producer judges fermentation by feel: rub a bean — if it's "rough" with mucilage gone, fermentation is done. Some use Brix meters; some use pH probes; many do it by feel.

### 5. Wash and clean

Beans drained, sluiced through wash channels with water (often 3–5× the volume of beans). Dense, mucilage-free beans sink and continue; lighter beans (poor density, defects) float and are removed.

Result: clean, white-yellow beans (the "parchment" — the silver skin and endocarp around the bean).

### 6. Drying

Washed parchment is spread on:

- **Patios** (concrete) — most common, slowest, exposed to weather
- **Raised beds** (African beds — wire mesh on raised frames) — faster, more even drying, more expensive
- **Mechanical dryers** — fastest, most controlled, sometimes considered to compromise flavor

Beans dry from ~50% moisture to **10–12% moisture**. Takes **5–25 days** depending on conditions.

### 7. Resting and milling

Dried parchment rests in storage for **1–3 months** ("conditioning") to stabilize. Then **milled** to remove the parchment, revealing the **green coffee bean** ready for export.

## What you taste

Washed coffee is associated with:

- **Cleanliness** — clarity of flavor, well-defined notes
- **Brightness** — high-acid coffees express their acidity well; the absence of fruit-fermentation muddle keeps acidity sharp
- **Terroir** — origin character comes through. A washed Ethiopian Yirgacheffe tastes like Yirgacheffe; a washed Colombian tastes like Colombian.
- **Lighter body** than natural processing
- **Complexity** without heavy fermentation overlay

Common descriptor families:

- **Citrus** (lemon, bergamot, orange)
- **Floral** (jasmine, bergamot, rose)
- **Tea-like** (black tea, green tea)
- **Stone fruit** (peach, apricot — moderate)
- **Sweet baking spices** (in lighter-roasted)

## Why it became the standard

Several reasons:

1. **Predictability.** Short, controlled fermentation → consistent results across lots. Producers can manage quality.
2. **Storage stability.** Clean beans without mucilage residue store better, age more gracefully.
3. **Defect filtering.** The float and density-grading steps remove a lot of defects mechanically.
4. **Specialty coffee preference.** The "third wave" cupping community grew up valuing **clarity and terroir** — exactly what washed delivers.
5. **Buyer expectations.** Most specialty roasters expect washed unless explicitly purchasing natural / honey / experimental.

## Variations within "washed"

### Kenyan double fermentation

Specifically Kenyan style. **Two fermentation steps with a wash in between:**

- 1st ferment: 12–24 hours, drain, wash
- Beans soaked in clean water 12–24 hours
- 2nd ferment: another 12–24 hours
- Final wash and dry

Result: even cleaner, brighter, more **acidic and complex** than a single-fermentation wash. Kenyan AA washes are famous for this character.

### Colombian "wet fermented"

Submerged-in-water fermentation, longer (24–48 hr) than typical dry-ferment washed. Cleaner extraction of mucilage, less "rough fermentation" character.

### Ethiopian wet processed

Often dry fermentation (no water in tank), shorter (~24 hr). Combined with the **heirloom varietals** of Ethiopia, this produces some of the most aromatic washed coffees in the world (jasmine, bergamot, citrus).

## The science / fermentation chemistry

What's happening biochemically during the 12–48 hour fermentation step is well-studied:

- **Sugars** (glucose, fructose, sucrose) in mucilage → consumed by yeast and LAB
- **Pectin** (the structural carbohydrate of mucilage) → enzymatically broken down by pectinase
- **pH drop** from ~5.5 → ~4.0 over 24 hours (LAB doing their job)
- **Brix drop** (dissolved sugar measure) from ~8 °Bx → ~3 °Bx
- **Aromatic compounds** — esters formed (LAB acid + yeast alcohol = ester), some of which get absorbed by the bean
- **Off-fermentation risks** if done too long: butyric acid (rancid butter), propionic acid (sweat), excessive acetic acid (vinegar)

A skilled producer manages fermentation to **stop at the right moment** — long enough to fully break down mucilage, short enough to avoid off-flavors.

Modern controlled-environment washed fermentations (temperature-controlled tanks, lactic acid inoculation, etc.) push this further — see [`anaerobic.md`](anaerobic.md) and [`experimental.md`](experimental.md) for the modern frontier.

## Pairing with brewing methods

Washed coffees generally shine in:

- **Pour-over / V60 / Chemex** — emphasizes clarity and acid expression
- **AeroPress** — versatile, brings out the cleanness
- **Espresso (light-roasted specialty)** — modern espresso tradition built around lighter-roasted washed Ethiopians and Kenyans

Less ideal for:

- **Dark espresso blends** — the brightness can clash with the roast character
- **Cold brew** — most of the brightness is lost; the body is light to begin with
