# Coffee

Coffee belongs here for two reasons that this repo treats as one:

1. **Coffee is fermented.** Every coffee bean spent days inside a fermenting fruit before it ever reached a roaster. The processing method (washed, natural, honey, anaerobic) is a *fermentation choice* — same biology as sourdough, beer, and wine, just constrained to a coffee cherry instead of a dough or wort. Acetic and lactic acid bacteria, wild yeasts, citrate metabolism, ester formation — all the same chemistry from [`../theory/fermentation.md`](../theory/fermentation.md), playing out in pulped fruit on a drying patio in Ethiopia or under tarps in Costa Rica.
2. **Coffee is mostly water.** A finished cup is **98–99% water**. The minerals in that water dictate what's extracted from the grounds and what you taste. You can drink the same beans through five different waters and have five different cups — sometimes dramatically so.

Water is also where this whole project started. The same minerals that make a great espresso (calcium, magnesium, bicarbonate buffering) also turn out to make a great sourdough and a great NEIPA — the chemistry rhymes across crafts. See [`../theory/water-minerals.md`](../theory/water-minerals.md) for the cross-cutting view.

## What's here

- **[`water/`](water/)** — water recipes for coffee. Mineral profiles, third-wave brewing community formulations (SCA, Lotus, Third Wave Water), DIY recipes, the chemistry behind each ion's effect.
- **[`processing/`](processing/)** — what happens between cherry and green bean. Washed, natural, honey, anaerobic, and the experimental territory (carbonic maceration, lactic, thermal shock).
- **[`brewing/`](brewing/)** — V60, AeroPress, espresso. Each has its own water sweet spot and technique tradition.
- **[`roasting.md`](roasting.md)** — coffee roasting on the Aillio Bullet R2 (the first commercial induction-heated drum roaster). Profile principles, reference profiles by bean origin, troubleshooting.

## How coffee fits the rest of the repo

| Concept                | Sourdough                          | Beer                                  | Coffee                                  |
|------------------------|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **Microbial culture**  | Wild yeast + LAB in starter        | Yeast (cultured) + sometimes LAB      | Wild yeast + LAB on coffee cherry        |
| **Substrate**          | Flour starches                     | Malted grain sugars                   | Coffee cherry sugars + mucilage          |
| **Fermentation phase** | Bulk + proof                       | Primary + dry hop window              | Cherry processing (washed/natural/honey) |
| **Locking it in**      | Bake                               | Boil + chill + ferment                | Drying + roasting                        |
| **Final transformation** | Maillard in oven                 | Maillard in mash + caramelization in boil | Maillard + caramelization in roasting |
| **Water role**         | Hydration, mash chemistry          | Whole beer, hop chemistry             | Extraction medium, the cup itself        |

The fermentation chemistry is the same. The water chemistry overlaps. The Maillard reactions are essentially identical. **Once you understand one of these crafts deeply, you're 70% of the way to understanding the others.**

## Suggested reading order

If you're new to thinking about coffee this way:

1. **[`water/recipes.md`](water/recipes.md)** — start here. The fastest path to better coffee is better water. Most "the espresso is bad" problems are water problems.
2. **[`water/theory.md`](water/theory.md)** — *why* the water recipes work. Understanding the ion roles unlocks why one bean tastes different through two waters.
3. **[`processing/`](processing/)** — once you know water, processing is the second-biggest variable. Why a natural-process Ethiopia tastes wildly different from a washed Ethiopia from the same farm.
4. **[`brewing/`](brewing/)** — the brewing method matters less than people think compared to water and beans, but each method has its own sweet spot.

## Key references

- **Maxwell Colonna-Dashwood & Christopher Hendon — *Water for Coffee*** (2015). The book that shaped the third-wave water conversation. Some of the chemistry has been refined since, but the framework holds.
- **Scott Rao — *The Professional Barista's Handbook*** (2008) and *Coffee Roaster's Companion* (2014). The most influential modern voice on coffee craft.
- **James Hoffmann** — YouTube and his books (*The World Atlas of Coffee*, *How to Make the Best Coffee at Home*). The most influential popular educator.
- **Specialty Coffee Association (SCA)** — water quality standard, sensory protocols, the definitions everyone references.
- **Barista Hustle** — paid courses, but their free articles are excellent reference.
