# Espresso

The most demanding brewing method. **Concentrated, complex, intensely-flavored coffee** extracted under high pressure (9 bar) at high temperature (~93 °C) in 25–30 seconds. Espresso has its own equipment, its own grind needs, its own water requirements, and its own troubleshooting universe.

This file is an opinionated overview, not a comprehensive espresso course. Espresso is **a multi-year skill**; this just gives you the recipe template, the variables, and the starting points.

## What you need

The barrier to entry for espresso is **equipment cost**:

### Minimum viable home espresso

- **Espresso machine** — a real one with a 9-bar pump and PID temp control. Examples: Gaggia Classic ($400–500), Rancilio Silvia ($700–900), Profitec Pro 300 ($1500), Lelit Bianca ($2500). Avoid cheap "espresso" machines under $200; they don't produce real espresso.
- **Espresso grinder** — burr grinder with extremely fine adjustment. Examples: Eureka Mignon ($350), Niche Zero ($600), Mahlkönig X54 ($800). **A drip grinder will not work for espresso.**
- **Distribution / leveling tools** — WDT (Weiss Distribution Technique) tool, leveler, tamper. ~$30–60.
- **Scale** with timer for shots
- **Bottomless portafilter** (eventually) for diagnosing problems
- **Knock box** for spent pucks

Realistic minimum cost: **$1000–1500** for entry-level real espresso.

### Recipe — the standard "1:2 ratio"

| Parameter         | Value                  |
|-------------------|------------------------|
| Coffee dose       | **18 g**               |
| Espresso out      | **36 g (1:2 ratio)**   |
| Brew time         | **25–30 seconds**      |
| Water temp        | **93 °C / 200 °F**    |
| Pressure          | **9 bar**              |
| Grind             | Very fine (talc-like)  |

The "1:2" ratio (18 g in → 36 g out) is the modern espresso default. Older espresso recipes used 1:1.5 ("ristretto") for more concentrated shots; 1:2 is now standard for balance.

## Pulling a shot

### Setup

1. **Preheat the machine** for 20+ minutes. Espresso needs the group head fully heated. **Don't pull shots cold.**
2. **Grind 18 g of coffee** into the portafilter
3. **WDT** — use a needle tool to break up clumps and distribute evenly
4. **Level** with a leveler tool or by tapping
5. **Tamp** firmly and evenly — 30 lb of pressure is the target; consistency matters more than absolute force

### Pull

1. **Lock portafilter into group head**
2. **Place cup on scale, tare**
3. **Start brewing AND start timer simultaneously**
4. **Watch the shot:**
   - **0–5 sec:** "preinfusion" — pressure builds, no espresso yet
   - **5–10 sec:** first drips, dark golden ("blonding" hasn't started)
   - **10–25 sec:** main flow, golden-brown, increasingly lighter
   - **25–30 sec:** stop the shot when you hit 36 g out

### Evaluate

- **Color:** rich brown with golden cremà on top
- **Cremà:** about 1 cm of fine, persistent foam
- **Mouthfeel:** thick, slightly syrupy
- **Taste:** **balanced** — sweet, slightly bitter, complex aromatic

## What "good espresso" tastes like

A great shot has:

- **Sweetness** — caramel, dark chocolate, fruit, depending on bean
- **Acidity** — bright but integrated, not sour
- **Bitterness** — present but balanced
- **Body** — thick, slightly oily mouthfeel
- **Aromatic complexity** — multiple notes that develop as you sip
- **Long finish** — flavor lingers pleasantly

A bad shot tastes:

- **Sour** — under-extracted (grind coarser, slow pull)
- **Bitter and harsh** — over-extracted (grind finer, faster pull)
- **Watery / thin** — under-extracted or wrong dose
- **Burnt** — water too hot, or coffee roasted way too dark
- **One-note** — staleness, water issues, or extraction problems

## Espresso troubleshooting matrix

The two most common problems and their fixes:

### Sour / weak / fast (under 20 sec)

= **Under-extracted**. Fixes:

- **Grind finer** (most important)
- Ensure proper distribution (WDT, leveling)
- Check tamp — even pressure
- Water hotter (raise PID 1–2°)
- Higher dose (18.5g instead of 18g)

### Bitter / heavy / slow (over 35 sec)

= **Over-extracted**. Fixes:

- **Grind coarser**
- Water cooler (drop PID 1–2°)
- Lower dose (17.5g)
- Or shot is just **fine but slow** — taste before adjusting

### Channeling (water shoots through one spot)

- Better distribution (WDT)
- Better tamp (level)
- Bottomless portafilter to diagnose (you can SEE channels)
- Dose may be too low or too high for basket

## Equipment notes

### The grinder is more important than the machine

A $300 grinder + $500 machine produces **much better espresso** than a $1000 machine + $200 grinder. **Espresso is grind-limited.**

If forced to choose: **buy the better grinder**.

### Single-dose grinders

Modern espresso grinders increasingly let you "single-dose" — weigh exactly 18 g of beans, grind them all, no retention. Examples: Niche Zero, DF64, ODE V2 (modified). Useful for switching between beans without waste.

### Pressure profiling

Higher-end espresso machines (Decent Espresso DE1+, Lelit Bianca, La Marzocco GS3) let you **vary pressure during the shot** — preinfusion at low pressure, main extraction at 9 bar, decline at the end. Genuinely improves shots for some beans. Requires investment and learning.

### Variable temperature ("temp surfing" / PID)

Different beans want different temps:

- **Light roast:** 94–96 °C (more heat to extract)
- **Medium roast:** 92–94 °C
- **Dark roast:** 88–92 °C (less heat to avoid bitterness)

A PID-controlled machine lets you adjust this. Gaggia Classic + PID mod is the cheap entry point.

## Light vs dark roast espresso

Modern specialty espresso splits into two camps:

### Traditional Italian / dark roast espresso

- **Beans:** dark roasted, often blends, with crystal/Italian-style flavors
- **Recipe:** 1:1.5 to 1:2 ratio, lower brew temp (90 °C), shorter time
- **Cup:** thick body, intense bitter-sweet, classic crema
- **Examples:** Lavazza, Illy, traditional Italian cafés

### Modern third-wave / light roast espresso

- **Beans:** light to medium roasted, often single-origin, complex
- **Recipe:** 1:2 to 1:3 ratio, higher brew temp (94–96 °C), longer time (28–35 sec)
- **Cup:** brighter, more acidic, more aromatic complexity, less crema
- **Examples:** modern specialty roasters globally

These are **fundamentally different cup goals.** A light-roast Ethiopian washed pulled in a "modern" style tastes nothing like a dark-roast Italian blend pulled "traditionally." Both are espresso.

## Milk drinks (latte, cappuccino, flat white)

- **Latte:** 1:5+ ratio of espresso to milk, lots of milk, light foam
- **Cappuccino:** 1:1 espresso to foam, traditional 6 oz cup
- **Flat white:** 1:2 espresso to milk, microfoam (not big foam)
- **Cortado:** 1:1 espresso to steamed milk, no foam
- **Macchiato:** espresso with a dot of foam on top

Steaming milk is its own skill. **Whole milk steams best**; alt-milks are getting better but each behaves differently.

## Water for espresso

**Critical** — see [`../water/recipes.md`](../water/recipes.md). Espresso is the most water-sensitive brewing method:

- **Zero bicarbonate** to prevent scale buildup (the espresso machine killer)
- **Mg-forward** for brightness with light-roast espresso
- **Examples:** Hendon Espresso recipe, Third Wave Water Espresso, Rao Water

**Don't use straight tap water in an espresso machine.** It will scale, it will damage components, and it will make worse coffee. RO + remineralization is the gold standard.

## Why espresso is hard

A few reasons espresso has a steep learning curve:

- **Fast feedback, hard to dial in** — the variables interact: grind, dose, distribution, tamp, temp, pressure, time
- **Equipment matters** — bad equipment gives bad results regardless of skill
- **Bean-specific** — same recipe doesn't work for two different beans; you re-dial in for every new coffee
- **Visual cues are subtle** — knowing if a shot is "good" by sight takes practice
- **Maintenance is constant** — backflushing, descaling, group head cleaning, grinder calibration

Plan to invest **months** to be reliably good at espresso, not weeks.

## What this gets you

Once you're dialed in, espresso is **uniquely satisfying**:

- The most concentrated coffee experience
- The fastest brew (~30 sec)
- The widest flavor range (any bean works in espresso)
- The base for milk drinks
- A daily ritual

Worth the investment if you drink coffee daily and want to master one method deeply.
