# V60 — pour-over

The reference brewing method for **bringing out a coffee's character**. The cone shape, paper filter, and gravity-driven extraction make the V60 the cleanest, most articulate way to brew most modern specialty coffees. The brew of choice for showcasing a single-origin.

This file is opinionated and method-focused. It assumes you have a Hario V60 (size 02 — the standard 1–2 cup size) and a gooseneck kettle.

## What you need

- **Hario V60 (size 02)** — plastic, ceramic, glass, or copper all work; ceramic is most popular
- **V60 paper filters (size 02)** — Hario brand or compatible
- **Gooseneck kettle** — controls pour stream. Almost essential for V60.
- **Burr grinder** — blade grinders produce uneven grind, which produces uneven extraction
- **Scale** with timer — accurate to 0.1g preferred, 1g acceptable
- **Server / decanter** — anything to catch the brew. A glass jug works.
- **Mug** to drink from

## Recipe — the standard

For **1 cup (~250 ml)**:

| Parameter         | Value             |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Coffee dose       | **15 g**          |
| Water dose        | **250 g**         |
| **Ratio**         | **1 : 16.7**      |
| Grind             | Medium-fine (kosher salt) |
| Water temp        | **94 °C / 200 °F**|
| Total brew time   | **2:30–3:00 min** |
| Brewing method    | Multi-pour, see below |

For **2 cups (~500 ml)**:

| Parameter         | Value             |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Coffee dose       | **30 g**          |
| Water dose        | **500 g**         |
| Total brew time   | **3:30–4:00 min** |

## The brew (single cup)

### Setup (30 sec)

1. **Boil water** — boil more than you need; you'll lose some to filter rinse and heating
2. **Place V60 on server, on scale, on a level surface**
3. **Insert paper filter**
4. **Rinse the filter** with hot water — this removes paper taste AND preheats the V60 + server. Pour about 100 ml through the filter, then **discard the rinse water** from the server.
5. **Grind 15 g of coffee** (medium-fine, like kosher salt)
6. **Add ground coffee to filter**, give the V60 a gentle shake to level the bed
7. **Tare the scale**

### The pour (2:30–3:00)

#### Stage 1 — Bloom (0:00 to 0:45)

- **Pour 30–40 g of water (2× the coffee weight)** in a slow, controlled spiral, **wetting all the grounds**
- The coffee will **release CO₂** in a "bloom" — bubbles, expansion, fragrant rise
- **Stop pouring. Wait 30–45 seconds.**

The bloom phase is critical for fresh coffee. CO₂ from roasting traps in the grounds; releasing it before main brewing prevents channeling.

#### Stage 2 — First pour (0:45 to 1:15)

- **Pour to 150 g total** (so 110–120 g additional water) in a slow spiral from center outward, then back to center
- **Avoid pouring directly on the filter** — keep water on the coffee bed
- This pour should take ~30 seconds

#### Stage 3 — Second pour (1:30 to 2:00)

- **Pour to 250 g total** (so 100 g additional water)
- Same slow spiral technique
- Should drop the water level back to even with the coffee bed by ~2:00

#### Stage 4 — Drawdown (2:00 to ~3:00)

- **Stop pouring**
- Watch the water drain through the bed
- **Total brew time: 2:30–3:00**

### Drink

- Pour into mug
- **Drink immediately** — V60 is at peak in the first 5 minutes after brewing

## What "good V60" tastes like

- **Bright, articulate, layered** — you can taste distinct notes
- **Clean** — no muddy / heavy residue
- **Balanced acidity and sweetness**
- **Lingering aftertaste** — flavor continues developing after each sip
- **Light-medium body** — nothing like a French press

## Troubleshooting by symptom

### Tastes sour / acidic / underextracted

- **Grind finer** (most common fix)
- **Hotter water** (95 °C instead of 94)
- **Slower pour** (more contact time)
- **Bloom longer** (60 sec instead of 45)

### Tastes bitter / overextracted

- **Grind coarser**
- **Cooler water** (92 °C)
- **Faster pour** (less contact time)
- **Don't agitate the bed** during pour

### Brew time too short (<2 min)

- Grind finer
- Pour slower
- Or coffee is **stale and isn't blooming** properly — channels form, water rushes through

### Brew time too long (>4 min)

- Grind coarser
- Pour faster
- Or coffee is **too fresh** (just-roasted; full of CO₂)
- Or filter clogged — try a different brand/lot

### Tastes flat / boring

- **Use better water** — see [`../water/recipes.md`](../water/recipes.md). The biggest single quality lever.
- Better beans (fresh, well-roasted)
- Different brewer? V60 emphasizes acidity; if you don't like that, AeroPress or French press might suit your beans better

### Channeling (water punching through one spot)

- Wet **all** grounds during bloom
- Pour more slowly
- Level the coffee bed before brewing
- Distribute pours evenly (spiral, not just center)

## Variations

### Slow, single-pour V60 (the "Tetsu Kasuya 4:6 method")

Highly regarded modern method:

- 4 pours in the first 40% of total water, in 45-sec intervals
- 2 pours in the last 60% of total water
- Adjust pour ratios for sweetness vs strength

A bit complex but produces excellent, repeatable results.

### Iced V60 (Japanese method)

- Use **half** the normal water amount for brewing
- Brew **directly into a server with ice** (the ice makes up the other half by weight)
- Result: dramatically aromatic, cold, fresh-tasting iced coffee
- Recipe: 30 g coffee, 250 g water, brewed onto 250 g ice → ~500 g iced coffee

### Strong V60 (1:14)

For darker roasts or denser cups:

- Same 15 g coffee, 210 g water
- Same technique, slightly slower
- Cup will be heavier, more concentrated

### Light V60 (1:18)

For very light roasts:

- 15 g coffee, 270 g water
- Same technique
- Brings out subtle notes; lower body

## Water — pick it deliberately

V60 is **the brewing method most affected by water choice**. The clean filter extraction makes mineral character very obvious.

For light/medium roast specialty coffee:

- **Colonna-Dashwood "Bright Filter"** water — Mg-forward, zero bicarbonate. See [`../water/recipes.md`](../water/recipes.md). Brings out brightness and clarity.

For darker roasts or beans you want softer:

- **SCA standard** with some bicarbonate — softens acidity, mellows the cup

**Don't use straight tap or distilled.** Either over-mineralizes or under-mineralizes; neither produces good V60.

## Why V60 specifically

Compared to other pour-over devices:

| Device              | Hole shape           | Filter        | Character                        |
|---------------------|----------------------|---------------|----------------------------------|
| **V60**             | Single large hole    | Cone, ridges  | Fast drawdown, bright, clean     |
| **Kalita Wave**     | Three small holes    | Flat-bottom   | Slower, more forgiving           |
| **Chemex**          | Single hole, glass   | Thick paper   | Cleanest cup, slow brew          |
| **Origami**         | Multiple ridges      | Either        | Versatile (V60 or flat filter)   |

V60 is **the most articulate** of these — it shows what's in the bean most clearly. **The least forgiving** for sloppy technique. The **most rewarding** for good technique.
