# AeroPress

The most versatile brewer in coffee. **Brews espresso-like concentration, filter-style cups, cold brew, and everything in between** — depending on how you use it. Invented in 2005 by Alan Adler (an aerodynamics engineer who was annoyed at his cup of coffee), it's become the cult favorite of brewers who travel, brewers who want speed, and brewers who want to experiment.

This file covers the **two main techniques** (standard and inverted) plus a couple of recipes worth knowing.

## What you need

- **AeroPress** (any version — standard or AeroPress Go for travel)
- **AeroPress filters** (paper) — or a metal mesh filter for less-clean style
- **Burr grinder**
- **Scale with timer**
- **Mug or server** to brew into

## The standard method (right-side-up)

The "by-the-book" technique.

### Recipe

For **1 cup (~250 ml)**:

| Parameter         | Value             |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Coffee dose       | **17 g**          |
| Water dose        | **220 g** (split: brewing + dilution) |
| Grind             | Medium-fine       |
| Water temp        | **88–92 °C**     |
| Total brew time   | **2 minutes**     |

### Brew

1. Place AeroPress (right-side-up) on top of mug
2. Insert filter into cap, **rinse with hot water**
3. **Add 17 g of medium-fine ground coffee** to chamber
4. **Pour 220 g of water at 90 °C**, stir gently 2 seconds
5. **Insert plunger, but don't push** — creates a slight vacuum that holds water in chamber
6. **Wait 1:30** total contact time
7. **Press slowly** for 30 seconds — total brew time 2:00
8. Stop pressing when you hear hissing

Result: a clean, balanced filter-style cup.

## The inverted method

Reversed AeroPress for **more contact time** without dripping. Many enthusiasts prefer this.

### Setup

1. Place plunger on counter, plunger-down
2. Insert chamber on top, upside-down
3. Result: AeroPress is **inverted**, water doesn't drain through

### Brew

1. Add coffee to chamber (which is now on top)
2. Add water
3. Stir or steep for desired contact time
4. Place filter cap on top, **flip carefully** onto a server, press

This lets you do **longer steeps** (3–4 minutes) without water dripping out the bottom.

## Recipes worth knowing

### James Hoffmann's "Ultimate" AeroPress

A famous recipe that won the 2008 World AeroPress Championship in spirit:

- **11 g coffee**, medium-fine grind
- **200 g water at 100 °C** (just-boiled)
- Standard (right-side-up) method
- **Pour, stir, wait 2 minutes, swirl gently, wait 30 sec**
- **Press slowly** for 30 seconds
- **Dilute** with **70 g of room-temp water** in the cup
- Total: 270 g cup, very clean, very flavorful

The dilution at the end is the trick — controls strength without controlling extraction.

### Strong / espresso-style

For when you want concentrated coffee (good base for milk drinks):

- **20 g coffee**, fine grind (just slightly coarser than espresso)
- **60 g water at 92 °C**
- Standard method
- **Stir, wait 30 sec**
- **Press slowly** for 60 seconds
- Result: 1:3 ratio, espresso-strength concentrate
- Mix with hot water for an "Americano," or with steamed milk for a latte

### Inverted long-steep

For darker roasts or beans you want richer:

- **18 g coffee**, medium grind
- **220 g water at 88 °C**
- Inverted method
- **Stir, wait 3 minutes**
- Flip and press for 30 seconds
- Clean but full-bodied

### Cold brew (super-fast)

The AeroPress can do cold brew in 2 minutes:

- **15 g coffee**, fine grind
- **200 g cold/room-temp water**
- Inverted method
- **Stir vigorously for 1 minute** continuously
- Wait 1 more minute
- Press
- Add ice to the cup
- Result: cold-brew character without the 12-hour wait

The aggressive stirring substitutes for time. Surprisingly good.

## Grind size guide

The same AeroPress brews very differently at different grind sizes:

| Grind size       | Cup style                       |
|------------------|----------------------------------|
| Fine (espresso-) | Concentrated, strong, espresso-ish |
| Medium-fine      | Standard cup, balanced (default) |
| Medium           | Lighter, faster brew             |
| Medium-coarse    | French-press-like, fuller body   |

Most brewers find **medium-fine** the sweet spot for the standard method.

## Why AeroPress

Compared to other methods:

- **vs V60:** more forgiving (less technique-dependent), fuller body, less articulated
- **vs French press:** cleaner cup (paper filter), more control, less sediment
- **vs espresso:** much faster, much cheaper, less concentrated but more nuanced

AeroPress shines for:

- **Travel** — light, durable, easy
- **Variety** — one device, many cup styles
- **Speed** — 2-minute brew time
- **Forgiving technique** — even sloppy AeroPress is usually OK
- **Single-cup brewing** — perfect for one drinker

Less ideal for:

- **Multiple cups at once** (max ~270 g per brew)
- **Showcasing very subtle nuance** (V60 wins)
- **Highest body** (French press wins)

## Filter choice

- **Paper filter (default):** cleanest cup, blocks fines and oils. Best for most beans.
- **Metal mesh filter:** more body, more oils through to cup. Good for darker roasts.
- **Cloth filter:** rare, between paper and metal in character.

Paper is usually right. Metal is for when you want fuller body without going to French press.

## Common mistakes

- **Pressing too fast.** A 5-second press extracts unevenly and produces sour cups. Press **slowly** — 30 seconds is the target.
- **Water too hot for darker roasts.** Use 85–88 °C for darker roasts; 90–94 °C for lighter.
- **Pre-wetting the filter inconsistently.** Rinse thoroughly OR don't rinse at all. The half-rinse leaves paper taste.
- **Using stale coffee.** AeroPress can hide stale coffee somewhat, but fresh is always better.
- **Overcomplicating it.** The "World AeroPress Championship" recipes can be elaborate; the basic method is excellent.
